Lowering one bid per square by $1/(m+1)$ forces $m$ strict drops of the last buyer's deletion minimum in one reached-set phase. An internal update always gives a nonunit alternating cycle in the symmetric difference of two deletion matchings. The price pinned by its ties is crossed only once. This transfers B3a's cycle argument to A4i1b, makes the chain count sharp, and bounds the ascent polynomially on cactus graphs and on logarithmic cycle rank per biconnected block. With common offsets, each positive offset can be reached only once by a charged cycle. Row-constant intercepts therefore give zero internal updates.
