The earlier lattice construction preserves local dependence along buyer–good edges. Fixing a vertex cover of size $\tau$ leaves independent scalar updates. A scalar unit-increment map has a least fixed point at the first crossing of the diagonal, found by binary search. Conditional elimination preserves unit increments, so the October block solver only searches the cover. The resulting bit bound is $2^{O(\sqrt{\tau}\log(2+L))}\mathrm{poly}(L)$ for input length $L$. A4c and L4b keep one good per target and one buyer per positive-prior type, giving the $\sqrt{\min(n,k)}$ exponent for games. [ChatGPT 6 Pro review](https://github.com/robbiemu/bayesian-simple-security-games/pull/12#issuecomment-7151840713) · [Proof](../notes/A4m-discrete-contraction.md#pin-a-vertex-cover)

Run the two A4m replays with Python 3. The lattice replay compares 256 scalar lifts with joint iteration on a six-node mixed-cover market, checks all 480 reduced unit increments, and recovers the regularized price $4831789056/4831838209$ on a $2^{150}$ mesh. The max-norm replay keeps the outside tie and the two-good tie at a nonzero residual to distinguish minimum prices from another clearing price.
