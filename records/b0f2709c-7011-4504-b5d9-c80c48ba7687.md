A4h8's fixed-quote switch and the related B3d switching lemma preserve the joint physical boundary flows. With a supplied certificate synchronizing the quotes, the signed load becomes monotone. The first passing parameter recovers the least physical prices. Multiple groups on a factorized core use an implicit convex objective, the A4c supply increase, and a final physical circulation. A4h9 gives ways to propose and test relations. L4c concerns which of those certificates can be reused across queries. [Research direction](../DIRECTIONS.md#direction-compose-solvers) · [Scalar result](../notes/A4h-synchronized-ports.md#one-parameter-can-replace-an-arbitrarily-large-separator) · [Multiple groups](../notes/A4h-synchronized-ports.md#many-groups-on-a-factorized-core)

For the signed-load check, set both boundary capacities to one and give each of two unit buyers the offers $6-p_1$ and $6-2p_2$. The common quote zero clears by splitting. At a common quote $1$, both buyers demand good 1 even though total load equals total supply. The record's theorem locates the first passing parameter and recovers all individual margins at that parameter.

Run `python3 experiments/market_checks/a4h10_synchronized_ports.py` from the repository root. The aggregate load passes at the higher quote while individual capacity fails. The three-group example clears and meets every strict lower Hall cut.
