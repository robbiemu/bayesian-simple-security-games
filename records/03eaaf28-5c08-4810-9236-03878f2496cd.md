Collins proposed testing ties exposed by ascent, then feeding certified relations back into the price search. For a positive-slope affine relation, one clearing solve after grouping two goods on that line decides its sign at the original minimum. The proof lifts the grouped allocation to a market with a single capacity transfer and uses strict Hall inequalities to orient the comparison. An event alone can produce a transient tie, as the three-buyer example shows. The unrestricted certificate selection question remains open. Run `python3 experiments/market_checks/a4h9_hybrid_examples.py`. The first example verifies that buyer A's tie at the first jump disappears at the componentwise minimum, while buyer B's comparison returns a physically clearing price under the enlarged capacities.
