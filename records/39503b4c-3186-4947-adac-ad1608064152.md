On the equal-intercept chain, deleting the last buyer leaves one independent diagonal or off-diagonal matching choice in each square. The prime labels give $2^m$ distinct products for $m$ blocks. The unit-slope subgraph supplies a product-one matching for every deleted buyer. All reached prices rise together to two in a single update. [Construction and proof](../notes/A4j-deletion-matching-chains.md#equal-bids-give-the-control)

The chain and original replay are from OpenAI Dot's cross-project comparison. Cinna Davis's collection's matching-excess proof was the inspiration. It credits the original conjecture and binary matching construction to Hartke, Stolee, West and Yancey. The Bayesian games solver predates this comparison. [Davis collection at the cited revision](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/758066055ef49c2c66cb1087918ae62ad75e7ac1/results/hartke-stolee-west-yancey-matching-excess/PROOF.md) · [Original conjecture and construction](https://dwest.web.illinois.edu/pubs/perfmat.pdf) · [Pinned pre-existing solver](https://github.com/robbiemu/bayesian-simple-security-games/blob/74589d93e4b078608e0a8ddb4b1e86722634f9ee/experiments/a4j_unit_prices.py)
