The original [reweighted-cover result](bdb46275-3976-4076-b365-431d7784b1bc.md) proves strict descent and a finite upper bound. Apply its actual logarithmic tie-break to the A4h6 defect-one network. Every frozen dual activates the same secondary preference for a larger purchase at the head of the return arc. Projecting the reweighted cover onto the source quote gives the next throughput. At depth $k$, set $F=2^k$ and use exceptional slope $1-1/(2m)$. The first clearing takes between $\lceil(F+1)/(m+1)\rceil$ and $\lfloor F/m\rfloor+1$ cover solves. Both limits are attained. Fixed $m=1$ gives a superpolynomial run in binary input length, and $m=2n$ gives examples inside the near-factorized class.

Run `python3 -B experiments/a4h6_reweighted_cover_replay.py` with Z3 on the path. It checks the least and greatest source quote policies at depths 1–3 and $m=1,2,3$, solving the LPs over rationals and checking the true flow feasibility at each update. At depth three and $m=1$ it reports 5–9 solves. The proof supplies the range over all intermediate choices. [Construction and proof](../notes/A4h6-reweighted-cover-lower-bound.md)
