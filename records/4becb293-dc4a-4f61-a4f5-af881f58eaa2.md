Collins proposed testing the generalized-flow contraction analogy. A certified tie identifies an affine relation between prices. The grouped minimum can then recover physical prices and an allocation at the original capacities. In the four-good example the minimum is (3, 3, 0, 0). The two-good example clears at (0, 1) but the possible-edge relaxation alone cannot certify the necessary tie. [Direction](../DIRECTIONS.md#direction-forced) · [Contraction and recovery](../notes/A4h-forced-price-relations.md#exact-contraction-after-certification) · [Stalled discovery](../notes/A4h-forced-price-relations.md#where-tie-discovery-stalls)

The replay checks both price vectors, demand sets, capacities and strict Hall inequalities. Finding enough forced relations in polynomial time on general inputs remains open. [Supplied-capacity issue](https://github.com/robbiemu/bayesian-simple-security-games/issues/1)
