L4b gives the two price extremes used in threshold search. A4h6's restricted Hall search can stop at a failed class test on one side of the answer, with the direction certified by threshold order. A1 bounds the reduced denominator of the endpoint threshold. A4g3's rank search can also test coverage of a supplied candidate library. A4h12 supplies an example of the required hereditary class, while A4h10 addresses which relations persist across changed parameters. [Research direction](../DIRECTIONS.md#direction-compose-solvers) · [Endpoint theorem](../notes/L4-structured-endpoints.md#the-two-endpoint-promises) · [Library tests](../notes/L4-structured-endpoints.md#testing-a-supplied-affine-library-of-endpoint-candidates)

The exact two-good example forces the third buyer to split at both price extremes. The reserve query at $(6/13,6/13)$ has least prices $(6/13,7/13)$ at the supplied budget. Buyer 3 sends all $1/4$ to A. [Counterexample](../notes/L4-structured-endpoints.md#certified-ties-need-a-separate-check-as-reserves-move)

Run `python3 experiments/market_checks/l4c_endpoint_ties.py` from the repository root. Both endpoint splits clear. At reserves $(6/13,6/13)$ the least prices are $(6/13,7/13)$ and buyer 3 uses A for all of her $1/4$ mass. This version attaches the exact replay and its input data after the [PR review](https://github.com/robbiemu/bayesian-simple-security-games/pull/7#issuecomment-7086828698).
