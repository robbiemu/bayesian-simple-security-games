The original degree-three proof with the distinguished buyer last implies that deleting any single buyer leaves a graph with a perfect matching. That graph property and strict Hall bound the pre-final prices for every insertion order. The same property, applied to the persistent deletion matchings, forces full reachability in the witness band during the final insertion. The plan omitting the distinguished buyer still gives the B3f matching product even when a different buyer was inserted last. This yields superpolynomially many internal updates for every order on the stated subfamily. The near-factorized solver computes the same markets' minimum prices in polynomial bit time.
