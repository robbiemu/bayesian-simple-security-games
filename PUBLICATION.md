# Publication record

This is a public working edition of the investigation of Vincent Conitzer’s question about Bayesian simple security games. The initial commits assemble completed research from 21–22 November 2026. They group material by research question and carry their actual publication-preparation dates. The contribution catalog identifies who proposed a direction, which work followed, and which evidence supports that attribution. A Git author on an import commit identifies the publisher. [Original problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08796)

The mathematical notes are working arguments. Their terms “proved” and “checked” describe the work reported in those notes. The B3g audit was a separate model-assisted proof check.

Shiva Kintali’s November 24, 2026 preprint gives a deterministic polynomial-bit-time algorithm for finding an exact Bayes–Nash equilibrium in Conitzer’s explicitly tabulated rational games. The A4i reduction then gives exact affine-market clearing and componentwise minimum-price recovery in polynomial bit time at the supplied capacities. The remaining market questions concern direct algorithms and bounds for specific methods. [Kintali’s proof at the author’s pinned revision](https://github.com/shivakintali/shivakintali.github.io/blob/b28db0428e4cdcef7e9048a386b05d942d1eb57e/papers/SecurityGames-short.pdf) · [A4i reduction](notes/A4i-market-to-game.md) · [Supplied-capacity result](notes/A4h-supplied-capacity.md#one-exact-candidate-at-the-supplied-capacities)

Lean verifies that the executable constructive variant returns an exact equilibrium for every valid rational input, including the public bitstring encoding. It proves a polynomial bound in the project’s binary word-cost model. Refining the solver to a single public `FinTM2` program with a polynomial bound on machine steps remains open. [Lean proof status](lean/Kintali/README.md) · [Companion manuscript](papers/kintali-lean/kintali-lean.pdf) · [Verification record](papers/kintali-lean/verification.txt)

The Lean package checks library lemmas and examples for the fixed-flow investigation in `LibraryChecks`. The H4 development proves that the executable binary compiler emits one deterministic mean-payoff game with the same decision answer as the source threshold instance. It also bounds the compiler's executed bit cost polynomially in the input length under the stated cost model.
