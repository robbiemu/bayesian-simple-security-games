\begin{abstract}
We verify in Lean a constructive variant of Shiva Kintali's polynomial-time
equilibrium algorithm for Bayesian simple security games. Kintali solves a linear program
whose constraints come from a quota sweep. Our executable tests the
sweep's allocation and uses a nonsingular integer minor to repair it.
A two-target restriction gives an exactly feasible pattern whose allocation
fails this test. We extend Kintali's fixed witness by at most one bid per
type and active target so that every failure exposes a blocking bid in
that set. To compare contacts, we prove that for stable allocations on
the same retained bids with the same rankings, the $\ell^1$ change in
outside-option and vacancy balances is at most the $\ell^1$ change in
quotas. Lean verifies exact output for every valid rational input and
polynomial measured binary word cost. A polynomial execution bound for
one machine in the public finite-machine model remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{An executable exact solver}
\label{sec:implementation}

Give the Lean solver one resource, one attacker type and two targets.
The defender gains $2$ or $3$ from covering an attack on the first or
second target. At coverage probabilities $x_1,x_2$, the two attack costs
are $2x_1$ and $\tfrac12+2x_2$. From this binary input Lean returns
coverage $(5/8,3/8)$ and attack probabilities $(3/5,2/5)$. Both attacks
then cost $5/4$, and either defense earns marginal gain $2(3/5)=3(2/5)=6/5$.
The independent Python solver returns the same fractions
(Section~\ref{sec:replay}). \cite{artifact,pythonfollow}

Shiva Kintali's algorithm for Conitzer's game uses Baïou and Balinski's
ordinal transportation model. Each type is a buyer, each positive-gain
target a site. A bid pairs the two at a \emph{quote}, a proposed coverage
probability. A trial keeps a subset of bids. Its coverage determines a
\emph{pattern} of linear constraints on coverage, attack masses and a
threshold. Kintali uses rational linear programming (LP) to find any
exact solution of that pattern.
\cite[Theorem~1 and \S3]{kintali} \cite{conitzer,bb}

We had yet to find a checked general rational LP solver callable from Lean.
Our market search gives every eligible bid below coverage one an initial
selection weight of one and repeats three steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{.15\baselineskip}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\item \emph{Select bids.} For each buyer--site pair, choose bids at equally
spaced fractions of its cumulative weight. Always keep eligible bids at
coverage one and the private buyers that measure coverage.
\item \emph{Sweep to the budget.} Hold the bid list fixed. Starting at zero,
raise a common pressure that increases the sites' quotas. Stop the first
time the resulting coverages sum to the defender's budget. This is the
\emph{budget contact}. Read its coverage, joint attack masses and pressure.
\item \emph{Test the contact.} Substitute those quantities into the pattern,
allowing a small error only in cost and pressure comparisons. If the point
passes, repair it and return the exact strategies. If it fails, double
the weights of the omitted bids that both endpoints would accept and go
back to step~1.
\end{enumerate}
Each new selection uses the same quote grid and starts its sweep at
pressure zero. A weight determines which bids are selected. The sweep
determines how much mass each selected bid carries. A trial can fail
step~3 even when its pattern contains an exact solution.
We use software revision \code{75bc25c} and the lemma numbers of Kintali's
nine-page preprint of 24 November 2026. \cite{artifact,review,followup,kintali}

\subsection{The constraints a trial tests}
\label{sec:pattern}

There are $m\ge1$ targets and an integer budget $0\le r\le m$. The
defender chooses $r$ distinct targets to cover as the attacker
simultaneously chooses one to attack. Only the attacker observes its type,
drawn from $k$ types with priors $\pi_i\ge0$ summing to one.
Type $i$ earns $a_{ij}$ for attacking uncovered $j$ and loses $b_{ij}\ge0$
of that payoff if $j$ is covered. It therefore minimizes the expected cost
$F_{ij}(x_j)=-a_{ij}+b_{ij}x_j$. The defender gains $d_j\ge0$ from covering
an attacked $j$. At coverage $x$ returned by the sweep, choose the least-index
minimizer $j_i$ for each type. Mark every $(i,j)$ whose cost at $x$ is
within the trial tolerance $\tau$ of that minimum. A target with $x_j=0$
has lower status, one with $x_j=1$ upper status, and all others balanced
status. These marks and statuses determine the pattern. \cite[\S\S1,3]{kintali}

Write $y$ for coverage in a candidate solution and $q_{ij}$ for the
probability that the attacker has type $i$ and attacks $j$. Then
$P_j=d_j\sum_i q_{ij}$ is the defender's marginal gain from covering $j$.
The marks and statuses chosen at $x$ stay fixed while $y,q$ and the
threshold $\lambda$ vary. At residual $e\in[0,1]$ the pattern requires
\[
  0\le y_j\le1,\quad \sum_j y_j=r,\qquad
  q_{ij}\ge0,\quad\sum_j q_{ij}=\pi_i,\quad
  q_{ij}=0\ \text{on unmarked pairs}.
\]
It also requires a threshold $\lambda\ge0$ and the rows
\[
 F_{ij_i}(y_{j_i})\le F_{i\ell}(y_\ell)+e\quad(\text{all }i,\ell),\qquad
 F_{ij}(y_j)\le F_{ij_i}(y_{j_i})+e\quad((i,j)\text{ marked}).
\]
These rows compare the reference to every target and each marked target
back to the reference. Lower status fixes $y_j=0$ and requires $P_j\le\lambda+e$.
Upper fixes $y_j=1$ and requires $P_j\ge\lambda-e$. Balanced permits
any $y_j\in[0,1]$ and requires $\abs{P_j-\lambda}\le e$.
Only the cost and pressure inequalities receive the allowance $e$. In
particular, an unmarked pair must carry zero mass even in the relaxed
test. At $e=0$ every positive attack is a minimizer. Moving coverage
from a target with $y_j>0$ to one with $y_\ell<1$ earns
$P_\ell-P_j\le\lambda-\lambda=0$ per unit. The two sets of rows thus
certify the two players' best responses.
\cite[\S1, (1)--(3)]{kintali}

\paragraph{The feasibility gap used by Lean.}
For the positive budgets that reach the search, Kintali's Lemma~2 gives
a single height $H$ such that a feasible residual $e<1/H$ implies exact
feasibility for the same pattern.
The implementation has $n=km+m+2$ variables including $\lambda,e$.
Take the distinct rational values among $1,r$, the priors, gains, cost
intercepts and slopes. With $D$ the product of their reduced denominators
and $M$ the maximum of one and their absolute numerators, clearing
denominators gives integer rows bounded by $C=2DM$. Every full-size row
determinant is bounded by $n!C^n\le H=(nC)^n$, with $\log H$ polynomial
in the input length. \cite[Lemma~2]{kintali}

The project already had a feasibility-gap lemma from H4, its reduction
of simple stochastic reachability games to deterministic mean-payoff
games. \decl{Kintali/ExactRecovery/Determinant}{86}{ExactRecovery.recover}
applies this lemma to the relaxed system with $e$ as its objective. The mass and
coverage constraints bound $q,y$. Since $r>0$, some $y_j>0$ and its
pressure row gives $\lambda\le P_j+e\le\max_j d_j+1$. For integer normals,
right-hand sides $b/D_0$ with integral $b$ and $D_0>0$, and absolute
full-size row determinants bounded by $B>0$,
\decl{StochasticGames/LinearFeasibilityGap}{42}{exists_point_with_gap_of_det_bound}
gives a feasible point with residual $e'\le e$ and every positive
coordinate at least $1/(D_0B)$. Here $D_0=1$ and $B=H$. Starting from
$e<1/H$ therefore forces $e'=0$. The executable constructs exact
coordinates by a separate argument in
Section~\ref{sec:repair}. \cite{h4}

\section{Computing the contact at the supplied budget}
\label{sec:sweep}

Let $S=\{j:d_j>0\}$ and $m_+=|S|$. At zero or full budget the coverage
is fixed. If $r\ge m_+$, cover $S$ and fill spare places in target order.
In each case every type chooses a cheapest target. The sweep handles
$0<r<m_+$. All $m$ targets are available for attack, and Lean keeps
all $k$ type rows, including those of mass zero. Put $\eta=1/(4H)$. Kintali's grid uses $\tau=\eta$.
The program takes the smaller of $\eta$ and the repair precision in
Section~\ref{sec:repair}. For either choice let
\[
 \sigma=\frac{\tau}{\max(1,\max_j d_j)},\qquad
 G=\max\!\left(1,\left\lceil\frac{\max_{1\le i\le k,\,j\in S} b_{ij}}{\tau}
                                  \right\rceil\right),\qquad
 p_z=z/G\quad(0\le z\le G).
\]
Then $d_j\sigma\le\tau$ and $b_{ij}/G\le\tau$ at active sites.
\cite[\S\S1--2]{kintali}

A real buyer $i$ has mass $\pi_i$ and outside cost
$w_i=\min_j F_{ij}(\mathbf 1_{j\in S})$. Its outside target $o(i)$ is the
least-index minimizer at coverage one on $S$ and zero elsewhere. An eligible bid $(i,j,z)$ has
$j\in S$ and $F_{ij}(p_z)\le w_i$. The buyer ranks eligible bids by
increasing cost, site index and quote. Sites prefer higher quotes, then lower buyer indices.
A restriction keeps eligible caps ($p_G=1$) and any selected eligible
bids below the cap. Each grid gap at an active site has a private buyer of
mass $\sigma/G$ whose sole bid ranks between the adjacent quotes and is always
retained. At pressure $t$ active site $j$ has quota $t/d_j$. A purchase counts
at both its buyer and its site, with no separate size limit. Unused buyer mass
goes to the outside option and unused site quota to a vacancy, each ranked last
at its sole endpoint. An endpoint \emph{accepts} a bid if it could move
positive mass to it from a strictly worse record. A bid that both endpoints
accept \emph{blocks}. Stability means that no retained bid blocks.
\cite[\S2, pp.~3--4]{kintali}

At an active site, adjoin $0,G$ to the retained quote indices. Consecutive cuts
$a<b$ give one private buyer of mass $(b-a)\sigma/G$. Expand its purchase
by filling elementary buyers in descending site priority. This prevents a block
within the group. Each other retained bid at the site ranks above the whole
group or below it. A block against another group, a real bid or a vacancy
would therefore block the grouped allocation. Expansion puts the restricted
and full allocations on the same elementary grid.
\cite[\S2]{kintali}

Each active site has one group plus one per interior cut. The $R$ retained real
bids supply at most $R$ interior cuts, giving at most $R+m_+$ positive-mass groups.
Lean \atfile{lean/Kintali/TrialWork/SelectedLists.lean\#L69}{enumerates all $m$ targets}.
An inactive target admits no real bid, so its \atfile{lean/Kintali/RetainedQuotes.lean\#L88}{cuts $0,G$}
give one \atfile{lean/Kintali/QuotaSweep/BuilderParameters.lean\#L24}{zero-mass group}.
Thus Lean stores at most $R+m$ groups. The
\atfile{experiments/kintali_market.py\#L52}{Python sweep} skips inactive targets.

Each active site has total private mass $\sigma$. If $U_j$ is the part
purchased at $j$, the fraction still outside is its coverage. Write $O$
for the total private mass still outside. Then
\begin{equation}
 x_j=1-U_j/\sigma\ (j\in S),\qquad x_j=0\ (j\notin S),\qquad
 O=\sum_{j\in S}(\sigma-U_j)=\sigma\sum_j x_j .
 \label{eq:contact}
\end{equation}
The sweep stops at $O=\sigma r$. At $t=0$ all buyers are outside, so $O=\sigma m_+$.
At $\bar t=1+\max_j d_j$ each active quota exceeds $1+\sigma$, the most a
site could buy. Any unfilled private buyer would block against that vacancy, so $O=0$.

At each stage a site proposes its highest-ranked bid whose buyer would
accept more of it, or points to a terminal for vacancy if there is none. Each positive-mass
buyer points along its worst occupied record, to the site on that bid or
to the terminal if the record is its outside option.
The site's arc adds mass and the buyer's arc releases it.
On a directed cycle, transfer the smallest mass on a releasing arc around
the cycle and recompute the pointers. The pressure stays fixed. A private
buyer accepting its sole bid releases outside mass to the terminal, so
this trade also leaves $O$ fixed.

If there is no cycle, inject rate $1/d_j$ at each active site. In
topological order, pass the total incoming rate and any injection along
each outgoing arc. Holdings now change linearly with pressure. A holding
of size $u$ draining at rate $v>0$ empties after an increment $u/v$.
Take the least of $\bar t-t$ and all such exhaustion increments. If the
segment reaches $\sigma r$, the slope $\dot O$ is negative and the sweep
stops at $(\sigma r-O)/\dot O$ units of additional pressure, including
equality at the event. Otherwise it advances to the event, recomputes all
pointers and continues. Kintali's Lemma~3 bounds the number of stages.
Lean verifies stability of the expanded contact at the supplied budget.
Every stable allocation on this restriction at a smaller nonnegative
pressure has $O>\sigma r$. \cite[Lemma~3]{kintali}

The search also needs to test bids omitted from the restriction.
A real holding at quote $p$ requires all $\sigma(1-p)$ private mass
ranked above it to be filled, or an unfilled private bid would block.
Thus $U_j\ge\sigma(1-p)$ and $p\ge x_j$. A vacancy forces $x_j=0$.
The site accepts an eligible bid at $p>x_j$
and rejects it at $p<x_j$. At $p=x_j$ it accepts exactly when it has
vacancy or a lower-priority positive real holding at the same quote.
\cite[\S2, (4)]{kintali}

The return from the sweep uses the two kinds of outside balance differently.
Equation~\eqref{eq:contact} reads coverage from the \emph{private} buyers.
For a \emph{real} buyer $i$, let $u_i$ be its outside balance and $X_{ijz}$
the mass it holds on bid $(i,j,z)$, zero for an omitted bid. Its joint
attack row is
\begin{equation}
 q^X_{ij}=\sum_z X_{ijz}+\mathbf 1_{\{j=o(i)\}}u_i .
 \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
This assigns every unit of the buyer's mass to a target, so the row sums
to $\pi_i$. The outside target $o(i)$ was fixed before the sweep. The
pattern reference $j_i$ is chosen at the resulting coverage. The trial
uses the common sweep pressure $t$ as its candidate threshold $\lambda$
and computes the individual target pressures $P_j$ from $q^X$.
\decl{Kintali/ExactRecovery/Contact}{106}{Holdings.joint}.

\section{Comparing a restricted contact with the full grid}
\label{sec:comparison}

\subsection{What Kintali's full grid certifies}

On the full eligible grid at $\tau=\eta$, Kintali's Lemma~4 shows that
the joint table in \eqref{eq:joint} uses only marked pairs at
the first budget contact $x$. At pressure $t$ it satisfies
$P_j\ge t-d_j\sigma$ wherever $x_j>0$ and $P_j\le t$ wherever $x_j<1$.
Thus $(x,q,t,\eta)$ satisfies the relaxed rows, and Lemma~2 gives
\begin{equation}
       \exists\,y,q',\lambda\quad
       (y,q',\lambda,0)\text{ satisfies the pattern selected by }x.
       \label{eq:full}
\end{equation}
This is the original full-grid feasibility result. On the paper's grid,
\decl{Kintali/ExactRecovery/FullMarket}{23}{Expanded.full_pattern_exact}
accepts any stable full allocation at the stated budget and nonnegative
pressure under the game hypotheses above. Its exact solution may have
different coordinates from the contact. The search must decide what it can
infer about an allocation computed on a restriction. \cite[Lemma~4]{kintali}

\subsection{Sensitivity of outside-option and vacancy balances}
\label{sec:variation}

Fix finite sets of vertices $V$ and records $E$. Each record $a$ has an
endpoint set $\partial a$ of size one or two. At each vertex the incident
records have distinct ranks, with smaller rank preferred. Each one-ended
record ranks strictly below every other incident record at its endpoint.
Let $E_1$ be the one-ended records. They represent outside options and
vacancies. Fix a common retained set $R$. An allocation $X:E\to\Rats_{\ge0}$
is \emph{stable on $R$ at quota $Q$} if
\[
   \sum_{a:\,v\in\partial a}X_a=Q_v\quad(v\in V),
\]
every positive two-ended holding belongs to $R$, and no two-ended record
in $R$ is accepted at both endpoints. Acceptance at $v$ means that $v$
holds positive mass on a strictly worse record. Thus a buyer can hold a
bid and still reject more of it. This definition permits
parallel bids. Bipartiteness enters the application below.

\begin{theorem}[Variation of one-ended balances]\label{thm:variation}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be stable on the same retained set with the same incidence
and ranks, at rational quotas $Q$ and $Q'$, respectively. Then
\begin{equation}
 \normone{(X-Y)|_{E_1}}
   =\sum_{a\in E_1}\abs{X_a-Y_a}
   \ \le\ \sum_{v\in V}\abs{Q_v-Q'_v}
   =\normone{Q-Q'}.                                      \label{eq:variation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\delta_a=X_a-Y_a$. At an endpoint of a differing record, count
$|\delta_a|$ if that endpoint rejects the record in the allocation giving
it \emph{less} mass. Count separately at each rejecting endpoint. Every
differing two-ended record is retained, because the larger holding is
positive. By stability in the allocation giving it less mass, at least
one endpoint rejects it. A one-ended record is always rejected at its sole
endpoint because it ranks last. Thus, writing $A$ for the sum of
$|\delta_a|$ on two-ended records, $B$ for its sum on one-ended records,
and $W$ for the total counted weight, we have $W\ge A+B$.

At a vertex $v$ counted differences have only one sign. Indeed, suppose
$\delta_a>0$ is counted in $Y$ and $\delta_b<0$ is counted in $X$.
Then $Y_b>0$. Rejection of $a$ in $Y$ forces $b$ to rank above $a$.
But $X_a>0$, so rejection of $b$ in $X$ forces $a$ to rank above $b$.
Strict incidence ranks make this impossible. Let $p_v$ and $n_v$ be the
sums of the positive and absolute negative incident differences. The quota
equations give $p_v-n_v=Q_v-Q'_v$. The weight $W_v$ counted at $v$ is
at most $\max(p_v,n_v)$, hence
\[
       2W_v\le p_v+n_v+\abs{Q_v-Q'_v}.
\]
In $\sum_v(p_v+n_v)$ each ordinary bid contributes $|\delta_a|$ at both
endpoints and a one-ended record contributes it just once. This sum
is $2A+B$, so
$2(A+B)\le 2W\le 2A+B+\normone{Q-Q'}$. Cancelling gives
\eqref{eq:variation}.
\end{proof}

This is \decl{Kintali/StableAllocation}{192}{Market.slack_variation_le}.
Kintali's proof of Lemma~5 supplies the counted rejections and their
one-sign property. At fixed quotas $p_v=n_v$. Retaining the imbalance
term in that proof gives the variable-quota estimate. When $Q=Q'$, the
right-hand side vanishes, so every outside balance and vacancy agrees.
Each private buyer's purchase is its fixed mass minus its outside balance,
which recovers Lemma~5. More generally the outside and vacancy vector
is a well-defined 1-Lipschitz function on quotas
admitting stable allocations. Individual ordinary holdings may still vary
at a fixed quota. A one-buyer market attains equality. Give the buyer mass
one and a single bid at a site of quota $t\in[0,1]$. Stability forces a
purchase of $t$, so increasing $t$ changes the buyer's outside balance by
exactly the quota increment.
\cite[Lemma~5]{kintali}

\paragraph{Relation to stable allocation theory.}
For a fixed capacitated ordinal transportation instance, Baïou and
Balinski's Lemma~8 proves equality of each row sum and each column sum
across stable allocations. Their Lemma~9 proves equality of the entire row
or column of an undersubscribed agent. \cite[p.~674, Lemmas 8--9]{bb}
In the discrete hospital model Roth's theorem
likewise fixes the identities of the residents at a hospital that is
underfilled in one stable outcome. \cite[p.~426]{roth} In schedule matching with consistent,
persistent, size-monotone choice functions, Alkan and Gale's Theorem~6
(\emph{Unisize}) fixes the size of each agent's schedule. Their Corollary~3
fixes the full schedule for an underfilled agent with quota-filling choice.
\cite[Theorem 6 and Corollary 3]{alkan}

There are also precedents for comparing different markets. Baïou and
Balinski's inductive algorithm extends a stable allocation after the
addition of one row. \cite[\S2, Theorem 2]{bb} Alkan and Gale's Theorem~3
orders the firm-optimal outcomes after entry of a firm or a worker.
For example, after a new firm enters, incumbent firms weakly prefer the
old outcome and workers weakly prefer the new one. \cite[Theorem 3]{alkan}
Equation~\eqref{eq:variation}
compares arbitrary stable allocations at two quota vectors on the same
records. It gives a numerical bound on the sum over both sides for
simultaneous, possibly oppositely directed quota changes. Fixed-quota
invariance thus has established precedents. We present the unit-constant
inequality as a transferable formulation of the rejection argument.
Priority for the general variable-quota estimate remains undetermined.
\cite[Lemmas 5--6]{kintali}

\subsection{A direct comparison on the union market}

Give sites sign $s_v=+1$ and buyers sign $s_v=-1$. Consider two stable
allocations on the same retained set. Suppose each two-ended record joins opposite signs and
$s_v(Q_v-Q'_v)=\abs{Q_v-Q'_v}$ at every vertex. Subtract and sum the quota
equations with these signs. Ordinary holdings cancel, leaving
\[
  \normone{Q-Q'}=\sum_{a\in E_1}s_{v(a)}(X_a-Y_a)
       \ \le\ \sum_{a\in E_1}|X_a-Y_a|\ \le\ \normone{Q-Q'}.
\]
Here $v(a)$ is the sole endpoint, and the last inequality is
Theorem~\ref{thm:variation}. Each difference
$|X_a-Y_a|-s_{v(a)}(X_a-Y_a)$ is nonnegative and their sum is zero,
so each vanishes. In particular, when site quotas rise and
buyer masses stay fixed, each buyer's outside balance weakly falls and
each site vacancy weakly rises. The mass released from outside options
and the new vacancies together account for the entire increase in capacity.
Coverage at $j$ is the sum of its private outside balances divided
by $\sigma$, so it can only decrease or stay constant as pressure rises.
For any subset of buyers, its outside total changes by at most
$|t-u|\sum_{j\in S}1/d_j$ between pressures $t,u$. The development also
extends continuity of private purchases to the real-pressure sweep.
\decl{Kintali/StableAllocation}{259}{Market.signed_slack_change},
\decl{Kintali/QuotaSweep/RealContinuity}{105}{privatePurchases_regular}.

Fix one full-grid contact $Y$ at pressure $t_F$. Let $W_0$ be its positive
\emph{optional real} bids. The paper bounds $|W_0|\le k+m_+$ by counting
site proposals and buyers' worst holdings. The formal proof gives a static
count. Assign each positive real bid to an endpoint rejecting it, which
exists by stability. At a vertex a positive rejected bid is its unique
worst positive record. This gives at most one assigned bid per real buyer
or active site. The full grid defines a fixed witness for the proof.
The search can proceed without computing $W_0$.
\cite[(5), Lemma~6]{kintali}

\begin{corollary}[Equal-budget contacts]\label{cor:union}
Let $X$ be stable at pressure $t_R$ on a restriction $R$, and $Y$ stable
at $t_F$ on the full book. Suppose their coverage sums agree.
Let $W$ consist of full-book records such that $R\cup W$ contains every
positive ordinary holding of $Y$. Then their coverages agree
coordinatewise, or some $a\in W\setminus R$ blocks $X$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
First expand both contacts onto the same elementary records. Passing from
$R$ to $R\cup W$ adds only bids in $W\setminus R$. If none blocks $X$,
it is still stable there. Passing from the full book to the same union
retains all positive holdings of $Y$ and can introduce no new blocker.
We can now apply the sign argument on a single retained set. If
$t_R\le t_F$, every site quota at $Y$ is at least its quota at $X$, so
$x^X_j\ge x^Y_j$ for all $j$. Their sums agree, forcing equality in each
coordinate. The opposite pressure order gives the reversed inequalities
and the same conclusion.
\end{proof}

The permanent records and $W_0$ satisfy the support premise for every
restriction. Kintali sweeps the union and invokes fixed-quota invariance
at each pressure. The quota bound lets us compare the restricted and full
allocations directly on that union, even when their pressures differ.
\cite[Lemma~6]{kintali}
\decl{Kintali/ContactComparison}{169}{Market.contact_eq_or_omitted_blocker}.

\section{A stopping rule for the computed allocation}
\label{sec:search}

For Kintali's LP, absence of a $W_0$ blocker gives equality of coverages
by Corollary~\ref{cor:union}, hence the same references, marks and statuses.
The full pattern has an exact solution by \eqref{eq:full}, so the LP may
return that solution. The executable substitutes the trial's own $x^X$,
the $q^X$ from \eqref{eq:joint}, and its contact pressure $t_R$.
The acceptance test is
\begin{equation}
 (x^X,q^X,t_R,\tau)\text{ satisfies every row of the relaxed trial pattern.}
 \label{eq:test}
\end{equation}
A positive answer passes these coordinates to the repair routine. A
negative answer triggers a search for blocking intervals. Equality of the
two coverage vectors fixes the pattern, but allocations at that coverage
can put mass on different pairs. \decl{Kintali/MarketTrial}{65}{relaxedCheck_spec}.

\subsection{A legal restriction with crossed holdings}
\label{sec:crossed}

Take two types, two sites, $r=1$, $\pi=(1/2,1/2)$ and $d=(1,1)$, with costs
\[
  F_{11}(x_1)=2x_1,\qquad F_{12}(x_2)=3/2,\qquad
  F_{21}(x_1)=3/2,\qquad F_{22}(x_2)=2x_2 .
\]
For each buyer $w_i=3/2$, so only its off-diagonal cap is eligible.
Select no optional quote for any of the four pairs. This is a legal
restricted market. Since $d_1=d_2=1$, each site has quota $t$. Up to
$t=1/2$ each buys only its off-diagonal cap, exhausting that buyer's
half-unit. Each then buys private mass at rate one, so $U_j=t-1/2$.
The budget equation is $2(1-(t-1/2)/\sigma)=1$. It gives
$t_R=(1+\sigma)/2$ and $x=(1/2,1/2)$. Compare
the returned joint masses with the diagonal reassignment in~\eqref{eq:crossed}.
\begin{equation}
    q^X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
    q^\ast=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:crossed}
\end{equation}
At this coverage the diagonal costs are $1$ and the off-diagonal costs are
$3/2$. Since $\tau\le\eta<1/2$, only the diagonal is marked. Both sites
are balanced. In \eqref{eq:test} the unmarked coordinates must vanish
even with residual $\tau$, so $q^X$ fails immediately. Since
$\tau\le1/4$ and the maximum slope is two, $G\ge8$. The first diagonal
quote above $1/2$ is at most $1/2+1/G\le5/8$. Its cost is below
$3/2$ and the site accepts it, giving an omitted blocker. With $q^\ast$
the support condition holds, row sums are $1/2$, each positive edge is a
minimizer, and $P_1=P_2=\lambda=1/2$. These are all the exact pattern
conditions at $e=0$. Thus the pattern LP can succeed on this very
restriction. The threshold for its exact point is free to differ from $t_R$.

The Lean executable assertion builds the sparse contact from four empty lists,
checks its coverage and crossed joint table, tests the diagonal reassignment
row by row at $e=0$, and requires a nonempty blocking interval. The example
specifies the restriction by hand. Section~\ref{sec:weights} traces a
separate game in which the default search chooses both restrictions itself.
\cite{followup,pythonrecord} \decl{Replay/KintaliSearch}{36}{checksCrossedTrial}.

\subsection{A fixed witness for the stricter test}

Fix one full contact on the executable's grid and take $W_0$ from that
contact. Its support bids are enough to recover the full coverage vector
when none of them blocks the trial. To control what each buyer actually
holds at that coverage we also need a quote the site will accept.
For each $(i,j)$ with
$j\in S$ and $x^Y_j<1$, consider $z_j=\lfloor Gx^Y_j\rfloor+1$. Add the
bid $(i,j,z_j)$ to $W_0$ if eligible and $z_j<G$. An eligible bid at $G$
is already permanent. The enlarged set $W$ is fixed throughout the search
and satisfies
\begin{equation}
                  |W|\le h:=k+m_++k m_+.                 \label{eq:witness}
\end{equation}
Suppose no bid from $W\setminus R$ blocks the trial. First apply the union
comparison using the support bids to get $x^X=x^Y$. The added bids are
therefore the first quotes \emph{above the trial coverage} as well.
The strict step above $x^X_j$ guarantees site acceptance even if the site
would reject a bid tied at $x^X_j$. An eligible next bid is in the witness
or is permanent. It cannot block, so its buyer must reject it. Every
record the buyer holds costs at most the cost of that bid, which in turn
is at most $F_{ij}(x^X_j)+b_{ij}/G$. If the bid is ineligible,
even $w_i$ is below its cost. For an inactive or fully covered site
$w_i\le F_{ij}(x^X_j)$ directly. Since $b_{ij}/G\le\tau$ at active
sites, every held record costs at most $\min_j F_{ij}(x^X_j)+\tau$.
A real purchase at $p\ge x^X_j$ therefore
uses a marked pair. Outside mass redirected to a target attaining $w_i$
also uses a marked pair. If that target is active, its permanent cap
would block unless the target were fully covered.

When $x^X_j>0$ there is no vacancy, so real purchases before redirection
equal $t_R/d_j-U_j$. Hence $P_j\ge t_R-d_j\sigma\ge t_R-\tau$.
An active site with $x^X_j<1$ receives no redirected mass, giving
$P_j\le t_R$ there. Inactive sites have $P_j=x^X_j=0$. Together these
bounds prove \eqref{eq:test} for the trial's own $(x^X,q^X,t_R)$.
By contraposition, every failed test has an omitted blocker in the same
fixed set $W$. The search updates all its blockers without computing $W$.
\decl{Kintali/ExactRecovery/Witness}{184}{extended_card_active},
\decl{Kintali/ExactRecovery/Trial}{86}{Expanded.relaxed_or_omitted}.

\subsection{Compressed weights and a finite trial bound}
\label{sec:weights}

On each pair the eligible optional indices form a prefix of
$0,\ldots,G-1$ because cost is nondecreasing in the quote. Give each such
index weight one. With $J=4h$ and pair total $V>0$, select the first index
whose cumulative weight, including that bid, is at least $aV/J$, for
$a=1,\ldots,J-1$. Only the distinct selected bids and eligible caps enter
the real book. An empty prefix needs no bid. For a fixed pair, lower quotes
are cheaper for the buyer, or preferred on a cost tie. Thus the
buyer-accepted optional indices are exactly those below some $u$ in the
eligible prefix. Site acceptance starts at an index $\ell$ and continues
to the end of the prefix, by the rule of Section~\ref{sec:sweep}. Either
cutoff can equal the length of the prefix. The blocker indices are
exactly those with $\ell\le z<u$, an empty interval if $\ell\ge u$.
The executable locates both boundaries by binary search on the saved contact.
After failure, double all weights in every such interval.
\cite[\S3, pp.~8--9]{kintali}

Two invariants control the search. A blocker interval cannot contain a
selected bid, by stability. If its weight exceeded $V/J$, the cumulative
weight would cross one of the selection thresholds inside the interval.
Hence doubling adds at most $V/J$ to the pair total. At the same time at
least one member of the fixed $W$ doubles on every failure. Put
$b=1+\lfloor\log_2 G\rfloor$. After $2hb$ failures, one of the at most
$h$ witness bids must have doubled at least $2b$ times, reaching weight
at least $2^{2b}$. Its entire pair started at weight at most $G$ and
grew by a factor of at most $1+1/(4h)$ per failure. Since
$(1+1/(4h))^{2h}\le\exp(1/2)<2$ and $G<2^b$, that pair would still
weigh less than $2^{2b}$. This contradiction bounds the total by $2hb$
trials, including the successful one. An empty witness precludes the
first failure. Kintali uses $h=k+m_+$ for the LP. The executable uses
$h$ from \eqref{eq:witness}.
\cite[\S3]{kintali} \decl{Kintali/QuoteSearch/Algorithm}{167}{QuoteSearch.solve_correct}.

Within a prefix the program stores lengths of constant runs and their
integer doubling exponents. After $T$ failures there are at most $1+2T$
runs per nonempty prefix, weights at most $2^T$, and totals at most
$G2^T$. Quantiles are recovered by cumulative lengths and weights with
integer comparisons. Consequently the binary length of $G$ enters the
representation and trial bound even when an elementary book of $G$ private
buyers per site would be prohibitive.

\section{Recovering an exact point from the accepted allocation}
\label{sec:repair}

An accepted trial supplies a rational vector $z=(q^X,x^X,t_R,\tau)$,
listing the flows first in the order used by the program. Form the integer
matrix of the relaxed rows of Section~\ref{sec:pattern}, writing each
equality as two inequalities. These rows already enforce $e\ge0$.
Append one \emph{unscaled} row $e\le0$. In the resulting system $Az\le b$,
the slack $s_i=b_i-A_i z$ measures how much room the candidate has in
row $i$. Every old slack is nonnegative. Only the added row is violated,
by exactly $\tau$ even after the other rows have been scaled. Satisfying
all the rows will force $e=0$. There are $n=km+m+2$ variables and
$N=6m+4km+2k+6$ stored rows, counting inactive rows.
\decl{Kintali/Recovery}{120}{contactTolerance}.

Let $C\ge1$ be an integer bounding the absolute coefficients of $A$,
including the added unit row. The program uses
\begin{equation}
 B=(n+1)!\,C^{\,n+1},\qquad
 A_0=n^2CB,\qquad K=A_0+2,\qquad
 \varepsilon=(B K^{N+1})^{-1}.                         \label{eq:precision}
\end{equation}
The dimensions and $C$ are determined by the input, so the market can
set $\tau=\min(\eta,\varepsilon)$ before its first trial. This is the
tolerance for the grid, private scale, marks and relaxed test. The
repair itself uses $\varepsilon$ to separate rows that it will make
tight from rows with enough room to absorb the correction. The factor
$B$ bounds the determinants and cofactors used below. These constants
have bit lengths polynomial in the dimensions and the bit length of $C$.

\begin{theorem}[Repair from row slacks]\label{thm:repair}
Let $A$ be an $N$-by-$n$ integer matrix with $\abs{A_{ij}}\le C$, and
let $b$ be integral. Given rational $z$ such that every $s_i\ge-\varepsilon$,
the following minor computation returns rational $z'$ with $Az'\le b$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider the $N+1$ disjoint windows
$(\varepsilon K^\ell,\varepsilon K^{\ell+1}]$ for $0\le\ell\le N$.
There are only $N$ row slacks, so one window is empty. Scan in order and
write $a$ for the lower endpoint of the first empty window. Every row is
either near, with $\abs{s_i}\le a$, or far,
with $s_i>Ka$. Negative slacks are near because $s_i\ge-\varepsilon$.

Start with the empty minor, whose determinant is $1$. Extend it by an
unused near row and an unused column whenever the enlarged square matrix
has nonzero determinant. Stop when no such extension exists. Write $I,T$
for the selected row and column lists, $M=A_{I,T}$, and $d=\det M$.
Maximality makes the rows of $A_I$ span every near row. Indeed, the determinant
obtained by bordering $M$ with row $i$ and column $j$ is, up to the row
and column order, $d(A_{ij}-A_{i,T}M^{-1}A_{I,j})$. If the row is
independent, one such test succeeds. Use this minor to correct the point by
\[
       z'_T=z_T+M^{-1}s_I,\qquad
       z'_{T^{\mathrm c}}=z_{T^{\mathrm c}} .
\]
This gives $A_I z'=b_I$. Each change of slack is at most $A_0a$ in
absolute value. To see this, expand
$A_{i,T}\operatorname{adj}(M)s_I/d$. There are at most $n^2$ terms,
the coefficients have magnitude at most $C$, the cofactors at most $B$,
and the nonzero integer $d$ has $|d|\ge1$. A far row began with
$s_i>Ka$, so its new slack exceeds $(K-A_0)a=2a>0$.

For a near row, set $\alpha_i=A_{i,T}\operatorname{adj}(M)$.
Since $dA_i=\alpha_i A_I$, its \emph{new} slack satisfies
\[
           d(b_i-A_i z')= d b_i-\alpha_i b_I\in\mathbb Z .
\]
This identity also covers dependent near rows whose right-hand sides
have yet to be shown consistent. Such a row started with $|s_i|\le a$
and changed by at most $A_0a$. Multiplying the new slack by
$|d|\le B$ gives an integer of absolute value at most $B(1+A_0)a$.
Since $a\le\varepsilon K^N=1/(BK)$ and $1+A_0<K$, the only possible
integer is zero. All near rows are now tight and every far row has
positive slack.
\end{proof}

The executable puts the input point over a common denominator, scans
bordered minors in a fixed order, and evaluates the Cramer numerators of
the displacement by stored integer determinants. It materializes the
intermediate matrices in Bird's division-free determinant algorithm, bounds
their entries, and identifies its result with Mathlib's determinant through
\code{BirdDet.birdDetSpec_eq_det}. The proof above explains why coordinates
outside the pivot can keep their original denominators. Both residual rows
now hold, so $e=0$ and the exact certificate yields the output strategies.
\cite{bird} \decl{Kintali/LinearProgramming/Recovery}{34}{System.recover_feasible},
\decl{Kintali/Recovery}{215}{ExactRecovery.repair_correct}.

For a nonzero correction one can check by hand, the repair replay supplies
one attacker type, unit gains, costs $(x_1,x_2)$ and budget one. Its input
has $q=(1/2,1/2)$, threshold $1/2$ and coverage
$(1/2+\tau/4,1/2-\tau/4)$. The cost gap is $\tau/2$, so both targets
are marked. At residual zero the marked costs must agree, while the
budget still requires $y_1+y_2=1$. Thus repair must subtract $\tau/4$
from the first coverage and add it to the second. The Lean and Python
checks both recover $(1/2,1/2)$ from this hand-supplied point.
\decl{Replay/KintaliSearch}{21}{rounded},
\href{\baseurl/experiments/kintali_replay.py\#L192}{\code{check_nonzero_repair}}.

\section{What the public theorems establish}
\label{sec:public}

\paragraph{Exact strategies.}
The Formal Conjectures Authors' typed input lists the resource count, the defender's uncovered
and covered payoffs per target, and each prior paired with a full attacker
payoff row. Validity requires a nonempty target set, a legal budget,
nonnegative normalized priors and the two weak payoff monotonicities of
Section~\ref{sec:pattern}. For every such game, the internal solver returns
$\mathrm{some}(x,\alpha)$, where $\alpha$ is conditional on type. The public
encoders represent rational numbers by reduced fractions and lists by
delimited bit strings. On the canonical encoding of a valid input, decoding
the solver's output succeeds and gives a well-shaped compact Bayes--Nash
equilibrium. For any stream decoding to a valid input the measured solver
agrees with the public bit solver and its output decodes to the typed
solution. A positive-prior type uses $\alpha_{ij}=q_{ij}/\pi_i$.
At zero prior the executable assigns probability one to a minimizing target.
A separate function implements Kintali's prefix-sum lottery. It returns
at most $m$ positive-weight defenses, each covering exactly $r$ targets,
with weights summing to one and marginals $x$. \cite[\S1]{kintali}
\cite{artifact,upstream}

\paragraph{Polynomial binary word cost.}
The bound concerns an executable measured function on \emph{all} bit
streams, including malformed streams and encodings of invalid tables.
A run carries a value, a slot count $s$ and a maximum charged operand width
$w$. Sequencing adds counts and takes the maximum of widths. For an input
of length $L$, write $\ell(u)$ for the binary length of a natural number.
H4's \code{CompilerRun} supplies the address allowance and word tariff
\[
 a(L,s,w)=\ell(L+1)+\ell(s+1)+3\ell(w+1)+8,\qquad
 c(u)=64(u+1)^3 .
\]
The theorem asserts that a \emph{single} $P\in\mathbb N[X]$ bounds
$s\,c(\max(w,a(L,s,w)))$ by $P(L)$ for the work recorded by every run.
The length here is the length of the actual input stream. The proof combines
decoding, validity checks, initialization, compressed selection and updates,
ranked sparse contact, relaxed tests, repair and compact output encoding.
The separate addressed bound for lottery construction measures the binary
description of the supplied marginals. \cite{artifact,h4}

This is the second concrete use of H4. Besides its feasibility-gap lemma,
the development imports H4's binary size estimates and its count-and-width
run operations for integer arithmetic and indexed data. The recovery work
theorem first bounds work in the length of its \emph{materialized} integer
matrix, right-hand sides, rational point and height. The solver composition
bounds those sizes in the original input length. For the sweep the
\code{ContactCharge.price} adapter takes the computed scalar/index-operation
count and gives each unit a uniform slot tariff at a proved width. The
arithmetic tariff covers normalized rational addition, multiplication,
division and comparisons. The program also charges for constructing the
selected inventory and its rank and quota tables. These conventions specify
what a slot measures. \decl{Kintali/LinearProgramming/RationalInput}{118}{RationalInput.run_binary_work},
\decl{Kintali/QuotaSweep/ContactCharge}{48}{ContactCharge.price}.

\paragraph{Finite-machine executions.}
The public Formal Conjectures proposition asks for one function computed
by one Mathlib \code{FinTM2} with a polynomial step bound on \emph{every
canonically encoded typed game}. Even an invalid typed game needs a
canonical output within the clock. Equilibrium is required on valid games.
Words outside the image of the public input encoder carry no execution
obligation in that proposition. \cite{upstream}

The artifact embeds finite programs on bit stacks with one machine step
per instruction. Its delimiter program terminates after $5L+4$ steps on
an $L$-bit word. The solver theorem at this level is conditional. Supply
one program, one polynomial clock, and for each typed input a run from the
specified initial configuration to the clean final configuration with
the encoded output, within that clock. On valid games the output must
agree with the verified solver. Those data imply the public proposition.
Constructing such a program and proving its execution and clock bounds for
the whole solver remain open. This requires, in particular, realizing the
charged operations by sequences of stack instructions within the clock.
\decl{Kintali/Machine/Delimiter}{137}{Delimiter.steps},
\decl{Kintali/Machine/PublicSolver}{22}{exists_polyTime_bayesNash_of_runs}.

\section{Independent implementation and observed runs}
\label{sec:replay}

The Python program reads normalized cost-form JSON, computes with
standard-library \code{Fraction}, and writes rational coverage, conditional
attack rows, and a lottery of zero-based target sets. It implements the
grouped sweep, the compressed quantiles and interval doublings, and the
same test and slack-window choice. Its repair eliminates rational rows and
back-substitutes for the pivot displacement. In the Lean implementation,
bordered and replaced-column integer determinants perform the pivot tests
and correction. The Python runner can execute without Lean. Its evidence
is experimental. \cite{pythonrecord,pythonfollow}

\paragraph{A fractional binary replay.}
For the opening example in Section~\ref{sec:implementation}, the Python
harness builds the public payoff table and encodes it in 387 bits. The Lean
adapter checks canonical input, calls \code{MeasuredSolver.run}, and emits
the output bits. Python independently decodes those bits and checks
$x=(5/8,3/8)$ and $\alpha=(3/5,2/5)$. On the JSON side the two singleton
defenses have weights $5/8$ and $3/8$. This case exercises the entire
binary route.
\cite{pythonfollow,pythonrecord}

\paragraph{Scope of comparison.}
All six shared fixtures gave identical decoded strategies in Lean and
Python. They cover zero budget, full budget, zero gains with a zero prior,
flat costs, a strict affine case and the mixed affine game above.
The independent checker verifies box and mass constraints. It compares
each type's expected cost with its cheapest attack and the defender's
expected gain with the sum of the $r$ largest pressures. For the Python
lottery it checks weights, cardinalities and every marginal. The harness
prints the exact-match count. In the flat-cost game a match also requires
the two programs to resolve ties alike. \cite{pythonrecord}

Python alone checked equilibria and lotteries on 224 further two-target
games. These comprise 128 one-type endpoint games with binary intercepts,
slopes and gains, 16 interior-budget flat-slope games, 32 one-type games
with a strictly preferred target at every coverage, and 48 two-type games varying priors,

interior zero-prior response. Sections~\ref{sec:crossed} and~\ref{sec:repair}

pure attacks. Cross-language agreement is established on the six shared

\section*{Contributions and use of AI}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Contributions and use of AI}

Robert Thomas Collins directed the investigation. On 22 November he asked
for a constructive Lean proof whose main theorem follows the written
argument, with a named lemma for each major step. He carried this design
into the formalization of Shiva Kintali's algorithm and requested the
independent Python implementation. \cite{leansetup,leanrecord,pythonrecord}

A verified rational LP solver would implement Kintali's pattern test
directly. At that point the project had yet to find one it could call from
Lean. The H4 investigation that Collins had directed already supplied a
determinant-based feasibility gap and a way to account for work on binary
integers. In that setting, OpenAI Codex proposed repairing the allocation
returned by the sweep. It enlarged the fixed witness to prove progress
when the test failed and composed the solver's work bounds.
\cite{h4,leanrecord}

The investigation used GPT-6 and GPT-6.2 models. GPT-6.2 Astra formalized
the grouped sweep, repair, strategy encodings and machine interfaces, and
implemented the sweep and repair in Python. GPT-6.2 Sol wrote the
cross-language replay and independent equilibrium checks. The ChatGPT Pro
review highlighted the two stopping rules and identified the quota-variation
bound for separate treatment. Codex drafted this manuscript, checked the
stable-allocation references, and ran the build, axiom audit and replays
in Appendix~\ref{sec:reproduce}. \cite{leanrecord,pythonrecord,review}
