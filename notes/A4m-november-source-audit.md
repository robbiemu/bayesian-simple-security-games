## What the written audit checked

**Extremal labels.** A consistent hypothesis can contain both signs at one coordinate. It requires the correct sign at every slot tying for the largest nonnegative surplus. A child that meets its assigned threshold gives the necessary feedback at any parent's slot above that threshold. At equality the slot is an extremal tie at the child quote, so the label condition still gives the direction. The median rule applies to signed coordinates as well. Along a leaf path, a surplus that survives the median would have been boosted at more than half the proposals. The resulting mass is greater than one by the depth choice. [Oracle specification](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/overview.tex#L58-L77) · [Feedback and leaf argument](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/prices.tex#L104-L220)

**Branching bound.** At the market minimizer the directional derivative pays each expert the worst gain among her maximizing options. Its sign bounds total wealth for every feedback. On a heavy probe a correct child gives a profitable event to the four-slot experts, including when several slots tie. The pivot allocation compares the child's bonus weights with the parent's, with factor $80(5/2)^4=3125$. Pivots in this comparison can be fixed after the run because they enter the proof rather than the algorithm. The child-failure bonus pays for the worst market loss, so failure would force positive log-wealth growth proportional to the number of probes. At the root that exceeds the bound from initial wealth. Exact rational checks verified the numerical comparisons for those constants and the tree-size exponent. [Profits and failure charge](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/prices.tex#L382-L565)

**Total bit time.** A vertex choice for each proposal is pinned by signed difference equations and outside ties. The signed components give half-integral coordinates and the ancestor pins increase their magnitude additively. Each expert group has at most five integer guesses. Option comparisons partition it by bounds on a guess or a difference of two guesses. Refining these constant-dimensional cells at each proposal keeps weights uniform on each cell. Their counts and first moments can be computed by summing polynomials over integer intervals, with ties assigned to one piece. This builds a rational separation row for the epigraph even though there are exponentially many experts. Ordinary bit-polynomial ellipsoid optimization and coordinatewise minimization on the optimum face then choose a short-bit vertex. The number of probes, precision, and LP cost are all bounded, including on transcripts inconsistent with every hypothesis. [Implicit market implementation](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/prices.tex#L558-L710)

**Fixed-point transfer.** In the contraction reduction, the hypothetical demands are shifted inward from the two signed coordinates of the fixed point. A nonnegative extremal surplus identifies a coordinate that is far enough from the fixed point to force the sign of the displacement after oracle rounding. Shrinking the image gives a strict contraction and transfers location accuracy to a small original residual in the nonexpansive case. For the exact circuit corollary, one common determinant bound gives a positive residual gap for each realizing cell. LP recovery in the cell of the approximate solution is therefore exact. The finite-sample Lipschitz extension in the corollary justifies the same stopping bound on attempted evaluations of a circuit whose stated promise fails. [Approximation theorem and exact circuit corollary](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/contractions.tex#L14-L158)
