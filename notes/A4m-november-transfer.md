# A4m · Fixed-point algorithms for exact market clearing

Two fixed-point results give new market algorithms. The unrestricted polynomial-time question remains open.

| Market result | What the source supplies | What the market proof supplies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Polynomial bit time on markets passing a factor LP** | OpenAI's November 24 manuscript gives polynomial total bit time for finding a small residual of a sup-norm nonexpansive self-map of a cube. Its accuracy parameter counts bits. | A path inequality and a polynomial-size LP certify nonexpansiveness of the frozen assignment map. Rational assignment solves answer the oracle queries. Preserving all frozen ties gives exact least prices through A4a's repair. |
| **Exact general search in $2^{O(\sqrt{\tau}\log(2+L))}\mathrm{poly}(L)$ bit time** | Ghorbani, Lopes and Mnich's October 20 preprint gives a least-fixed-point algorithm for a lattice map with unit increments in a supplied block. Its separate cube-root bound uses switch-graph structure. | Conservative splits and comparisons round the market map. Pinning a minimum vertex cover leaves independent scalar solves. Block search on the cover of size $\tau$ and rational recovery finish the algorithm. |

The path certificate covers some slope matrices outside the earlier column condition in A4a and can be computed before the tight support is known. An inverse-polynomial strict gap suffices by ordinary iteration. The general market algorithm works for arbitrary positive slopes and binary quantities using the October theorem alone. Its game bound uses $\min(n,k)$ in place of $\tau$ for $n$ targets and $k$ positive-prior types. ## Smaller search spaces and direct recovery

- Pin a vertex cover of size $\tau$ and use the November rational-circuit corollary on $2^\tau$ cut coordinates. The resulting exact solver takes $2^{O(\tau)}\mathrm{poly}(L)$ bit time. In particular, logarithmic cover size now suffices for arbitrary slopes and binary quantities, and $\min(n,k)=O(\log(2+L))$ suffices for the explicitly supplied game. A component LP evaluator extends the cut method to a supplied boundary with small remaining components. [Cut coordinates on a vertex cover](A4m-structural-elimination.md#cut-coordinates-on-a-vertex-cover)
- If deleting $r$ buyer and good coordinates leaves balanced slopes, a conditional convex quadratic program leaves only those $r$ coordinates for the October continuous search. The bit bound is $2^{O(\sqrt r\log(2+L))}\mathrm{poly}(L)$. There are further bounds using unbalanced biconnected blocks and graph separators. [Balanced interior and structural bounds](A4m-structural-elimination.md#a-convex-quadratic-program-for-a-balanced-interior)
- The October lattice search can read a feasible allocation from the integer transfers, with integral flow for integer quantities. A price-and-utility LP repairs the original demand constraints while keeping that allocation. [Integer transfer recovery](A4m-discrete-contraction.md#recover-a-clearing-from-the-integer-transfers)

## What remains

The factor LP is a sufficient condition. For the unrestricted problem the block recursion gives a subexponential time bound. One route is a polynomial-time fixed-point algorithm for the integer maps made of rational splits, conservative comparisons, additive merges and clipping. Another requires a polynomial-size max-norm formulation with an evaluator for all of its oracle queries. Two markets with the same local A4f formula require opposite extremal labels, so a single native-map evaluation does not give the manuscript's oracle. The reduced cut construction still has exponential dependence on the boundary size. [Interface examples](A4m-extremal-price-interface.md) · [Reduced cut construction](A4m-structural-elimination.md#cut-coordinates-on-a-vertex-cover)
