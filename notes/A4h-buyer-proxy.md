# A4h12 Apply the one-good solver to shared buyers

A4h's block algorithm works on trees joined at goods. B3d and A4h9 reveal a symmetric role for a buyer's fixed utility. An exact transformation makes every buyer's utility a good price, keeps the original goods, and turns every original trade into a two-good choice. The new goods-interaction graph is the original bipartite incidence graph. This brings mixed buyer and good articulations into the existing one-good algorithm. No tight-edge guesses are required. ## Replace one buyer by a good and a set of two-option buyers

Normalize reserves and outsides to zero, writing each offer as $b_{ij}-d_{ij}p_j$, with $d_{ij}>0$. We may omit $b_{ij}\le0$ since these edges can be used only in an outside tie at reserve, where sending the flow outside preserves clearance. Keep all original goods and capacities. For an original buyer $i$ of positive mass $m_i$ with $k_i$ retained offers, choose $K_i=1+\max(0,\max_j b_{ij})$, taking the latter maximum as zero if she has no offers.

Add a good $h_i$ with price $z_i$, capacity $(2k_i+1)m_i$, and zero reserve. For each retained offer $ij$, add a buyer of mass $2m_i$ with just two real options

$$
 1+K_i-z_i \quad\text{on }h_i,\qquad
 1+b_{ij}-d_{ij}p_j \quad\text{on }j.
$$

Add one more buyer of mass $2m_i$ who can only buy $h_i$, with offer $K_i-z_i$. All outside utilities are zero.

**Recover the old market.** If $z_i=0$, every new buyer incident to $h_i$ strictly demands it, overloading its capacity. Hence $z_i>0$ and $h_i$ sells out. The total mass of the new buyers is $(2k_i+2)m_i$, so their total departures from $h_i$ amount to exactly $m_i$. Each of those buyers retains at least $m_i$ on $h_i$. In particular the one-option buyer gives $u_i=K_i-z_i\ge0$ and can depart only to outside when $u_i=0$. Every two-option buyer gets utility $1+u_i>0$ at $h_i$. Its retention there implies $u_i\ge b_{ij}-d_{ij}p_j$. Its departure onto $j$ can occur only at equality. Use the departures as the allocation of the original buyer. They carry $m_i$, obey her best responses, and leave every original good's sales unchanged.

**Lift an old clearing.** Given an original clearing flow and its maximum utilities $u_i$, set $z_i=K_i-u_i$. This is strictly positive. Route the original flow on $ij$ from the corresponding two-option buyer, and route the original outside flow from the one-option buyer. Put every remaining mass on $h_i$. All used options maximize utility and the $h_i$ totals are the prescribed capacities. Thus the clearing price sets correspond by $p\mapsto(p,K-u(p))$. This map preserves componentwise order because utility decreases as original prices increase. In particular the transformed minimum projects to the original minimum. No supply perturbation is needed for this equivalence.

## An exact solver on bounded incidence blocks

The transformed instance uses one new good per retained buyer and one new buyer per retained edge plus one per retained buyer. Its margins and offers have polynomial binary length. Its goods-interaction edge $h_i j$ is exactly the original viable incidence edge $ij$. The extra one-option buyer adds no graph edge.

**Corollary.** For each fixed $b$, exact minimum prices and a clearing allocation can be computed in polynomial bit time on markets whose viable bipartite incidence graph has at most $b$ vertices in every biconnected block. Indeed, each new goods block has at most $b$ goods and $O(b^2)$ two-option buyers assigned to it. Assign each one-option buyer to a block containing its good or to an isolated good, so it adds at most one buyer per vertex. This is exactly the A4h bound on original buyers and goods per block. Its additive message count and rational breakpoint recovery handle arbitrary branching and depth with binary margins. The graph promise is decidable by block decomposition. Use the old prices and the departures to recover the physical market in polynomial bit time.

For example, join a long chain of four-cycles alternately at buyer and good vertices, and add many private leaf goods at the shared buyers. Choose a slope mismatch in each four-cycle. The incidence blocks still have at most four vertices. The original goods-interaction graph can have large cliques from the high-degree buyers, the whole slope system need not factor, and deleting a fixed number of shared goods can leave components with arbitrarily many types. The transformed graph keeps the small blocks and exposes each shared buyer as a good articulation.

For Bayesian simple security games, use the viable incidence graph from B2. A4c capacity changes preserve this graph bound, and reserves and outside folds can only delete viable edges. The proxy algorithm therefore supplies L4b's clearing oracle uniformly. There are polynomially many rational queries, and the transformation and each transformed market solve take polynomial bit time for fixed $b$. Weak losses and gains are also covered under the same incidence-block bound on the weak viable graph. Use a strict perturbation small enough to preserve every original omitted-edge gap, solve the perturbed game, and use A1's support LP once. ### Larger blocks when the original margins have few values

Each incidence block can instead have a vertex cover of fixed size $k$, if the original market has a fixed number $s$ of distinct positive masses and capacities. The bounded side can differ from block to block. The transformed block has the same vertex cover. Adding unary child offers leaves that cover valid, so the A4b bounded-deletion oracle can evaluate each adaptive breakpoint query on the augmented block. Enumerating all subsets of size at most $k$ finds a cover at polynomial cost.

At any genuine subtree, every load level is a Hall difference of subsets of transformed masses and capacities. The transformed margins use integer multiples of the original margin values. The sum of all coefficients is $O(|E|+|V|)$, since each two-option or one-option replacement has mass $2m_i$, and the capacity of $h_i$ is $(2k_i+1)m_i$. There are at most $(O(|E|+|V|))^s$ such signed coefficient vectors. Thus the adaptive sweep has polynomially many actual levels even if a transformed block contains arbitrarily many vertices. The original games with a fixed number of distinct priors and defender gains inherit this class, because their threshold queries and uniform supply perturbations have only a fixed number of physical margin values per query. The transformation also transfers graph conditions on selected goods of the new interaction graph to conditions on selected vertices of the original incidence graph. For example a forest after a fixed number of deletions on either side gives an exact solver by A4b. A certified tie relating a buyer utility to a good price is a positive-slope price relation between the corresponding proxy and original goods, so it can also be passed to A4h7's certified grouping. The unrestricted incidence graph and the unrestricted two-option market remain general cases of the same problem.
