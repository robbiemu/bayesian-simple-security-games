# A4m · What the extremal-price interface requires

The November maximal-surplus theorem is an algorithm for a specific oracle. At the largest nonnegative unmet demand of a consistent hypothesis, every maximizing signed coordinate has to get its correct sign. The contraction proof obtains those signs from the residual of a sup-norm nonexpansive or contractive map. A4f has a stronger aggregate sign inequality in its own norm, but its individual coordinates need not provide those labels. [Extremal-price theorem](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/overview.tex#L58-L76) · [Proof of the max-norm label](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/contractions.tex#L77-L94)

## Identical local formulas and opposite required signs

Take two buyers of mass $3$, goods of capacities $(1,5)$, slope matrix and trial signed node vector
\[
 D=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&8\\1/2&4\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 h=(6,6,-6,-6).
\]
Use intercept matrices
\[
 b^A=\begin{pmatrix}1&50\\7&23\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 b^B=\begin{pmatrix}11/2&34\\1&25\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All four hinges are strictly inactive near $h$ in both markets because the four cover values at $h$ are $(9,54,9,30)$. Their A4f maps therefore agree on an open neighborhood of $h$, including their exact replies and active affine formulas. Use the same sufficiently small regularization and step in both maps and a common box extending past the displayed coordinates.

As the regularization tends to zero, the signed fixed points converge to $(10,3,-8,-5)$ in market A and $(2,9,-7,-4)$ in market B. The limiting active trees omit edge $11$ and edge $21$, respectively. The limiting flows are $((0,3),(1,2))$ and $((1,2),(0,3))$. Each positive tree edge is tight, each omitted offer has strict slack, and the node balances hold. To justify the limit, on each tree use three independent signed node balances, box-interior complementarity, and the equation $\sum_v h_v=0$. The latter is the unused node balance at positive regularization, since total mass equals capacity. At zero regularization this square system is nonsingular. The incidence balances determine the tree flows, and the tight-offer equations have a one-dimensional nullspace generated by a strictly positive vector, fixed by the sum constraint. Perturbed solutions converge and keep the strict signs.

The limiting errors from the trial are $(4,-3,-2,1)$ and $(-4,3,-1,2)$. Thus buyer 1 is the unique largest absolute-error coordinate and needs opposite labels, even with the exact signed total supplied. A single local formula likewise cannot identify which of the two omitted edges is active at the solution. The example holds for a common regularization below both A4d recovery bounds. It leaves open queries using the full bids or adaptive information.

For a strict $\ell_1$ contraction with constant $q$ and fixed point $h^*$, write $e=h^*-h$. Every sign vector $s$ with $s\cdot e=\|e\|_1$ satisfies
\[
 s\cdot(F(h)-h)\ \ge\ (1-q)\|e\|_1 .
\]
This follows by applying $s$ to $F(h)-F(h^*)+e$. The certified object is an aggregate over a sign pattern. On the exact-total subspace $\sum_i e_i=0$, each nontrivial subset has an open sign cone, so the exact linear absolute-maximum representation of this norm needs $2^{d-1}-1$ subset forms up to sign. Physical subset quotes are also not closed under coordinatewise medians, an operation in the manuscript. For three coordinate unit vectors the singleton medians are zero, while their total-sum median is one. Thus feeding the subset forms to the manuscript would require an oracle that also handles proposals off the physical image. [Oracle recursions and medians](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/prices.tex)

## A fixed market excludes separate coordinate changes

Use $m_i=c_j=1$, slopes $d=(1,1;1,2)$ and bids $b_{ij}=1+d_{ij}$. Put $t=\varepsilon^2$. For small $\varepsilon>0$, choose a common base $(u_1,p_1,u_2,p_2)$ by solving
\[
 (1+t)u_1+p_1=2-\varepsilon,\quad
 u_2+(1+t)p_1=2-\varepsilon,\quad
 (1+t)u_2+2p_2=3-\varepsilon,\quad
 u_1+(1+t)p_2=2-\varepsilon .
\]
The system is nonsingular at $\varepsilon=0$, with all four coordinates one. Use four separate interior cells, where only the edge selected for the output row is active. In order $(u_1,-p_1,u_2,-p_2)$ these edges are $11,21,22,12$. Set the selected edge's endpoints to their common bases and the two other unsigned coordinates to four in a box of width five. The selected edge is strictly active, the rest are strictly inactive, and the selected output fixes its common coordinate by the displayed equations.

In each such cell perturb the two selected signed input coordinates by the corresponding entries of $\tau v$ for $v=(8,9,10,6)$ and sufficiently small $\tau>0$. The selected output increases by more than $\tau v_i$ in every row. In units of $a\tau/\varepsilon$, the four excesses are $1-8t,1-9t,2-10t,2-6t$, positive when $t<1/9$. Under any separate strictly monotone coordinate changes, take the largest transformed increment between a common base and its own base plus $\tau v_i$. Its corresponding cell produces a still larger transformed increment in that output, while only two of the four input increments were used. This contradicts sup-norm nonexpansiveness. The common bases make the comparison work even if the four rows are realized in different cells.

There is also a representation bound for a norm-only translation. A bi-Lipschitz embedding from a full-dimensional $\ell_1^d$ box to $\ell_\infty^M$ with distortion $D<d/(d-1)$ has $M\ge2^{d-1}$ for $d\ge2$. A Lipschitz map is differentiable almost everywhere. Normalize the derivative so each row has entries of absolute value at most one. On a vector of $d$ signs some row must attain a scalar product of absolute value at least $d/D>d-1$. After a global sign it must agree with every sign, so each row can serve at most one antipodal pair. For a binary-small contractivity gap below $1/d$, using distortion alone to preserve nonexpansiveness needs such a low distortion. This is a bound on norm-embedding transfers.

## An explicit max-norm lift with all cuts

Set $s_S=\sum_{v\in S}h_v$ for all node subsets. For an edge $ij$ crossing $S$ with buyer $i$ inside, add the term
\[
 (a/\varepsilon)\max\big((1+d_{ij})s_S,\
 b_{ij}+s_{S\setminus\{i\}}+d_{ij}s_{S\cup\{j\}}\big).
\]
For good $j$ inside instead, add
\[
 (a/\varepsilon)\min\big((1+d_{ij})s_S,\
 -b_{ij}+s_{S\cup\{i\}}+d_{ij}s_{S\setminus\{j\}}\big).
\]
Add $-a\sum_{v\in S}g_v$ and the residual self coefficient
\[
 (1-a\varepsilon)-(a/\varepsilon)\sum_{ij\ \mathrm{crossing}\ S}(1+d_{ij})
\]
on $s_S$. Choose $a$ small enough that the latter is nonnegative. Each selected row has nonnegative coefficients summing to $1-a\varepsilon$. On physical cut vectors, the formulas reproduce the sum of the unclipped A4f outputs. Separately clipping a physical node $v$ replaces each cut containing it by the clip of $s_S$ between $s_{S\setminus\{v\}}+L_v$ and $s_{S\setminus\{v\}}+U_v$. This map is sup-norm nonexpansive. Compose these clips and a final common cube clip large enough for all physical cut sums of the original box. The resulting circuit is a strict sup-norm contraction and its unique fixed point is the embedded market solution.

This is an exact translation to the manuscript's circuit problem with exponential explicit size. It isolates the missing task of finding a polynomial-size lift that can be evaluated on independent proposed coordinates, or a search that keeps proposals physical and still uses the cut information. Eliminating independent interior vertices before the lift reduces its dimension to the number of cuts of a vertex cover. [Reduced cut construction](A4m-structural-elimination.md#cut-coordinates-on-a-vertex-cover)

The stronger bound above limits only near-isometric norm lifts, and the separate-coordinate example limits only coordinatewise conjugations of the raw map. The LP part of the manuscript uses an orthogonality alternative between a subspace and its orthogonal complement. Ordinary market flow incidence and weighted demand incidence form a different pair. On $K_{2,2}$, let $d=(1,11;4,5/4)$, $f=(1,-1,-1,1)$ in edge order, and $h=(1,2,1,1)$ in signed node order. The ordinary incidence gives $Af=0$, and $B^{\mathsf T}h=(0,-10,-2,3/4)$ for the weighted incidence. Reorienting edges by $f$ gives a positive vector in the first space and a nonnegative vector in the second with positive scalar product. Thus this pair cannot supply that LP alternative without a different construction. The signed vector here is a subspace witness.
