# Prior constructions for eliminating averaging from feasibility systems

The dyadic min/max circuit in H4 has a direct predecessor from 2008. Its use with one-sided gate inequalities was explicit by the time of the work on tropical central paths. The literature search found the circuit and both endpoints of the reduction. The candidate contribution is the exact decision reduction obtained by combining the published circuit with a uniform rational violation bound. [Bezem, Nieuwenhuis and Rodríguez-Carbonell, §3](https://www.cs.upc.edu/~roberto/papers/dam08.pdf#page=4) · [Allamigeon, Benchimol, Gaubert and Joswig, Lemma 20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.4161#page=20)

## Identifying the same circuit

In §3 of their 2008 counterexample, Bezem, Nieuwenhuis and Rodríguez-Carbonell double all previous offsets at each new layer and introduce the pair $(\min(a,b)+1,\max(a,b))$. The final depth-$r$ system therefore has offsets $2^{r-j}$ at layers $1\le j\le r$. Divide values and offsets by $2^r$, then subtract the cumulative common translation $\sum_{k=1}^j2^{-k-1}$ from both outputs of layer $j$. Writing the previous normalized inputs as $a,b$, the resulting pair is

```math
  (\min(a,b)+2^{-j-1},\ \max(a,b)-2^{-j-1}).
```

Sorting these coordinates before the next step yields H4 with initial width one. The authors count $2r+2$ variables and $3r+1$ equations including their boundary equation. Their theorem concerns exponential running time of the Butkovič–Zimmermann algorithm. [2008 paper, construction and Theorem 1](https://www.cs.upc.edu/~roberto/papers/dam08.pdf#page=4)

Allamigeon, Benchimol, Gaubert and Joswig give a particularly close formulation. Their $j$th map on all of $\mathbb R^2$ takes $(a,b)$ to $(1+\min(a,b),\,1-2^{-j}+\max(a,b))$. Its common translation per stage is $1-2^{-j-1}$. Lemma 20, equations (21a) and (22), also encodes each stage by componentwise upper inequalities and uses monotonicity to identify the greatest feasible evaluation. Their boundary data vary one parameter to study a tropical central path. As the input difference varies over $[-1,1]$, this covers all normalized input differences relevant to H4. The later SIAM Review article repeats the recurrence and the one-sided argument in Proposition 20 and equation (30). [Long and winding central paths](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.4161#page=20) · [SIAM Review, p. 149](https://www.cmap.polytechnique.fr/~gaubert/PAPERS/sigest-paper.pdf#page=27)

Both outputs of the normalized recurrence have constant sum $a_0+b_0$. If their incoming gap is $d_{j-1}$, the next gap equals $\left|d_{j-1}-2^{-j}\right|$. By induction it is at most $2^{-j}$ when the initial gap is at most one. Thus the larger output exceeds the midpoint by at most $2^{-j-1}$. Scaling offsets by $W>0$ gives error $W2^{-j-1}$ for $|a_0-b_0|\le W$. This midpoint bound follows directly from the published recurrence.

## A limit on local formulations

The following deduction helps state precisely what an exact feasibility reduction accomplishes. Consider any existential conjunction of binary max-atoms and difference inequalities over finite rationals, with arbitrarily many auxiliary variables and arbitrary rational offsets. Fix $y=0$. Suppose that for $0\le z\le W$ its projection onto $(x,z)$ contains every point with $x\le z/2$ and contains only points with $x\le z/2+\epsilon$. If $m$ is the number of binary max rows, then

```math
                      2^m\,4\epsilon\ \ge\ W .
```

In particular, an exact fixed finite gadget for the midpoint hypograph is impossible on this interval, and the number of binary max rows required for one-sided approximation grows logarithmically in $W/\epsilon$.

To prove the inequality, select one right-hand term in each max row. Each of the at most $2^m$ selections gives a system of difference bounds. Projecting away one auxiliary variable combines every lower and upper bound on that variable, leaving only difference bounds on the remaining variables. A nonempty projected branch on the slice $y=0$ is consequently described by upper or lower bounds on $x,z,z-x$. If it contains $(s/2,s)$ and $(t/2,t)$, with $s<t$, then it contains $(t/2,(s+t)/2)$. Each of its three relevant differences lies between the corresponding endpoint values. At this new point $x-z/2=(t-s)/4$, so $t-s\le4\epsilon$. The intervals spanned by each branch's boundary points cover the rational points of $[0,W]$. There are finitely many such intervals, so their closures cover $[0,W]$, giving the bound. This proof applies even when the auxiliary-variable constraint graph contains cycles.

## The candidate exact contribution

Once the finite source box is fixed, a common LP-slack variable measures the worst average-row violation for each selection of maximizing terms. Clearing denominators and bounding determinants yields one input-computable positive lower bound on the optimum of every infeasible selection that admits a relaxation. Choose the circuit error strictly below that bound. Any compiled witness then identifies a feasible **exact** branch of the source system. A rational LP on that branch recovers a source witness, which may differ from the projected compiled values. Full graph cycles and equality at the threshold fit this argument because it uses simultaneous linear feasibility. [H4 proof, §§2–5](H4-ssg-to-mean-payoff.md)

The two published circuit constructions establish why the gate count stays polynomial at the required precision. The fixed-branch gap explains why that precision preserves the decision on the entire instance. The focused search through 22 November 2026 located the gate construction and both ends of the game reduction. It identified no earlier source containing the full composition or its exact branch-repair conclusion. [Bodirsky and Mamino, open question in §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.04184#page=4) · [Bodirsky, Loho and Skomra, reduction diagram and §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.09646v2#S3) · [Warsaw seminar, 28 October 2026](https://mimuw.edu.pl/en/seminars/talk/quasipolynomial-algorithms-for-mean-payoff-and-simple-stochastic-games/)

## Matching the expanded BLS domain

The December 2025 version allows both $x_i\le c+x_j$ and values of $-\infty$. For example, $x_i\le x_i-1$ forces $x_i=-\infty$. First remove empty maxima using the forced-bottom closure in Section 3 of the [threshold proof](H4-ssg-to-mean-payoff.md#3-remove-the-box-and-allow-strict-inequalities). On the residual system, fix a candidate support of finite coordinates and a branch for each max row with finite left-hand side. A consistent choice must supply a finite selected term for each such row, finite operands of each average with finite left side, and finite values of all pinned variables. Drop inequalities with bottom left side and retain the induced rational linear inequalities on the finite coordinates. Appending $R\ge0,\ -R\le x_i\le R$ gives a pointed polyhedron. A Cramer bound over all possible coefficients gives a uniform, polynomial-bit $B\ge1$ such that every feasible selection has a witness with finite coordinates in $[-B,B]$. The same pointed lift with $0\le\rho\le1$ gives a uniform inverse-exponential lower bound on a positive optimal common average-row violation, by taking a vertex of the optimal face. The encoder calculates the two bounds without enumerating supports. [Expanded BLS Definition 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.09646v2#S3)

Take $W=2B$ and choose the circuit error below that violation bound. Work first over $\mathbb Q\cup\{-\infty\}$. Add two difference rows $x_i\le x_j+W/2,\ x_i\le x_k+W/2$ along with the circuit approximation to every $x_i\le(x_j+x_k)/2$. In a bounded source witness all finite operands differ by at most $W$, so each added upper bound is at least their average. A bottom operand requires a bottom left side and all gate evaluations are defined. Conversely, a finite target left side forces both original operands finite. If their difference exceeds $W$, the tighter new bound is at most their true average. At smaller separations use the circuit estimate. Thus every finite left side satisfies the exact row to the required tolerance, and a support and branch chosen from the target witness admit exact LP repair as above. This argument gives an affine max-atom system with bottom values. Its known correspondence to a deterministic game with a designated start is Theorem 3.4 of Akian, Gaubert and Guterman. [The correspondence between tropical convexity and mean payoff games](https://www.cmap.polytechnique.fr/~gaubert/PAPERS/220_462.pdf)

One can also compile that last system directly to finite max-atoms. Introduce one finite reference $x_0$ for all constants, so only homogeneous shifted max rows remain. Let $n$ count all variables including $x_0$, let $C$ bound the absolute values of the offsets, and choose $M>nC$. Add a fallback term $x_0-M$ to every row. From an extended solution, clamp every coordinate up to $x_0-M$. An increased left side may use the fallback, and all right sides are monotone. In the other direction, select one satisfied term of every finite row, marking fallback selections. Starting at $0$, follow all selected original edges to obtain the reachable set $S$. Any $i\in S$ is reached by a simple path of length less than $n$, so telescoping gives $x_i\ge x_0-nC>x_0-M$. No row at $i$ can have selected a fallback. Hence the assignment retaining $x$ on $S$ and setting all other coordinates to $-\infty$ satisfies all original rows. Binarizing maxima, clearing rational offsets and flooring yields finite integer max-atom feasibility with polynomial total bit length. This proves the domain extension without identifying the finite support in advance.
