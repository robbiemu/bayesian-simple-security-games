# L3. Prior partial results for Bayesian simple security games

The supplied *Bayesian Simple Security Games*, subtitled *Polynomial-time questions, partial algorithms, and market-theoretic reductions*, collects three working drafts dated November 21, 2026. It contains an exact rank-one algorithm, an exact fixed-type algorithm and a normalized additive approximation scheme for arbitrary type counts. The general exact question remains open in the sources checked. The references below use physical PDF pages, since each draft restarts its numbering. [Collected drafts, pp. 1–2](../sources/prior-collection.md#collection-guide) · [Conitzer’s problem, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08796)

**The missing endpoint test in Part I.** Take one type, three targets, \(r=2\), \(a=(2,0,0)\) and all losses and gains equal to one. The parametric objective becomes \(2-x_1+t\sum_i x_i\). At \(t=0\), the optimal coverage sums range from 1 to 3. For every \(t>0\), the largest optimal sum is at most 1. The midpoint-only search on p. 6 never satisfies its displayed bracket (10). First compute the minimum and maximum sum on the optimal face at zero and return an interpolated optimizer whenever they bracket \(r\). Otherwise its minimum exceeds \(r\), since the all-ones vector is optimal there. The strict bracket then holds, and the finite rational breakpoint and one-sided endpoint-vertex arguments on pp. 6–7 apply. [Part I, pp. 5–7](../sources/prior-collection.md#part-i)

**The two-good auction example.** With zero outside options and reserves, take utilities \(A=(2/5-p_1,-p_2)\), \(B=(2-2p_1,2-p_2)\) and \(C=(1-p_1,2-2p_2)\). Match \(B\) to good 1 and \(C\) to good 2 at zero, then consider \(A\). Maintaining the alternating tree requires \(B\)'s tie, hence prices \((t,2t)\). \(C\) changes demand at \(t=1/3\) inside the tree. Line 7 of Dütting–Henzinger–Weber’s published Pseudocode 2 tests only an item outside the tree or a utility-segment endpoint. The first such event is \(A\)'s outside tie at \(2/5\). Prices are now \((2/5,4/5)\) and never decrease, so \(B\)'s attainable utility is at most \(6/5\). At \((2/5,7/10)\), assigning \(A\) outside, \(B\) to good 2 and \(C\) to good 1 is envy-free and gives \(B\) utility \(13/10\). This admissible run violates bidder optimality. The example is from the collection, and its implication for the printed pseudocode follows by the substitutions above. [Part II, pp. 15–16](../sources/prior-collection.md#part-ii) · [Dütting, Henzinger and Weber, Pseudocode 2 and Theorem 3.9](https://ingmarweber.de/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/An_Expressive_Mechanism_for-Auctions_on_the_Web_TEAC.pdf#page=16) · [Oki and Sato, footnote 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10563v3#page=22)

## Scope of the approximation and of the published results

Part III invokes a separate, applicable theorem. Arunachaleswaran, Barman and Rathi compute the componentwise minimum nonnegative envy-free prices of a square assignment instance when the slope magnitudes are integer powers of \(1+\delta\), including negative exponents. After rounding, the collection has \(N=\lceil64(n+m)/\epsilon\rceil\) unit buyers and at most \(n(N+1)\) real rooms. Dummy buyers clear unsold rooms at their reserve. Dummy rooms price at zero in the minimum square solution and implement outside options. Bipartite flow integrality establishes the converse even for fractional assignments. Their prescribed-total Theorem 3 is an approximation result for rent division. [Part III, pp. 20–22](../sources/prior-collection.md#part-iii) · [Arunachaleswaran, Barman and Rathi, §§2.2, 4, 6–7 and Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.04163)

At a supply jump of total size \(\Delta\le n/N\), raising reserves along \(tq\) bridges the smaller and larger markets’ minimum prices continuously. A fixed assignment gives affine price branches with determinant-bounded slopes. Their number can grow exponentially while the logarithm of the Lipschitz bound on p. 25 stays polynomial in the expanded input. The lost allocation on positive-price goods is at most \(\Delta\), including at \(t=1\) when the *old* allocation is used. Price-positive goods in the old market must have sold their full supply, so the cap at \(N+1\) copies preserves the lower score bound wherever it is needed. Correcting the resource sum by at most \(\epsilon/128\) and restoring priors gives the displayed \(7\epsilon/64\) defender bound. Each retained type has regret at most \(\epsilon/16\), and omitted types take exact final best responses. The dependence on \(1/\epsilon\) prevents using exponentially small \(\epsilon\) to obtain an exact polynomial algorithm. [Part III, pp. 23–28](../sources/prior-collection.md#part-iii)

- Oki and Sato already give the factorized-slope rescaling and scaled Lyapunov potential (Assumption 4.1 and Theorem 6.4). Part I adds the security-game budget constraint and scalar recovery. The authors’ October follow-up, absent from the collection, allows integral multi-unit trade capacities and reaches minimum equilibrium prices in finitely many steps. Individual updates are strongly polynomial in its oracle model with unit-cost exact \(\log,\exp\). Appendix B.5 bounds changes of the overdemanded set by a quantity linear in numerical total capacity and proves finiteness within a fixed set. A4 gives a superpolynomial iteration lower bound for the September auction and the October trading-network algorithm on its single-seller reduction, even with two units per good. [September preprint, §§4.1 and 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10563v3#page=14) · [October preprint, §§2, 3.1, Theorem 4.4 and Appendix B.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40626) · [A4 lower bound](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#lower-bound-for-the-directional-ascending-auctions)
- Galichon and Jacquet already formulate linearly transferable utility matching with type masses and map stable outcomes to equilibria of a generalized hide-and-seek bimatrix game. At floors \(\ell_i\), the fixed-threshold market’s stability inequalities read \((u_k-o_k)+b_{ki}(p_i-\ell_i)\ge a_{ki}-o_k-b_{ki}\ell_i\), tight on used edges. Their correspondence supplies a finite-game formulation. Selecting minimum prices and enforcing the prescribed coverage sum require additional algorithms. [Galichon and Jacquet, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12200#page=3)
- Chan and Jiang’s bilinear-game FPTAS requires a bounded-treewidth constraint-resource graph. In the direct many-type representation, each type row connects through a separate attack coordinate to every defender target, giving a \(K_{m,n}\) minor. Xu’s exact security-game reduction expressly sets aside the Bayesian extension. A more recent exact rent algorithm with room-specific budgets assumes the common unit rent slope \(v_{ki}-p_i\). [Chan and Jiang, Theorem 2](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2018/0021.pdf#page=4) · [Xu, §1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.02377#page=3) · [Gangam, Taherijam and Vazirani, §§3.1–3.4](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol360-fsttcs2025/html/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2025.32/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2025.32.html)

## What the investigation adds

A1’s weighted Hall interval at fixed coverage, direct minimum-slack proof for weak signs, and NP-completeness of *selecting* a threshold-zero equilibrium under flat utilities are distinct from the collected results. B2 uses Part II’s threshold order and adds an interval theorem for component budgets and a polynomial composition algorithm with arbitrarily many components of bounded type count. L1 gives a strict-payoff edge-revisit trace for Catcher–Evader. B1 counts feasible signatures and gives an exact path-length family. L2 supplies the fixed-seed non-repetition and rational arithmetic argument for the \(2^{O(\sqrt{nm}\log(nm+1))}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) continuation bound. A2–A3 give a log-price solver with arbitrary slope matrices when intercepts have a common affine offset representation. External priority for these additions remains unverified. ## Conditional exact reduction to a minimum-price oracle

The later L4b proof gives a public route from a clearing oracle to the requested budget. It recovers the exact threshold, fixes its capacities, and raises reserve floors continuously between the least and greatest clearing prices. The strict Hall condition identifies the least prices along this path. A denominator bound permits exact recovery where the total price equals the budget. The original flow is then recovered at those prices. [Threshold, reserve path and budget proof](L4-newton-and-continuation.md#exact-threshold-and-budget-recovery-with-a-clearing-oracle) · [Recovering least prices with one any-clearing call](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#one-call-at-enlarged-capacities)

**Sufficient oracle.** Given binary rational buyer masses, positive rational supplies, rational reserves, outside utilities and strictly decreasing pair-specific affine utilities, return equilibrium prices componentwise at most every other equilibrium price. Buyers may split mass among best options, unsold supply is permitted at reserve, and a good above reserve must sell out. Compatible flows can be recovered from the prices by one rational flow LP. The following deduction combines Part III’s reserve path with A1’s exact support repair. It reduces the full exact game to polynomially many calls to this oracle. A4 gives a polynomial-time test for a candidate minimum price. Finding those prices in polynomial bit time is the remaining algorithmic target. [A4 price certificate](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#certifying-a-candidate-minimum-price)

1. **Bracket the resource total.** Treat \(r=0,n\) directly. First assume \(b_{ki},d_i>0\), and use \(o_k=\max_i(a_{ki}-b_{ki})\). Write \(D=\max_i d_i\), \(H=\sum_i1/d_i\). At zero reserves query positive supplies \(c_i(\lambda)=\lambda/d_i\). Greater supplies weakly decrease minimum prices by Part III, Lemma 2. At \(\lambda=0\) use \(q=\mathbf1\) and the all-outside allocation as the initial upper price endpoint. All types are indifferent to their best fully covered target, and zero supply is fully sold at zero flow. At \(\lambda=D+1\) every supply exceeds one and the only clearing prices are zero. Return immediately if a queried total is \(r\), using Part II’s market-to-game conversion. Otherwise bisect until the price totals at \(\lambda_L,\lambda_U\) straddle \(r\) and \(\Delta=(\lambda_U-\lambda_L)H\le\eta/(D+1)\), for the tolerance specified in step 4. This takes polynomially many rational bisections in the input length and \(\log(1/\eta)\).
2. **Tune reserves in the original dimension.** Let \(q\) be the stored lower-supply price, and let \(P(t)\) be the minimum price at the upper supplies and reserves \(tq\), \(0\le t\le1\). The old allocation remains feasible for buyers at \(q\). Domination and continuity (Part III, Lemmas 2–3) give \(P(1)=q\), \(0\le P(t)\le q\le\mathbf1\), and a continuous nondecreasing path from the other bracket endpoint. Their proofs use transportation duality and apply to arbitrary rational masses. Existence of minimum prices for real \(t\) also follows from the square-market minimum-price theorem by finite rational-mass replication for existence only. For the *precision bound*, fix the positive flow edges, the outside users and the goods with slack supply. At these fixed supplies their flow conditions are independent of \(t\). Equate each used option to a designated best option in its row, impose that it weakly dominates every option, set slack-supply prices to \(tq_i\), and impose all reserve floors. This is a price polyhedron in \(n\) variables with fixed rational matrix and right-hand side affine in \(t\). For every compatible pattern the minimum price uniquely minimizes the sum and is a vertex. If \(B\) bounds the bit lengths of entries of the matrix and both right-hand sides, row clearing bounds integer coefficients by \(C=2^{(n+3)B}\). Cramer’s rule bounds each branch slope by \(n!C^n\). A continuous selection from finitely many affine branches is piecewise affine, so \(n\,n!C^n\) bounds the slope of the total. At zero reserves the same vertex argument with fixed right-hand side bounds the bits of \(q\) in the original input (and the exceptional \(q=\mathbf1\) is explicit). The logarithm of the slope bound is therefore polynomial. Dyadic bisection therefore obtains \(|\sum_iP_i-r|\le\eta\) with polynomially many oracle queries. [Part III, pp. 22–25](../sources/prior-collection.md#part-iii)
3. **Bound the threshold error.** For \(t<1\) take any compatible allocation at \(P=P(t)\). For \(S=\{i:P_i<q_i\}\), every type previously demanding any good in \(S\) now strictly prefers \(S\) to its complement and its outside option. Old positive-price goods were full, so the new flow into \(S\) is at least the old supply of \(S\). Outside \(S\), any \(P_i=q_i>0\) is above reserve and full. Hence total unused supply on positive-price goods is at most \(\Delta\). At \(t=1\) take the stored old allocation instead, giving the same bound including the all-outside endpoint. Assign outside mass to a target attaining \(o_k\) at full coverage. Whenever outside is optimal, that target has \(P_i=1\). All resulting attacks are exact best responses at \(P\). Writing \(Q_i\) for their aggregate, \(d_iQ_i\le\lambda_U\) if \(P_i<1\), and \(d_iQ_i\ge\lambda_U-D\Delta\) if \(P_i>0\). [Part III, pp. 26–27](../sources/prior-collection.md#part-iii)
4. **Recover an exact original equilibrium.** Keep the attack support and zero/interior/full coverage classes from step 3. In the closed original support LP keep the attack equalities and inequalities, row probabilities and box constraints exact. Relax the budget by \(|\sum_i x_i-r|\le s\) and each required defender threshold inequality by \(s\). Minimize \(0\le s\le1\), allowing \(\lambda\in[0,D+1]\) and using the bounded type-value box from A1. A uniform determinant bound is available *before* the search. If \(V\) is the number of variables and \(M\ge1\) bounds the integer matrix entries after clearing the original rational template rows, every positive basic \(s\) is at least \(1/(V!M^V)\). Choose \(\eta=1/(2V!M^V)\). The strategies above with \(\lambda=\lambda_U\) witness \(s\le\max(\eta,D\Delta)\le\eta\). The compact LP thus has optimum zero. The recovered solution has exactly the required budget and both players best-respond. For weak signs use A1’s polynomial reduction to a strictly positive game and repair its support on the original data. Assign discarded zero-prior types exact best responses and decompose the coverages as on p. 28.
