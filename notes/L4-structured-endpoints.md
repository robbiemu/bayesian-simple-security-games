# L4c Structure at one answer endpoint suffices

L4b finds an equilibrium at one supplied budget using market queries. The queried markets can have different positive-price goods. The Hall search from A4h6 lets us answer some threshold queries without solving their full markets. The original orientation needs structure only on the covered goods at one endpoint. The reciprocal orientation needs it only on the participating buyer types at the other endpoint. ## The two endpoint promises

Assume positive attacker losses and defender gains and discard zero-prior types. Normalize row outside payoffs to zero by writing $b_{ij}=a_{ij}-\max_h(a_{ih}-d_{ih})$. At threshold $\lambda>0$, let $x^-(\lambda)$ and $x^+(\lambda)$ be the least and greatest market prices at capacities $c_j=\lambda/\beta_j$ and zero reserves. Run the A1 zero-threshold test first. If it fails, the compatible thresholds for the supplied budget $0<r<n$ have positive least and greatest elements $\lambda_{\min}$ and $\lambda_{\max}$.

Fix a class $\mathcal C$ of slope-labeled offer graphs. Assume polynomial recognition, inheritance under vertex and edge deletion, and a uniform exact polynomial-bit-time minimum-price solver for graphs in the class with arbitrary rational masses, capacities, reserves, and outside utilities. For a candidate active set $A$, define its folded graph by placing all other goods at zero, including their best offer in each buyer's outside, and keeping only strictly viable edges on $A$. In the transpose use the reciprocal offers and slopes. A4h9's recognizable factorized core with factorized private markets is one example of $\mathcal C$. So are bounded incidence blocks via the [buyer proxy](A4h-buyer-proxy.md#an-exact-solver-on-bounded-incidence-blocks).

**Theorem.** An exact equilibrium at $r$ is obtainable in polynomial bit time if either of the following promises holds.

- The folded graph of the goods priced strictly above zero at $x^+(\lambda_{\max})$ belongs to $\mathcal C$.
- The reciprocal folded graph of the buyer types with strictly positive normalized utility at $x^-(\lambda_{\min})$ belongs to $\mathcal C$. These types are the goods in the reciprocal market.

The promises concern the endpoint of the budget search. The algorithm need not know its active set or which of the two promises holds. To combine several available classes, use the union of their recognizers on each trial and dispatch a successful recognition to the corresponding solver. A finite union of hereditary classes is hereditary. Disconnected components can also use distinct solvers when each component belongs to the union.

## Search from the greatest compatible threshold

Given a threshold trial $\lambda>0$, compute $x^+(\lambda)$ as the minimum price of the market with capacities $c_j-\delta$, where $\delta=1/(2nD)$ and $D$ is a common denominator of the trial's unshifted capacities and buyer masses. L4b's two-sided Hall argument proves the equality of these price vectors. Run A4h6's restricted Hall search. It maintains a set $A$ certified to be active at this trial minimum, pins other goods at reserve, and folds their offers into outside. Before each restricted solve, test $\mathcal C$ on that folded graph. Shrinking $A$ raises the outside option, so it deletes vertices and may delete additional edges.

If the test fails, return $\lambda\le\lambda_{\max}$ for this bisection query. In fact, at any $\lambda>\lambda_{\max}$, strong threshold order gives $x^+(\lambda)\le x^+(\lambda_{\max})$. Its active goods are a subset of those at the answer endpoint. The promise and heredity would therefore force the folded graph on $A$ to pass. If every test passes, A4h6 finds $x^+(\lambda)$ in at most $n+1$ restricted solves. Now compare its price sum to $r$, as in L4b. This implements the same monotone test as an unrestricted oracle.

Find $\lambda_{\max}$ by exact bisection and rational reconstruction. At a budget-$r$ equilibrium attaining it, it is the upper endpoint of the fixed-coverage threshold interval. The A1 cut formulas bound its reduced denominator by $D_m\sum_j D_\beta/\beta_j$, where $D_m,D_\beta$ are common denominators of the priors and inverse gains. Use $s=x^+(\lambda_{\max})$ as L4b's endpoint. Its query passes by the promise. On the interpolation with reserves $t s$, every queried minimum $P(t)$ satisfies $P(t)\le s$. A good active above $t s$ is among the active goods of $s$. Goods zero at $s$ are still pinned at zero, so folding them into outside retains at least the endpoint's outside offers. Raising other reserves can only delete more edges in a restricted query. The same Hall solver applies. L4b's determinant bound for the first budget crossing gives the exact reserve parameter. One original market flow and restoration of the outside mass recover an equilibrium.

## Search from the least compatible threshold

At a positive threshold the reciprocal market has buyer $j$ with mass $\lambda/\beta_j$, good $i$ with capacity $m_i$, and offers $(b_{ij}-U_i)/d_{ij}$ at reciprocal good prices $U_i$. Both floors and outsides are zero. Unused capacity and outside mass exchange roles when transposing the flow. Since every row mass and every capacity is positive, the two sets of quotes determine each other:

$$
 U_i=\max\{0,\max_j(b_{ij}-d_{ij}x_j)\},\qquad
 x_j=\max\{0,\max_i (b_{ij}-U_i)/d_{ij}\}.
$$

The greatest reciprocal price $U^+(\lambda)$ therefore corresponds to $x^-(\lambda)$. Compute it as the least reciprocal price with slightly decreased **reciprocal** capacities. This decreases the original buyer masses in the trial market used for the extremum computation. For $\lambda<\lambda_{\min}$, strong threshold order gives $x^-(\lambda)\ge x^-(\lambda_{\min})$ and hence $U^+(\lambda)\le U^+(\lambda_{\min})$. A failure of the restricted class test now certifies $\lambda\ge\lambda_{\min}$. Completed trials test $\sum_j x^-_j(\lambda)\le r$, which holds exactly for $\lambda\ge\lambda_{\min}$. At a budget-$r$ equilibrium attaining it, $\lambda_{\min}>0$ is a lower endpoint of the A1 Hall interval. Its denominator obeys the same cut bound.

After reconstructing $\lambda_{\min}$, query the greatest reciprocal price $s=U^+(\lambda_{\min})$. Use L4b's interpolation in the reciprocal market, with its original reciprocal capacities and reserves $t s$. Its least prices $P^{\rm tr}(t)$ clear the reciprocal market with zero reserves as well. Their positive coordinates lie in the promised active set. The inverse-offer formula gives continuous nonincreasing original coverages from $x^+(\lambda_{\min})$ to $x^-(\lambda_{\min})$. On each affine piece of the reciprocal reserve path and each region of maximizing inverse offers, each original coverage is an affine function of the reciprocal prices. At the first interior budget crossing, append the attaining inverse-offer equalities and the budget row to an independent system for the reciprocal prices and the reserve parameter. The incoming slope of the budget sum is nonzero. The augmented system is nonsingular and has polynomial-bit coefficients. This gives a uniform determinant bound for bisection and exact reconstruction. Then use one original fixed-price flow and the L4b conversion to game strategies.

Both algorithms have a fixed polynomial bound on their number of threshold and reserve queries. Each invokes at most the number of goods in its orientation plus one restricted solves and flow tests, at rational inputs of polynomial bit length. There is no need to choose the orientation using an unknown optimum. Run both with their stated bounds and accept only an exact original equilibrium verified at budget $r$. At least one succeeds under the disjunctive promise. ## The reciprocal promise can hold by itself

Use four types of equal prior $1/4$, four targets, and budget $r=3$. For the first three types and targets, use $d_{ij}=2^{ij}$ and $a_{ij}=d_{ij}$, with indices $i,j=0,1,2$ and unit defender gains. The fourth type has the private offer $1-x_s$ at the fourth target, of gain $1/8$. Take all off-block offers to be $-1-x_j$. The normalized outsides are zero.

For $\lambda<1/32$, aggregate capacity on the first three targets is $3\lambda<1/4$. Each of their three rows must use outside, which forces all three prices to one. The fourth row must also use outside because its capacity is $8\lambda<1/4$, forcing the fourth price to one. At $\lambda=1/32$ the fourth target can instead have zero coverage. Thus $\lambda_{\min}=1/32$, the least coverage there is $(1,1,1,0)$, and only the private type has positive utility. In the reciprocal market the active graph is a single edge.

For every $\lambda>1/32$, the fourth target has oversupply and price zero. Budget three then forces all three other prices to one, requiring total real capacity $3\lambda\le3/4$. At equality a perfect matching clears those columns, so $\lambda_{\max}=1/4$ and the greatest coverage is again $(1,1,1,0)$. Its active graph is $K_{3,3}$. Every two-by-two cycle violates factorization, since the alternate products of the slopes differ by $2^{(i-i')(j-j')}$. Deleting any one vertex leaves an unbalanced cycle. The single-block graph therefore fails the factorized-core/polynomial-factorized-branch recognition test, while the reciprocal endpoint passes it.

For the reverse separation, keep the same core slope matrix and give each core type mass $1/6$, every core offer intercept one, and every core target gain $1/8$. Give the isolated type mass $1/2$, its same offer $1-x_s$, and its target unit gain. Set $r=1$. The largest compatible threshold is $1/2$ with only the isolated target covered at its greatest price. For the smallest compatible threshold, the isolated target must have coverage one while $\lambda<1/2$. The budget then leaves all three core prices zero, requiring $3\cdot 8\lambda\ge1/2$ because all core rows prefer real purchases. Equality at $1/48$ clears by a matching. Every core row has positive utility there. Now the direct endpoint passes the structural test, while the reciprocal active graph contains the unbalanced $K_{3,3}$.

## Certified ties need a separate check as reserves move

At threshold $1/2$, take two unit-gain targets of capacity $1/2$ each. The three buyer masses are $3/8,3/8,1/4$, with offers

$$
 (1-8p_A,-5p_B),\qquad (-2p_A,2-5p_B),\qquad (1-p_A,1-p_B)
$$

and game outside payoffs $(-5,-2,0)$. Both $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ clear and are the two price extremes. At the lower point the first two rows demand $A,B$, respectively. At the upper point their only demanded real goods are $B,A$, respectively. The third row must send $1/8$ to each target in every clearing flow at either endpoint. Its tie is therefore physically forced at both endpoints.

With reserves $(1/2,1/2)$, both of the first two rows strictly prefer $B$ whenever $p_B<3/5$ and $p_A\ge1/2$. Together they overload $B$. At $(1/2,3/5)$ those rows tie, and the third strictly demands $A$. Assign its $1/4$ to $A$ and split the remaining $3/4$ over the residual capacities. This clears at the least permitted prices. The endpoint tie has disappeared.

For reserves $(t,t)$ with $1/3<t<1/2$, the interpolation's least clearing prices are $P(t)=(t,(8t-1)/5)$. If $p_A\ge t$ and $p_B<(8t-1)/5$, both large rows strictly prefer $B$ and overload it. At $P(t)$ the first row ties and the second demands $B$. Send $1/4$ of the first row to $A$ and $1/8$ to $B$, the whole second row to $B$, and the whole third row to $A$. The sum of the least prices reaches budget one at $t=6/13$, giving $(6/13,7/13)$ for that reserve query. Thus structural graph promises can persist through reserve queries, while a tie certified at an endpoint needs a separate basis for reuse. [Safe regrouping and restarts](A4h-hybrid-jumps.md)

A certificate can be uniform when its proof covers the query family. For example, in a threshold bracket $[a,b]$, start from certified price bounds $[L,U]$ covering all relevant zero-reserve clearings. Retain each buyer option that can be best somewhere in this box. In a flow relaxation give each good capacity $\lambda/\beta_j$ with a common variable $\lambda\in[a,b]$, and require full sales wherever $L_j>0$. If deleting an edge makes even this relaxation infeasible, that edge must carry positive flow at every covered clearing. Two such edges of a row give an affine price relation valid throughout the bracket. The same test applies at a fixed threshold to an interpolation using a box that contains the zero-reserve clearing prices along it. If a certified group is used, form the quotient for each full query before making any further restricted-market search. A tie in a full query need not remain forced after fixing a subset of its goods at reserve. ## Testing a supplied affine library of endpoint candidates

At a fixed threshold let $s$ be the greatest coverage and use the original capacities and reserves $ts$. Suppose a library supplies $N$ rational affine trial price functions across the goods. For example, propagate tight paths from either endpoint forest using the moving reserve or a fixed outside anchor. Include the moving reserves. Create an arrangement on $0\le t\le1$ containing every crossing between two candidates of one good, between a candidate and its reserve, and between two offers of the same buyer evaluated at any candidates for their respective goods. Include comparisons with outside. This takes $O(k(N+1)^2)$ rational events for $k$ buyers, with polynomial encoding length.

Within each open interval, the admissible sorted lists, the demand graph at every product of ranks, and the above-reserve statuses are constant as combinatorial data. With fixed tie conventions, A4g's Hall finisher makes the same rank changes and passes or rejects its exact minimum certificate throughout that interval. Run it once at a rational interior sample and once on each boundary point. A passing cell gives affine minimum prices for the entire cell. Intersect their summed price with the desired budget. This either returns an exact equilibrium or certifies that this library misses the required reserve-path point. It also checks completeness over the full reserve path without taking the product of the lists. A generally short complete library would contradict the B3f/L4b many-piece examples for the whole path. The test leaves room for a small library that works at a supplied budget. [Hall finisher](A4g-budgeted-support-search.md#searching-a-product-of-lists-without-enumerating-it) · [Path lower bound](L4-newton-and-continuation.md#limits-on-following-the-entire-path)

There is a separate test for a constant price library over the threshold search. For a trial vector $p$ on the grid of supplied prices and $a_j=1/\beta_j$, let

$$
 R(p)=\max_{\varnothing\ne S}\frac{f_p(S)}{\sum_{j\in S}a_j},\qquad
 H(p)=\min_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\{j:p_j>0\}}
              \frac{h_p(S)}{\sum_{j\in S}a_j}.
$$

Use $H(p)=+\infty$ if all prices are zero. Each finite bound is obtained by a scalar-capacity flow LP. At rational $\lambda>0$, buyer feasibility at the L4b decreased capacities holds exactly when $R(p)<\lambda$ by the quantity grid. Run the A4g rank search for its least feasible grid point, without its last global minimum test. If the result is $p$ at $\lambda_0$, it remains the least feasible grid point on $(R(p),\lambda_0]$, since those feasible sets nest as $\lambda$ decreases. If $R(p)>0$, rerun at $R(p)$. Some rank must strictly increase, or no strictly feasible grid point remains. The total number of different winners is at most one plus the total list length. On the plateau of $p$, its prices are the original greatest clearing prices exactly when $\lambda\le H(p)$, by strict upper Hall and ordinary lower Hall. This tests exactly where the library covers the upper threshold endpoints using polynomially many rational rank searches.
