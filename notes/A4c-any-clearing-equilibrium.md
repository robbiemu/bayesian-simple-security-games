# A4c. Any clearing equilibrium suffices for minimum prices

**Theorem.** In the binary rational fractional assignment market of A4, computing the componentwise minimum clearing prices reduces to computing *any* clearing prices by a single increase in supplies. Every clearing price vector of the enlarged market equals the minimum of the original market. Both exact search problems belong to \(\mathrm{PPAD}\cap\mathrm{PLS}=\mathrm{CLS}\). Consequently the exact Bayesian simple security game in L3 also belongs to CLS. A polynomial bit-time algorithm for either market search problem would give one for the game. The number of local-search steps in the proof below can depend on the number of transportation supports. [A4 market and minimum-price criterion](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#certifying-a-candidate-minimum-price) · [L3 game reduction](L3-prior-partials.md#conditional-exact-reduction-to-a-minimum-price-oracle) · [Fearnley, Goldberg, Hollender and Savani on CLS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01929)

Let buyer type \(i\) have mass \(m_i>0\), outside utility \(o_i\), and utility \(a_{ij}-d_{ij}p_j\) for good \(j\), with \(d_{ij}>0\). Good \(j\) has capacity \(c_j\ge0\) and reserve \(r_j\). All inputs are binary rationals. At prices \(p\ge r\), a buyer may split her mass among her utility maximizers, including outside when tied. Sales are at most capacity and must equal capacity above reserve. Zero-mass types may be omitted. We treat markets with at least one good and buyer, since empty sides clear at reserves.

## One call at enlarged capacities

Write \(D\) for the product of the denominators of all original masses and capacities and \(n\) for the number of goods. Keep the utilities and reserves and set

\[
      c'_j=c_j+\delta,\qquad \delta=1/(2nD).
\]

The bit length of \(D\) is at most one plus the sum of the denominator bit lengths. The new market has the original numbers of goods and types and polynomial encoding length.

**Feasibility at fixed prices.** For a set \(S\) of real goods, write \(f(S)\) for the mass of buyers all of whose demanded options belong to \(S\), and \(h(S)\) for the mass with at least one demanded option in \(S\). A buyer tied with outside never contributes to \(f(S)\), but may contribute to \(h(S)\). Write \(P=\{j:p_j>r_j\}\). At this price, a clearing flow exists for capacities \(c\) exactly when

\[
  f(S)\le c(S)\quad(S\subseteq [n]),\qquad
  c(S)\le h(S)\quad(S\subseteq P).                    \tag{1}
\]

For sufficiency, form a circulation with a vertex \(s\), arcs \(s\to i\) fixed at \(m_i\), and unrestricted nonnegative arcs from each buyer to her demanded options. A single outside vertex suffices since her edge to it records her individual outside option. Arcs from real goods to \(s\) have upper bound \(c_j\) and lower bound \(c_j\mathbf1_{j\in P}\). Outside has lower bound zero and upper bound \(\sum_i m_i\). In the circulation cut criterion, a vertex set omitting \(s\), with buyer side \(A\) and option side \(B\), requires \(m(A)\le u(B)\) when all options demanded by \(A\) lie in \(B\). If \(B\) includes outside, its capacity already covers all buyers. Otherwise this is the first family in (1). For a vertex set containing \(s\), denote the buyers and options in its *complement* by \(A,B\). Its finite outgoing cut requires every buyer demanding an option of \(B\) to belong to \(A\), and its inequality is \(\ell(B)\le m(A)\). The second family in (1) implies this since \(\ell(B)=c(B\cap P)\). The other cuts have an unrestricted outgoing edge. Necessity also follows by counting the mass forced into \(S\) and the mass available to fill it.

**Recovery and uniqueness.** Let \(p\) clear at \(c'\). Demand at this same price is independent of capacities. For every \(S\),
\[
 f(S)\le c'(S)\le c(S)+1/(2D).
\]
Both \(f(S)\) and \(c(S)\) are multiples of \(1/D\), giving \(f(S)\le c(S)\). For every nonempty \(S\subseteq P\), we have \(h(S)\ge c'(S)>c(S)\). Equation (1) therefore gives a clearing flow at \(p\) for the original market. Recover this flow by solving the original rational circulation, since the enlarged-market flow can exceed an original capacity.

To prove minimality directly, suppose another original clearing vector \(z\) lowers some coordinate of \(p\). The nonempty set \(S=\{j:z_j<p_j\}\) lies in \(P\). Every buyer counted by \(h(S)\) strictly improves on her old maximum on at least one good of \(S\). Every option outside \(S\) is weakly worse than before. All these buyers must therefore buy inside \(S\), contradicting \(h(S)>c(S)\). Thus \(p\le z\) for every original clearing vector. Existence for enlarged rational capacities follows independently from either construction below. This proves existence of the original componentwise minimum and uniqueness of the enlarged market's price vector, even if its clearing flow is nonunique. In the reverse direction, an original minimum price is itself an answer to the any-price search problem.

## A piecewise-linear fixed point for any clearing

Choose a price ceiling \(U_j=1+\max\{r_j,\max_i(a_{ij}-o_i)/d_{ij}\}\). At that ceiling every buyer strictly prefers outside to good \(j\). Use the box \([r,U]\) for prices and \([0,m_i]^{n+1}\) for each buyer's row \(x_i\), including an outside coordinate. Let \(w_i(p)\) list her utilities for these options. Use the following simultaneous map, where \(\Pi_i\) projects in Euclidean distance onto the simplex \(\{z\ge0:\sum_kz_k=m_i\}\).
\[
 x'_i=\Pi_i(x_i+w_i(p)),\qquad
 p'_j=\min\{U_j,\max\{r_j,p_j+\textstyle\sum_i x_{ij}-c_j\}\}.
\]
It is a continuous self-map. At a fixed point the row projection gives \(w_i(p)\cdot(y-x_i)\le0\) for every row \(y\) in the mass simplex. This is exactly allocation to demanded options. A good at its ceiling receives zero mass. If it has positive capacity, the update strictly lowers its price. If its capacity is zero, its sales already equal capacity. At every other price the update imposes the reserve and market-clearing conditions. Conversely, any clearing allocation and prices within the box satisfy these fixed-point equations.

For a row input \(b\), sort its \(n+1\) coordinates into \(b_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge b_{(n+1)}\) by min/max comparators. Set
\[
 \theta=\max_{1\le k\le n+1}\frac{\sum_{h\le k}b_{(h)}-m_i}{k},
 \qquad \Pi_i(b)_j=\max\{0,b_j-\theta\}.
\]
Indeed, at the threshold satisfying \(\sum_j(b_j-\theta)_+=m_i\), every prefix gives a lower bound on \(\theta\) and the prefix of positive coordinates attains equality. Sorting and all other gates require polynomial size, allowing multiplication only by rational constants. Affine coordinate rescaling gives a cube circuit. Exact fixed-point search for such circuits is in PPAD and yields a polynomial-bit rational solution. [Etessami and Yannakakis, linear FIXP characterization](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/080720826)

## A polynomial local search on fractional transportation supports

The following adapts the maximum-log-slope matching and price-LP descent for unit rent division to binary fractional row and column totals. A dummy good with an adjustable price implements all outside options during the descent. One dummy buyer guarantees that this good reaches price zero whenever any real price reaches reserve. [Arunachaleswaran, Barman and Rathi, Lemma 2 and §8.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.04163#page=8)

**A balanced market with an initial assignment.** Shift prices to \(q_j=p_j-r_j\) and intercepts to \(b_{ij}=a_{ij}-d_{ij}r_j\). Let \(M=\sum_i m_i\), \(C=\sum_j c_j\), and \(e=\tfrac12\min_{ij}d_{ij}>0\). Add a dummy buyer of mass \(C+1\) and a dummy good \(0\) of capacity \(M+1\). Require all rows and columns to be filled. Real buyers value the dummy good at \(o_i-e q_0\). The dummy buyer values every good, including good zero, at minus its \(q\)-price. All slopes are strictly negative. A full transportation flow must put at least one unit from the dummy buyer into good zero. Thus at any supporting nonnegative prices, \(q_0\le q_j\) for every real good.

Set all prices equal to
\[
 T=1+\max\left\{0,\max_{ij}\frac{b_{ij}-o_i}{d_{ij}-e}\right\}.
\]
Every real buyer strictly prefers the dummy good, and the dummy buyer is indifferent among all goods. Send all real mass to good zero, and from the dummy buyer send \(c_j\) to each real good and one to good zero. This initial pair has polynomial bit size. At any supported full transportation flow with \(q_0=0\), real buyers using the dummy good take their original outside utility, and dummy flow to real goods can occur only at zero excess price. Dropping the dummy buyer and interpreting good zero as outside gives exact original clearance.

**States and potential.** A state is an edge set \(E\) of the augmented bipartite graph. Test by flow feasibility that all row and column totals can be met using \(E\). Form the price polyhedron \(K(E)\) of all \(q\ge0\) at which every edge in \(E\) is a utility maximizer in its row, using pairwise affine inequalities. Require \(K(E)\ne\varnothing\). Both tests run in polynomial bit time. Choose \(q(E)\) to minimize \(\sum_j q_j\) on \(K(E)\), breaking ties lexicographically among minimizers. The optimum face is bounded by nonnegativity, and lexicographic minimization gives a vertex. The support of the initial flow above is a valid initial state.

Write \(s(E)=\sum_j q_j(E)\). With \(k=n+1\) price variables, clear the denominators in every possible comparison row and the nonnegativity rows. Let \(H\ge1\) bound their integer coefficients and right sides. There are polynomially many possible rows, including all pairwise comparisons, so \(\log H\) is polynomial in the input. By Cramer's rule at a vertex, all coordinates have a common denominator at most \(Q=k!H^k\) and their numerators have absolute value at most \(Q\). Thus \(s(E)\le kQ\), and distinct values are separated by at least \(1/Q^2\). The integer \(\lfloor Q^2s(E)\rfloor\) is an order-preserving potential of polynomial bit length to be minimized.

**An improving neighbor.** If \(q_0(E)=0\), decode an original clearing flow. Otherwise every price is strictly positive. On the full demand graph at \(q(E)\), find a full transportation flow \(y\) maximizing
\[
                    \sum_{ij} y_{ij}\log h_{ij},
\]
where \(h_{ij}>0\) is the slope magnitude of that edge, including dummy edges. The graph supports a full transportation flow because it contains \(E\). Return \(E'=\{ij:y_{ij}>0\}\). Optimal transportation dual variables \(\alpha_i,\beta_j\) obey \(\alpha_i+\beta_j\ge\log h_{ij}\) on the demand graph and equality on \(E'\). Hence \(v_j=\exp(-\beta_j)>0\) makes all positive-flow choices maximize \(h_{ij}v_j\) among the old utility-maximizing goods in their row. For sufficiently small \(t>0\), all prices \(q_j(E)-t v_j\) remain nonnegative, and every old strict utility gap persists by continuity. All edges of \(E'\) are still demanded there. Therefore
\[
               s(E')\le s(E)-t\sum_jv_j<s(E).
\]
This proves improvement from *every* valid nonterminal state. The algorithm only computes the support \(E'\) and the two rational LP optima. The real vector \(v\) and the step \(t\) serve as existence witnesses.

**Rational arithmetic for the log objective.** Let \(N\) be the number of augmented row and column vertices and bound every numerator and denominator of \(h_{ij}\) by \(2^B\), with \(B\ge1\). On a simple residual cycle, the exact signed sum of log costs is \(\log(A/B')\), where \(A,B'\) are positive integers at most \(2^{BN}\). Whenever \(A>B'\), \(\log(A/B')\ge(A-B')/A\ge2^{-BN}\). Reversing handles negative costs. Approximate each log to absolute error below \(2^{-BN}/(4N)\). This preserves the sign of every *nonzero* simple-cycle cost. Such rational approximations use polynomial bit time, for example by range reduction to \([1,2]\) and the series \(\log z=2\sum_{h\ge0}((z-1)/(z+1))^{2h+1}/(2h+1)\), whose ratio parameter has magnitude at most \(1/3\). Approximate \(\log 2\) with enough additional bits for its integer multiplier in the range reduction.

Solve the rational-cost transportation LP and take the lexicographically first optimal vertex in fixed edge order. This uses polynomially many rational LPs. After scaling the margins to integers with a common denominator, transportation vertices are integral, and every entry is at most the scaled total mass. Consequently their bit lengths are polynomial even when their numerical values are large. Its residual graph has no positive approximated-cost cycle. If it were suboptimal for the exact logs, the difference to a better full transportation would be a circulation in its residual graph. Cycle decomposition would give a simple exact-positive cycle, which is still positive with the approximations. Thus the vertex has the required exact optimality. Zero-cost cycles under the true objective may be broken either way. The precision bound depends on the slope descriptions and the number of vertices, including when a mass has exponentially large numerical value.

The finite support space, initial state, polynomial-bit potential and polynomial improving-neighbor routine establish PLS membership. This also supplies a deterministic finite exact algorithm. Its visited supports after initialization are forests since an optimal vertex of the transportation polytope can be chosen. With \(b\) buyer types and \(n\) goods there are at most \(\sum_{h=0}^{b+n+1}\binom{(b+1)(n+1)}{h}\) possible forest supports, counting many invalid ones. The strict potential prevents revisits. Combining with the piecewise-linear fixed point and the single-call reduction puts both market problems in CLS.

**The unique minimum-price witness.** Encode a price vector by reduced fractions with fixed coordinate order and canonical signed-integer encodings. A polynomial-time verifier can recompute the enlarged capacities and check clearance at the supplied prices by rational circulation. Exactly one vector is accepted. It has polynomial bit length by the exact fixed-point construction, or by applying the vertex bound for the balanced construction to the enlarged market and adding the reserves. If requested as part of the answer, a canonical original clearing flow is available by lexicographic minimization over its bounded rational circulation polytope. This gives a TFUP formulation of minimum-price computation. A4d gives a separate UniqueEOPL reduction. [P-matrix complementarity](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md)

## Transfer to the exact game

For strictly positive gains and losses, L3 brackets the prescribed coverage sum by minimum-price queries at different supplies and then tunes the reserves between the bracket endpoints. Each rational query has polynomial bit length. The support and threshold-error determinant in L3 fixes a tolerance with polynomially many binary digits, so the two bisections use polynomially many queries. Replace each query by the single enlargement above, compute its prices, and recover a feasible flow with the *pre-enlargement* capacities of that query. In particular the stored lower-supply allocation and any flow used to bound unused supply must refer to their original query capacities. This reproduces the exact minimum-price answers L3 requires. The final support LP is on the original game and returns an exact equilibrium. For weak signs, A1 first supplies a polynomial-bit strict perturbation and then repairs its equilibrium support on the unperturbed data. Zero-prior types can be assigned exact best responses afterwards. These rational LP and flow operations, and the optional decomposition into pure defender strategies, take polynomial bit time. [L3, four-step reduction](L3-prior-partials.md#conditional-exact-reduction-to-a-minimum-price-oracle) · [A1, weak-sign repair](A1-threshold-transport.md)

This is an adaptive polynomial-time Turing reduction to an exact CLS problem. The game search relation admits polynomial-bit rational strategies and a polynomial verifier for feasibility and best response. Composing the oracle calls with the PPAD reduction and, separately, with the PLS reduction gives two Turing reductions to the respective complete problems. Buss and Johnson's Corollary 6.2 converts each to many-one membership. The equality of PPAD ∩ PLS with CLS gives the exact game the same upper bound. [Buss and Johnson, Corollary 6.2](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/NPSearch/NPSearch.pdf#page=32) · [Fearnley, Goldberg, Hollender and Savani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01929)

There is also a unique-witness refinement of game search. Fix deterministic tie-breaking for the flow and LP routines and all choices in the reduction. A witness can contain the ordered transcript of canonically encoded minimum-price answers. Verify it by replaying the polynomial-time reduction, checking each answer by one circulation in its enlarged market and requiring the transcript to end at the halt. Induction on the queries shows there is exactly one accepted transcript, and it decodes one exact game equilibrium. The game itself can have several equilibria.

## What the published algorithms imply

- Balkanski, En and Faenza's July 2026 manuscript states a polynomial algorithm for explicitly listed buyers and unit-supply items with imperfectly transferable utility. In Algorithm 3, line 6 tests whether a buyer gains an item outside that buyer's old demand set. Such an item can already be in the alternating tree. Lemma 34 and its proof instead count a new item entering the tree at each step within a linear domain, and the proof describes the event as an edge to a good outside the tree. Oki and Sato's footnote 13 and Appendix F identify internal demand changes in precisely this setting. For instance, their Example F.1 has three unit buyers and two unit-supply goods with affine utilities. At equal prices increasing from zero, both goods already occur in the tree rooted at buyer 3 when buyers 1 and 2 hold goods 1 and 2. Buyer 2 acquires a tie to good 1 at prices \((1,1)\). A new edge has appeared with the same two goods and no payment breakpoint. This defeats the item-growth inference. The exact polynomial bound in that proof consequently cannot be applied here. A4 separately proves a worst-case lower bound for two Oki–Sato auctions. [Balkanski, En and Faenza v3, Algorithm 3 and Lemma 34](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13620v3#page=15) · [Their proof in Appendix E.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13620v3#page=35) · [Oki and Sato, footnote 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10563v3#page=22) · [Oki and Sato, Example F.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10563v3#page=38)
- An algorithm for explicit unit buyers and integral copies must also address representation size. Scaling by a common denominator preserves the feasible fractional flows up to scale. Integrality of bipartite transportation then permits grouping or ungrouping unit agents at fixed prices. The number of agents after scaling can be exponential in the original binary encoding, even for a single buyer type of integral mass \(2^L\). With unit masses and polynomially bounded aggregate copy counts, expansion is polynomial. Clearing the supply perturbation starting with integral masses and capacities requires scaling by only \(2n\).
- Galichon and Jacquet's Theorem 1 maps positive-surplus linearly transferable matching to a structured nonzero-sum hide-and-seek game. Shifting buyer utilities by \(o_i\) and prices by \(r_j\) gives inequalities \(u_i+d_{ij}q_j\ge a_{ij}-d_{ij}r_j-o_i\), with \(u_i,q_j\ge0\) and equality on used pairs. In a generalized-flow LP an arc gain in the dual inequality also rescales the flow in the primal balance equations. Here one unit of buyer mass consumes exactly one unit of capacity for every slope. This prevents directly using the usual generalized-flow constraints with \(d_{ij}\) as arc gains. The bimatrix correspondence, the generalized-flow algorithm and the auction bounds cited above leave exact polynomial bit time for arbitrary slope matrices and binary masses unresolved. [Galichon and Jacquet, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.12200#page=3) · [Végh on generalized flows](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.6809)

### A4's path bound for the stated partial-stability invariant

There is also a global test of the item-growth claim. Use A4's strict affine unit-demand family, with each capacity-two good replaced by two identical, explicitly listed, unit-supply items. Copies of a good have the same singleton value and payment slope for every buyer. The valuation of a nonempty bundle is its greatest singleton value. Consider any continuous piecewise-affine price path from zero to a competitive equilibrium in the nonnegative orthant along which every price has a **feasible partially stable** matching. In the terminology of Balkanski, En and Faenza, unmatched items have price zero and each buyer's bundle weakly dominates every bundle of its size or smaller. These are the intermediate invariants asserted for their price steps. [Balkanski, En and Faenza, Definitions 7 and 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13620v3#page=5) · [Their Lemma 34](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13620v3#page=15)

At each price, the two copies of each good have the same price. Otherwise a higher-priced copy must be sold. If its holder owns the cheaper copy too, dropping the expensive one preserves her maximal singleton value and saves its positive payment. If she does not, swapping copies preserves her valuation and strictly lowers her payment. Both contradict partial stability. Write \(p_j\) for the resulting common price.

In a bundle supporting a positively priced item, dropping it must lower the bundle's value. At most one item can be essential to the maximum. Thus there is only one paid item in this bundle, and its singleton has exactly the same utility as the bundle. Partial stability compares the bundle to every singleton and to the empty bundle, so the paid singleton is a best option. Consequently, every pair of copies of an above-reserve good sells to two distinct buyers who demand its singleton. A buyer is counted for at most one such sale. For a set \(J\) of positive-price goods, let \(I(J,p)\) be the original types demanding at least one good in \(J\) as a best singleton. There are two buyers of each original type and one extra buyer in A4, giving exactly the needed bound
\[
                      2|J|\le 2|I(J,p)|+1.
\]
A4's price-spread argument now applies for budgets \(b=\sum_jp_j\in[1/4,1]\). Throughout that range every projected price is positive, and the singleton demand graph of the original types has a perfect matching by Hall's theorem. The terminal equilibrium has \(b>1\), since at \(b\le1\) every buyer strictly prefers some singleton to outside, and there are \(2n+1\) buyers but only \(2n\) items. Continuity therefore forces the path through A4's \(n^{\Omega(\log n)}\) budget intervals, which have pairwise different unique matchings of the original types. A single affine segment in copy prices projects to an affine segment of common prices and has \(O(n^3)\) singleton-comparison breakpoints. The witness-interval counting argument in A4 requires \(n^{\Omega(\log n)}\) segments. Replacing each aggregate good by two unit items costs only a constant factor in the dense singleton data. [A4, forcing and segment count](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#why-the-auction-must-pass-through-the-forced-assignments)

In particular, a version that maintains these invariants on every affine update and returns a clearing equilibrium needs superpolynomially many updates on this family. This identifies the conflict between the simultaneous correctness and iteration guarantees claimed in the July 2026 manuscript. It leaves open algorithms that use other paths or other invariants.
