# A4h7 Certifying and eliminating forced price relations

A market flow certificate can show that an edge must be used at the unknown minimum. The strict Hall test can also show that an edge must be demanded even if it never carries flow. Certified ties can reduce the number of price variables while preserving the original physical capacities at recovery. The certificates give polynomial algorithms for some supplied markets and can stall on small instances. Végh's generalized-flow contraction identifies tight edges of an optimization dual. Market capacities instead use unweighted sales on edges selected by buyer-specific slopes. [Végh, generalized-flow contraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.6809) · [A4e capacity mismatch](A4e-covering-lp.md#the-weighted-dual-can-miss-clearance)

## Capacity certificates at the unknown minimum

Normalize reserves and outside utilities to zero. Drop edges with nonpositive intercepts since their use can be replaced by outside at reserve. Suppose a rational box contains \(p^*\). It can start at reserves on the lower side and offer ceilings on the upper side. Any buyer-feasible vector also gives an upper bound by price domination. To include a good in buyer \(i\)'s **possible demand** list, lower that good to its box lower endpoint and raise the other goods to their upper endpoints. It is possible precisely if it is best there. Outside is possible if it is best at the upper endpoint. For positive-price goods it is also safe to use the larger set of all viable buyers.

Keep all possible edges in a transportation relaxation, with the actual row masses and physical column caps. Require full sales on goods already certified above reserve. If forbidding edge \(ij\) makes this circulation infeasible, \(ij\) has positive flow in any original allocation at \(p^*\). Two forced edges in the same row give the required equality of their offers. Each test is one flow. Certified equalities can be added to the covering polyhedron, and LP feasibility with a single tight-edge equality can then refine the possible graph. A failed test excluding a set of edges gives a disjunction. [Hall feasibility at given prices](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#one-call-at-enlarged-capacities)

One way to certify positivity uses the same relaxation with a coordinate fixed at reserve. If this relaxed flow fails, the coordinate cannot be at reserve at \(p^*\). To obtain an upper vector, for every good set its price to the smallest value at which the total mass strictly preferring it to outside is at most its capacity. This vector is jointly buyer-feasible. Buyers with positive utility may choose best goods independently since each good has capacity for all buyers who strictly prefer it to outside. Buyers at outside utility can take outside.

An unconditional test for real purchases uses strict minimum Hall. For nonempty \(J\) of goods on which buyer \(i\) has viable offers at reserve, let \(N(J)\) contain all buyers with a viable offer on \(J\). If \(c(J)\ge m(N(J))\), at least one good in \(J\) is at reserve, so \(i\) strictly prefers real goods to outside. Such a set can be found by maximum closure, maximizing \(c(J)-m(N(J))\) with a specified good required in \(J\). Checking the full viable set of a buyer is often enough.

For a stronger tight-edge certificate let \(E_i\) be the possible-demand goods of buyer \(i\) and \(P\) a certified positive-price set. Suppose \(S\subseteq P\) satisfies \(E_i\cap S=\{j\}\) and

\[
 c(S)\ge m\{k\ne i:E_k\cap S\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

Strict Hall at the least price forces \(i\) to demand a good in \(S\), and her only possible one is \(j\). Check this by maximum closure on \(P\setminus(E_i\setminus\{j\})\), requiring \(j\). Give goods rewards \(c_j\), other buyers costs \(m_k\), and direct an infinite-capacity arc from each good to each possible other buyer. One minimum cut finds the maximum, with vertex and coefficient counts polynomial in the input. This certificate can find a tie even when the affected buyer spends all her mass outside. For example, take two unit goods with one private unit buyer bidding 10 on each, and one extra unit buyer bidding 2 on both, all with unit slopes. The minimum is \((2,2)\). The private buyers exhaust capacity and the extra buyer always takes outside in the original clearing flow. Strict Hall for either singleton forces the extra buyer to demand it. [Strict minimum-price certificate](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#certifying-a-candidate-minimum-price)

### Pivotal buyers give clipped price formulas

If all possible buyers for good \(j\), other than a designated buyer \(i\), have total mass at most \(c_j\), strict Hall for the singleton gives

\[
 p_j^*=\max\{0,(b_{ij}-u_i^*)/d_{ij}\}.
\]

At a positive price \(i\) must be tight, and at zero the formula follows from the cover. Goods with total possible buyer mass at most \(c_j\) stay at reserve. If the remaining prices are covered by formulas for a fixed number of designated buyers and a fixed number of free goods, use their utilities and those free prices as variables. Enumerate the faces of the arrangement of formula breakpoints, reserve tests and all physical offer comparisons on each affine piece. Fixed dimension gives polynomially many candidates of polynomial bit length, including faces of all dimensions. On the face of the exact solution the demand graph and reserve statuses are constant, so the clearance and strict Hall certificate accept the representative and identify the original \(p^*\). One can find the designated set in polynomial time by enumerating the bounded number of buyers and free goods. [A4g anchored price candidates](A4g-budgeted-support-search.md#candidate-prices-from-tight-paths)

## Exact contraction after certification

A certified tie yields \(p_k=A+B p_j\) with \(B>0\). Propagate along a spanning forest of certified relations. Each resulting group has either a pinned price or coordinates \(p_j=A_j+B_j z_g\) on one free variable. A cycle with nonunit composition pins the whole group. The original data and simple paths bound all coefficients by polynomial bits.

For a free group \(g\), set \(R_g=\max_{j\in g}(-A_j/B_j)\), \(C_g=\sum_{j\in g}c_j\), and use the strictly decreasing envelope

\[
 V_{ig}(z)=\max_{j\in g}\bigl(b_{ij}-d_{ij}(A_j+B_j z)\bigr).
\]

Fold pinned offers into the buyer's outside option. An original clearing allocation at \(p^*\) projects to a grouped one at \(z^*\). If \(z_g^*>R_g\), every physical member of \(g\) sells out. The buyers who demand any positive-price group are precisely those demanding some physical good in its union. The original strict Hall inequalities on such unions hold for the group problem. Therefore \(z^*\) is the componentwise least grouped clearing vector, by price domination. Recover the physical prices and run one rational clearing flow at the original capacities. An arbitrary grouped allocation can send too much to one physical alias. The recovery argument uses the grouped **minimum** and the original flow test.

With a fixed number of free groups, directly enumerate the arrangement of the original comparisons in those variables and use the physical flow and minimum tests. With arbitrarily many groups, the grouped problem remains polynomial if their interaction graph is a forest. The A4b recursion extends to explicit decreasing piecewise-affine offers. For a buyer on an edge \(g,h\), replace its comparison line by the increasing piecewise-affine function \(V_{ig}^{-1}(V_{ih}(t))\). Its piece count is at most the sum of the two envelopes' piece counts, by strict monotonicity. Partition the parent line at the comparison functions' breakpoints, crossings and reserve and outside events. The strictly parent-preferring buyers in each cell are a prefix in the comparison order. Thus the number of possible prefix masses is polynomial, while a buyer with positive utility can drop out of that set of positive-utility buyers at most once as both prices rise. A4b's additive bound on child drops and its sweep apply. Each retained breakpoint is an original affine comparison or an inverse image across one known segment of a child breakpoint at each level, giving additive bit growth. With strictly decreasing continuous piecewise-affine offers, the conditional existence and least-price arguments still follow from the price fixed point and strict Hall. [Forest recursion](A4b-direct-hall-search.md#polynomial-time-on-arbitrary-forests) · [Fixed-point and Hall recovery](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#one-call-at-enlarged-capacities)

For an example beyond factorization, give goods \(a,b,s,t\) capacities \((11/2,11/2,2,2)\). A guard of mass 10 has offers \(10-p_a,10-p_b,1-p_s\). A second buyer of mass 2 has offers \(9-3p_a,7-p_b,4-p_t\). The first buyer must buy real goods by the reserve-set test on \(\{a,b,s\}\), whose capacity is 13 against total possible mass 12. The capacities other than \(a\), or other than \(b\), available to her sum to \(15/2<10\). Thus she must buy both, and \(p_a=p_b=z\). The second buyer's group offer \(\max(9-3z,7-z)\) switches at \(z=1\). The physical slope cycle is unbalanced and the grouped goods graph is a tree. The exact minimum is \((3,3,0,0)\), supported by guard purchases \(11/2,9/2\) on \(a,b\) and one unit each on \(b,t\) from the second buyer.

## Where tie discovery stalls

Take two unit buyers and two unit goods. A has bids \((4,5)\) and slopes \((1,1)\). B has bids \((4,7)\) and slopes \((1,2)\). The minimum is \((0,1)\), with A tied and B strictly preferring good 2. The lower bound \(p_2\ge1\) follows from the capacity of good 2. At the buyer-feasible upper vector \((1,2)\) both buyers tie both goods. In the resulting box both buyers must buy real goods, but either perfect matching is feasible in the possible-edge relaxation. No original positive trade is forced by that relaxation. With price \((1/2,8/5)\) both buyers are strict and the market clears, so A's equality is specific to the minimum.

Strict minimum Hall on positive good 2 requires both buyers to demand it. Good 1 needs a buyer, so at least one must tie. In \(h\) copies, an explicit product of the alternatives “A ties” and “B ties” gives \(2^h\) branches that all admit an ordinary clearing flow. For the B alternative use \((0,3/2)\) and the same assignment. Only the all-A branch contains the minimum, and the other branches fail its strict certificate. These markets may be connected by adding cross-copy offers \(7/2-p_j\), which are strictly worse at every displayed witness. This bounds a joint list made by enumerating the local disjunctions. A selector exploiting the original capacities and the minimum criterion could bypass that product. A general polynomial-time tie discovery procedure remains open.
