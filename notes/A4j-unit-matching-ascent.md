# Bound unit-market ascent by deletion matchings

An exact matching-based ascent solves any supplied unit market. Its running time is polynomial if deleting \(O(\log L)\) viable offers makes the slope matrix factorable, even when the deletion set is unknown. It is also polynomial when every reached buyer–good graph has logarithmic cycle rank, including either degree-two promise. The proof gives input-polynomial endpoint bit lengths even on dense instances with many updates. [Reference solver](../experiments/a4j_unit_prices.py) · [Direct conditional solver](A4j-unit-market-search.md)

## Compute a safe direction from a fixed-margin assignment

Normalize reserves and outsides to zero. Insert buyers one at a time and start each insertion at the previous market's least prices, with its clearing matching. These prices are lower bounds on the new minimum by buyer-feasible domination after dropping the added row from any new-market clearing. The new buyer is a free root. Try ordinary alternating augmentation on demanded edges, traversing an edge from a buyer to a best option and going from a matched real good to its owner. Reaching outside or a free good permits an assignment of the root, while all previously sold positive-price goods stay filled.

If augmentation fails, write \(R,S\) for the reached buyers and goods. Each good in \(S\) is matched, and every buyer in \(R\) other than the root is such a good's owner. Thus \(|S|=s\), \(|R|=s+1\), and all these buyers demand exclusively in \(S\), with positive utilities. The reachability paths give a full matching of \(S\) leaving any specified \(r\in R\) free, by flipping the path from the current root to \(r\). For each \(r\) find such a matching \(M_r\) of minimum product of slopes. Average the \(s+1\) resulting matchings. Their average solves the transportation problem on the current tight edges with row total \(s/(s+1)\), column total one, and costs \(\log d_{ij}\). To see optimality, every flow with these margins can be extended by a dummy column of \(1/(s+1)\) in every row. The resulting doubly stochastic matrix is a convex combination of matchings, with total weight \(1/(s+1)\) on those leaving each real row free.

The positive support of the average is connected. Every support component balances some number \(a\) of rows of mass \(s/(s+1)\) against an integer number of goods. Coprimality forces \(a=s+1\). If \(\alpha_i+\beta_j\le\log d_{ij}\) are transportation dual constraints, complementary slackness gives rates \(v_j=\exp(-\beta_j)>0\) for all \(j\in S\) and \(w_i=\min_{j\in D_i} d_{ij}v_j>0\) for all \(i\in R\). Every edge in every \(M_r\) attains this minimum in its row. Fix any one good's speed at one. Connected support pins all ratios, so \(v,w\) are computed by rational multiplication and division on a support tree. Speeds outside \(S\) are zero.

Rematch \(S\) using \(M_{\mathrm{root}}\), keeping the same root free. The selected edges stay tight when prices rise at rates \(v\) and these buyers' utilities fall at rates \(w\). Buyers outside \(R\) have a static assigned best option. Advance to the first initially inferior offer or outside option that catches up. An old tied edge of higher rate becomes worse throughout the step. The time is the minimum positive slack divided by positive catch-up rate, and is finite because every reached buyer eventually meets outside.

The prices never overshoot the new minimum. Otherwise take the first time before the next event when some coordinates \(J\subseteq S\) reach their minimum clearing prices. The persistent support touches \(J\) with at least \(|J|+1\) buyers since every row of its average contributes at most \(s/(s+1)\) and \(J\) uses \(|J|\). Each of those buyers demands a good in \(J\). At the minimum, her other choices inside \(S\) become dearer and the alternatives outside \(S\) were strictly worse at this time. She must buy in \(J\) at the minimum, contradicting stock. If the first contact is at the event, continuity gives the required weak bound at that event.

The old reached sets persist at the endpoint when augmentation still fails. If a nonempty part \(J\subseteq S\) ceased to be reached, only its \(|J|\) owners could touch it in the persistent support. The same average gives a contradiction. Therefore each event either augments, expands the reached sets, or is internal. After a successful augmentation the prices clear and are bounded by the current minimum, so they equal it. Continue to the final buyer.

## Matching values bound the number of steps

During one phase with fixed \(S,R\), let \(\mu_r\) be the optimal logarithmic matching cost when \(r\) is left free. All old chosen matchings survive each step, so each \(\mu_r\) weakly decreases. An internal event produces a new tight edge with \(d_{ij}v_j<w_i\). It violates the old normalized dual. If the transportation optimum stayed equal, its new dual would still satisfy equality on the old connected support, hence coincide after normalization. This is impossible. The optimum is the average of the \(\mu_r\), so at least one strictly drops. Thus the number of internal events is at most the total number of different matching values for the separate free rows.

For local cycle rank \(c\), any two matchings leaving the same row free differ by an element of the binary cycle space of the viable induced graph of that phase. At most \(2^c\) distinct matchings are possible. When each reached buyer has at most two viable goods, this graph has at most \(2(s+1)\) edges and \(2s+1\) vertices and is connected, so \(c\le2\). When each good has at most two viable buyers, it has at most \(2s\) edges and \(c=0\). More generally, suppose that deleting \(b\) viable offers from the full instance makes every remaining slope \(\alpha_i\beta_j\) for positive row and column factors. For fixed \(R,S,r\) every matching product has the common factor \(\prod_{i\in R\setminus\{r\}}\alpha_i\prod_{j\in S}\beta_j\) times the product of its exceptional offers' slope ratios. There are at most \(2^b\) values. The algorithm does not need the factors or exceptional offers. There are at most \(n+1\) reached-set phases per buyer, so the total updates and time are \(2^b\operatorname{poly}(L)\), with the same bound using a maximum local cycle rank instead of \(b\). The general finite bound follows by counting all matchings in a phase. [Earlier safe ascent and count limits](A4h-alternating-tree.md)

To compute a minimum-product matching exactly, use successive shortest augmenting paths for costs \(\log d\). A forward edge has multiplicative label \(d\) and a reverse edge label \(1/d\). Current size-optimality rules out a residual cycle of product below one. Layered Bellman–Ford with exact rational multiplication and comparison chooses a shortest augmenting path, breaking ties in favor of fewer edges to obtain a simple path. This uses polynomially many operations on polynomial-bit products. The standard alternating decomposition of a larger matching against the current one gives optimality of successive augmentations.

Every internal update has an input-only bit bound. One spanning tree of the persistent support supplies the tight equations in \(p_S,u_R\) with one free scalar, and the catching internal edge fixes it since its derivative is nonzero. If the event ties an unchanged good or outside, the same tree is anchored instead at one stationary quote or zero utility. Each updated coordinate is then a rational affine function of that one anchor with coefficients of polynomial input size. A step that needs such a stationary anchor expands reachability or ends the insertion. There are only polynomially many of those steps, so repeated internal updates cause no bit growth. This gives polynomial bit time per update on unrestricted instances as well.

## A direct LP formulation with few exceptional offers

If a deletion set \(K\) is available, one can avoid ascent. Enumerate matching subsets \(A\subseteq K\), interpreted as the exceptional offers used by an integral equilibrium. Remove the endpoints of \(A\) and retain only factorable offers on the remaining vertices. Let \(W_A\) be the ordinary assignment optimum on bids \(b_{ij}/\alpha_i\) in that residual market. In the full cover LP, impose equality on the edges of \(A\), the original nonnegative floors, and the additional equality

\[
 \sum_{i\text{ remaining}} u_i/\alpha_i + \sum_{j\text{ remaining}}\beta_j p_j = W_A.
\]

This certifies complementary slackness of a residual optimal integral matching with the proposed prices and utilities. Combining it with \(A\) gives original clearance. Conversely, every clearing has an integral supporting matching, whose exceptional part gives one of these feasible LPs. Minimize price sum over the at most \(2^{|K|}\) faces to return the original componentwise minimum.

The deletion set can be found by Group Feedback Vertex Set on the graph with one vertex per offer. Give each offer \(e=(i,j)\) a group variable \(z_e\) representing \(1/\alpha_i\). Two offers sharing a buyer require equal variables. Two offers \(e,f\) sharing a good require \(z_f=z_ed_e/d_f\). These equations are consistent exactly when the remaining offers factor. For bounded-bit group arithmetic, let \(P\) be the product of all slope numerators and denominators and work in the multiplicative units modulo \(1+P\). All slope numerators and denominators are invertible there. The alternating numerator and denominator of each simple original incidence cycle are at most \(P\), so they agree modulo \(1+P\) exactly when they agree over the rationals. Consistency after any deletions in the group instance therefore implies rational factorization, recoverable along a forest. The oracle-access Group Feedback Vertex Set algorithm finds a smallest set of size \(b\) in \(4^b\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bit time, using iterative budget and witness recovery if needed. [Iwata, Wahlström and Yoshida, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.2841#page=16)

## Exact replay

Run `python3 experiments/a4j_unit_prices.py --self-test 2000`. It compares the exact augmenting-path matching subroutine with enumeration on small instances and checks each returned matching and every strict Hall subset. The run made 4,727 updates, of which 283 were internal. The two-good Dütting example returns \((2/5,7/10)\) after two updates, one internal. [Reference solver](../experiments/a4j_unit_prices.py) · [Dütting example and internal events](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10563v3#page=38)

For one supplied market, run `python3 experiments/a4j_unit_prices.py < market.json`. The input has `bids` and `slopes` matrices and optional `reserves` and `outside_utilities` vectors. Write rationals as integers or fraction strings. The output gives exact prices and a supporting matching, with zero-based good indices and `-1` for outside. The executable uses the ascent above.

```json
{"bids": [[2, 2], [1, 2], ["2/5", 0]], "slopes": [[2, 1], [1, 2], [1, 1]]}
```
