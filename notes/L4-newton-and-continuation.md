# L4 Newton bounds and the resource continuation

Ma, Végh and Vladu’s November 2026 preprint applies to the regularized P-matrix LCP in A4d. Its improvement is in the dependence on matrix handicap. A4d’s specified regularization can make even the square root of that handicap exponential in the original binary input length. The market analogy also gives a separate result for L1/L2. Two clearing calls give the exact endpoints at a fixed defender threshold, and the published algorithm’s eligible coordinates are exactly those below the upper endpoint. [Newton preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07815v1#S1) · [A4d reduction](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md) · [L2 analysis](L2-exact-arithmetic-audit.md)

## What the Newton theorem supplies

Use \(N\) for the LCP dimension and \(L'\) for the encoding length of the matrix and right side. The handicap \(\widehat\kappa(M)\) is the least \(\kappa\ge0\) such that for every real \(v\), writing \(P(v)\) for the sum of positive coordinate products \(v_i(Mv)_i\) and \(R(v)\) for the absolute sum of the negative ones, \(R(v)\le(1+4\kappa)P(v)\). Matrices with finite handicap are the sufficient matrices. Every P-matrix is sufficient. [Preprint, §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07815v1#S2.SS2)

Theorem 1.2 gives an exact solution or infeasibility using

\[
 O\!\left(
 \min\{\sqrt N(1+\widehat\kappa),N^{9/8}\sqrt{1+\widehat\kappa}\}
 (L'+\log(2+\widehat\kappa))\right)
\]

linear-system solves and \(O(\log(2+\widehat\kappa))\) feasibility LPs on complementary supports. Section 8 doubles the handicap guess, initializes at a known central point in a \(2N\)-dimensional embedding that preserves handicap, and recovers a rational solution by LP. Theorem 8.6 bounds the log of the handicap by a polynomial in the binary input size. The stated main bound counts system solves. It gives no intermediate-iterate bit-length bound for the literal exact Newton recurrence. A4d then needs its own exact support-repair LP, and in the minimum-price version one flow at the original capacities. [Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07815v1#S1.Thmtheorem1.2) · [Initialization and recovery](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07815v1#S8) · [A4d repair](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md#one-exact-p-lcp-call)

The square-root branch improves the general handicap dependence of sufficient-LCP interior-point methods compared in §1.2. E.-Nagy and Végh’s scaling algorithm can trade the original handicap for the best positive diagonal rescaling with larger polynomial factors. These are parameterized bounds. Generic P-LCP algorithms have a separate dimension dependence, including \(O^*(2^{N/2})\) expected time via Aldous’ algorithm. The October 2026 improvement for finding sinks of acyclic unique-sink orientations needs acyclicity. General P-LCP orientations can have directed cycles. [Newton preprint, §1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07815v1#S1.SS2) · [Handicap reduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10701v2) · [Fearnley et al., §5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03841#page=38) · [Acyclic orientation preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22546)

## The size of the A4d handicap

A4d writes \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&B^{\mathsf T}\\-A&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) in the order of flow variables followed by node variables. On the two-buyer, two-good slope block \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) use \(f=(1,-1,-1,1)\) and \(y=(5,7,-6,-4)\), with buyers first in \(y\). Then \(Af=0\), \(B^{\mathsf T}y=-f\), and \(\|y\|_2^2=126\). For the regularized matrix \(M+\varepsilon I\) evaluate the handicap inequality on \(v=(f,2\varepsilon y)\). The four edge products are \(-\varepsilon\) each and the node products sum to \(504\varepsilon^3\). Thus

\[
 \widehat\kappa(M+\varepsilon I)\ \ge\ \frac{1}{504\varepsilon^2}-\frac14 .
\]

The same witness works in the game with unit defender gains, setting all extra coordinates to zero. Take two types of prior \(1/2\), \(n\ge2\) targets, budget one, all uncovered payoffs one, and all losses one except \(d_{22}=2\). All displayed coefficients have bounded magnitudes. The direct LCP has \(N=4n+3\). A4d takes \(\varepsilon^{-1}=2KQ\) with \(Q\ge(N+1)!\), so even the square-root factor in the Newton solve-count guarantee can be factorial in \(N\). A fixed-dimension example also gives an exponential guarantee in the binary encoding length by taking one uncovered payoff \(2^t\) and the others one. Then \(K\) grows as \(2^t\) under A4d’s row shift and the same test applies. These are lower bounds on the parameter entering the upper bound. They do not give a lower bound on the number of accepted Newton steps. [A4d regularization and two-by-two witness](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md) · [Obstruction after arbitrary positive diagonal scaling](A4f-handicap-formulation.md)

For these inputs the Newton theorem is a different guarantee from L2’s \(2^{O(\min(k,n)\log(kn+1))}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) deterministic bit bound, where \(L\) is the game length. L2 counts feasible demand patterns and has a rational bit analysis of each step. A4d has \(N=kn+k+2n+1\) in the game and \(N=kn+k+n\) in the market. The substitution of A4d into the handicap theorem gives no improvement to L2’s general bound.

## The market endpoints of one threshold

Assume strictly positive attacker losses \(d_{ij}\) and gains \(\beta_j\), and set \(o_i=\max_j(a_{ij}-d_{ij})\). For a fixed positive defender threshold \(\lambda\), use capacities \(c_j=\lambda/\beta_j\), reserves zero and outside offers \(o_i\). The market prices are exactly the game’s coverage vectors at that threshold. If an original flow attacks a full target beyond \(c_j\), move the excess to the tied outside option. Conversely, outside mass can be sent to any target attaining \(o_i\) at full coverage. When outside is optimal, that target must have coverage one. Positive capacities imply that a price above one cannot clear. [Prior matching correspondence](L3-prior-partials.md#conditional-exact-reduction-to-a-minimum-price-oracle)

Let \(D\) be a common denominator of the masses and capacities, and \(\delta=1/(2nD)\). A4c gives the least coverage \(x^-(\lambda)\) by one clearing call at \(c+\delta\). A clearing call at \(c-\delta\) gives the greatest coverage \(x^+(\lambda)\). The smaller capacities remain positive. To prove the latter claim at those prices, write \(f(S)\) for buyer mass forced into a set \(S\) of goods and \(h(S)\) for mass touching \(S\). On nonempty sets, \(f(S)\le c(S)-|S|\delta<c(S)\). For sets of positive-price goods, \(h(S)\ge c(S)-|S|\delta\) gives \(h(S)\ge c(S)\) by the \(1/D\) grid. Thus the prices clear at the original capacities. If a different original clearing price raises a nonempty set \(S\) of coordinates, buyers who fill \(S\) there must be strictly restricted to \(S\) at the old prices. Their mass is at least \(c(S)\), contradicting \(f(S)<c(S)\). [A4c and both Hall conditions](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#one-call-at-enlarged-capacities) · [Extremal equilibria in unit markets, Alaei et al.](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~saeed/archive/competitive_equilibrium_with_general_utilities.pdf#page=12)

### Where a continuation seed can move

At a game state \((x,q)\) at threshold \(\lambda>0\), the L1/L2 residual graph has a forward arc on each best response and a reverse arc on each positive attack. An eligible seed is an unsaturated target at score \(\lambda\) whose reachable targets all have that status. Its eligibility is equivalent to \(x_j<x^+_j(\lambda)\).

For necessity of eligibility when a coordinate can rise, compare \(x\) with any other coverage \(w\) at \(\lambda\) and put \(S=\{j:w_j>x_j\}\). Let \(y^x,y^w\) be the attack column loads. A buyer sending any flow into \(S\) at \(w\) strictly prefers \(S\) at \(x\). Every target in \(S\) is unsaturated at \(x\) and covered at \(w\). Therefore

\[
 \lambda\sum_{j\in S}\tfrac1{\beta_j}
 \ \le\ y^w(S)\ \le\ \sum_{i:D_i(x)\subseteq S}m_i\
 \le\ y^x(S)\ \le\ \lambda\sum_{j\in S}\tfrac1{\beta_j}.
\]

Equality makes every target in \(S\) an open boundary target. It also makes every buyer using \(S\) at \(x\) demand only \(S\), so \(S\) is closed under residual reachability. Conversely, at an eligible seed the published shortest-path rates give a positive sufficiently small step at the same threshold. Eligibility at fixed margins is invariant under optimal flow tie choices, so the same characterization applies before choosing the normalizing flow. Taking \(w=x^+(\lambda)\) proves the claim. At a fixed threshold the set of eligible coordinates can only shrink as coverage rises. A run can still perform many flow switches while the same seed remains eligible. [Published Algorithms 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=7) · [L2 residual invariance](L2-exact-arithmetic-audit.md#non-repetition-with-exact-flow-ties)

After jumping to \(x^+(\lambda)\), A1’s LP at that coverage finds its smallest feasible threshold \(\lambda'\). It is strictly less than \(\lambda>0\), since a small decrease of \(\lambda\) lowers the capacities by less than \(\delta\) and leaves the shrunk market’s upper Hall bounds valid, while original lower bounds relax. By threshold order this coverage is the least coverage at \(\lambda'\), and it is the only coverage at thresholds strictly between \(\lambda'\) and \(\lambda\). If \(\lambda'>0\), the interval at \(\lambda'\) must have positive length, since a greatest point there would allow a further threshold decrease. Starting from the smallest threshold admissible at zero coverage enumerates the positive-length intervals with one clearing call and one ordinary LP each. Under uniform priors and equal gains, B2 restricts their positive thresholds to at most \(kn\) ratios. This counts intervals independently of any internal demand or flow switches. ### Exact threshold and budget recovery with a clearing oracle

For a rational requested budget \(0<r<n\), first test threshold zero by A1. Otherwise let \(\lambda^*>0\) be the largest threshold at some equilibrium of budget \(r\), which exists by compactness. At that coverage the upper endpoint of A1’s fixed-coverage Hall interval is \(\lambda^*=m(I)/\sum_{j\in J}(1/\beta_j)\) for some sets \(I,J\) with \(J\ne\varnothing\). For common denominators \(D_m,D_\beta\) of the masses and inverse gains, this ratio has denominator at most \(D_m\sum_j D_\beta/\beta_j\) in reduced form. The bit length is polynomial. Strict threshold ordering from B2 gives

\[
 \sum_j x^+_j(\lambda)\ge r \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0<\lambda\le\lambda^*.
\]

Above \(\lambda^*\), strong order bounds the sum by \(r\), and equality would contradict maximality. Below it, every coverage dominates an equilibrium at \(\lambda^*\). Bisection on \([0,1+\max_j\beta_j]\) followed by rational reconstruction therefore finds the exact threshold in polynomially many calls. [A1 Hall interval and zero threshold](A1-threshold-transport.md) · [B2 threshold order](B2-component-composition.md#budget-intervals-at-a-common-threshold)

At that exact threshold keep the capacities fixed. Put \(q=x^+(\lambda^*)\) and let \(P(t)\) be the least market prices at reserves \(tq\), \(0\le t\le1\). Domination of buyer-feasible prices implies monotonicity in the reserves and \(P(t)\le q\). At \(t=0\) the price is \(x^-\), and at \(t=1\) it is \(q\). For continuity, take convergent subsequences of these bounded prices and their flows. The limiting prices and flows clear at the limiting reserves. For every set strictly above those reserves the strict minimum-price Hall inequality holds along the sequence eventually. Every best edge along the sequence close enough to its limit is best at that limit. This preserves the strict inequality and identifies the limit as the least price.

Each \(P(t)\) also clears with zero reserves. For \(S=\{j:P_j(t)<q_j\}\), the buyers touching \(S\) at \(q\) are all restricted to \(S\) at \(P(t)\), and their mass is at least \(c(S)\). Upper feasibility gives equality and forces full sales on \(S\). No other buyer can strictly improve by buying in \(S\), since that would overload it. These other buyers can therefore retain their flows from \(q\) on its unchanged coordinates and outside. This supplies a clearing flow at the original reserves. Equivalently, run one original fixed-price flow after the search. A straight chord between extremes can fail even in a two-by-two game. Take equal priors, unit gains and \(\lambda=1/2\), with rows \((1-8x_A,-5x_B)\) and \((-2x_A,2-5x_B)\). The two extremes \((0,0)\) and \((1,1)\) each support a balanced attack, and their midpoint makes both rows strictly prefer \(B\).

To find the earliest \(t\) with \(\sum_j P_j(t)=r\), discard the endpoint cases and fix the support and unsold goods at a trial point. The price constraints for that support have rational coefficients and right sides affine in \(t\). The least price uniquely minimizes the sum within that polyhedron and is a vertex. Thus \(P(t)\) is a continuous selection of finitely many affine branches. At the earliest crossing, the affine branch on its left has nonzero sum slope. Its \(n\) independent active price rows, together with \(\sum p_j=r\), give a nonsingular rational system in \(n+1\) variables for the price and \(t\). Clearing the rows and applying Cramer’s rule gives a computable polynomial-bit denominator bound for \(t\), uniform over the branches. Exact bisection and reconstruction find it. The supply increase from A4c implements each least-price query in this search. This replaces L3’s approximate threshold and budget bracketing in the strict game with exact rational searches. For weak signs A1’s perturbation and original support repair transfer the endpoint algorithm. The underlying extremal statement can fail without strict losses. With one indifferent type, two unit gains and \(\lambda=3/4\), each of \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) is attainable, while covering both requires more attack mass than exists. ## Limits on following the entire path

B3f’s different unique perfect matchings occur in one threshold interval. On any continuous equilibrium path that crosses the witness budgets, an affine coverage piece has only \(O(kn^2)\) possible demand cells, counting a comparison breakpoint itself as a cell. A piece with polynomial coordinates of degree at most \(d\) has \(O(dkn^2)\) cells. The bound also holds for pole-free rational coordinates of degree at most \(d\) in numerator and denominator, since clearing two denominators gives degree at most \(2d\). A demand cell cannot witness two different unique matchings. For polynomial \(d\), any such path needs \(n^{\Omega(\log n)}\) pieces, even if the attack is allowed to change within a piece. [B3f witness intervals and unique matchings](B3f-forced-flow-switches.md#why-every-equilibrium-uses-the-prescribed-assignment)

The hard family has slopes within \(1/(64nM)\) of one. This difference can be made arbitrarily smaller with polynomial-bit precision by decreasing B3f’s perturbation. Its spread, assignment-gap and strict log-cost inequalities still hold. The slopes meet A4a’s contraction condition in every query above, since supplies and reserves change while slopes do not. Thus the two searches compute a specified rational budget of that family in polynomial bit time, including between witness intervals. The Newton central path also solves a fixed endpoint and need not follow an exact resource-budget path. The two methods' shared use of products along graph paths has different roles. L2 computes the rate of a valid equilibrium direction in the current residual graph. The new preprint uses products in a scaled Newton inverse to control a centrality neighborhood.
