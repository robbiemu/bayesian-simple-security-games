# Degree three already contains the general unit problem

**Theorem.** Every supplied unit market with rational affine offers reduces by one oracle call to a supplied unit market whose viable buyer–good incidence graph has maximum degree three on both sides. The offers with slopes other than one can be confined to a matching. Deleting the edges representing the original offers leaves a collection of alternating paths, with a common intercept and slope one on all their offers. This makes degree three sufficient for the full unit problem. Degree two on either one side is tractable by the direct algorithm. [Degree-two solver](A4j-unit-market-search.md)

## Split one buyer into a path of unit buyers

Shift reserves and outside utilities to zero. Discard nonpositive net bids, since an allocation on those edges can take outside without changing prices whenever they tie at reserve. A buyer with no viable edge can be left outside, and one with a single viable edge can be kept. Suppose a buyer has \(d\ge2\) distinct viable offers. Introduce unit buyers \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\) and private unit goods \(h_1,\ldots,h_{d-1}\). Each \(v_k\) keeps just the original offer with index \(k\), and she has offer \(K-p_h\) on her consecutive private goods \(h_{k-1},h_k\) that exist. Only \(v_k,v_{k+1}\) can use \(h_k\). Choose \(K\) strictly larger than zero and every original intercept of this buyer. The out-of-component offers can all have negative intercept.

Every private good has positive price at a clearing. If a maximal consecutive run of \(z\) private goods were at zero, its \(z+1\) incident buyers would all strictly require that run. They can obtain \(K\) there. Every external offer, outside, and every adjacent private good outside the run would give less. This contradicts the run's capacity.

The fixed-price clearance flow can be integral because all margins are one. The \(d-1\) private goods sell out to distinct clone buyers. Exactly one clone, say \(v_r\), takes her original real offer or outside. The private matching is then forced, with \(v_k\) holding \(h_k\) for \(k<r\) and \(h_{k-1}\) for \(k>r\). Posted private utilities \(K-p_h\) weakly decrease away from \(r\), because each private buyer chooses the edge toward \(r\) over her adjacent alternative. The departing clone's attained utility is at least both adjacent private utilities that exist, hence at least the attained utility of every other clone. Every other clone's attained utility is in turn at least her external offer and outside. Accordingly the departing clone chose a maximizer among **all** original offers and outside. Keep just this choice to obtain the original buyer's allocation. The original goods retain their capacities and sell out at positive prices.

Conversely, take any integral clearing in the original market. Let the clone of the assigned edge depart, or choose any clone when the original buyer takes outside. Set all private prices for this buyer to \(K-u_i\), where \(u_i\) is her original indirect utility. This is positive. Fill them with the matching directed toward the departing clone. The private utility \(u_i\) weakly dominates every external bid and outside, while the departing clone attains \(u_i\) by her original option. The original price vector thus lifts to a clearing. Applying the split independently to all buyers preserves the full original price set by projection and adds only \(O(|E(H)|)\) buyers and goods, where \(H\) is the viable incidence graph.

## Split each original good and decode its maximum price

After the buyer splits, replace an original good incident to \(d\) viable offers by \(d\) unit goods in order, one per offer. Between consecutive goods place a unit buyer offering \(K-p\) on both neighbors. The original offer connects only to its designated copy of the good. Keep the first-stage private goods unchanged. Use the common intercept \(K>\max(0,\max_{ij}b_{ij},\max_{ij}b_{ij}/d_{ij})\).

At any clearing the largest price on a good's path is strictly below \(K\). Otherwise let a maximal consecutive run attain that largest price. The run's goods are positive-price and cannot be bought through their external offers, whose utilities there are negative. A guard buyer at a boundary of the run prefers the cheaper neighbor, and the guards strictly internal to the run number one fewer than its goods. The run cannot sell out. Thus each of the \(d-1\) guard buyers has positive utility and purchases a real good. In an integral clearing matching the guards purchase all positions except one \(r\), which is bought through the associated external offer or is unsold. The private matching assigns each guard to the good farther from \(r\). Since that good is weakly cheaper than her other option, the price at \(r\) is a maximum on the path. When \(r\) is unsold this maximum is zero.

Merge each path to its maximum price. Every external sale keeps its old price, and the competing external offers only become more expensive. Retain the external purchases of the original buyers' clones and their allocations on the first-stage private goods, and drop the guards. This clears the market after the buyer splits. Then remove those splits using the departure rule proved above. Conversely, from a clearing after the buyer splits, set every copy of each original good to its old price. A positive original price must be affordable relative to outside on a sold offer, so all these prices are below \(K\). Choose the port of the external sale, or any port when unsold, and assign the guards away from that port. They all maximize utility, and the old purchases retain their prices. Together the two lifts represent every clearing of the original market.

The resulting market has degree at most three on each side. Every potentially nonunit original offer joins dedicated ports, with at most one original offer incident to any port. Removing these edges leaves an alternating path for each original vertex with at least two offers and singleton or isolated vertices for the rest. All path offers have slope one and intercept \(K\). Call the least-price oracle once on this market and take the maximum port price on each original good's path. The decoded vector clears the original market and is at most its least vector, since a lift of the original minimum was available to the oracle and the decoder is order preserving. Hence it equals the original minimum. Restore reserves and recover a matching by one original flow. The number of new goods, buyers and viable offers is linear in the original incidence size. A dense listing and all rational coefficients have polynomial length.

This places the unrestricted task on bounded-degree graphs with a matching of heterogeneous offers. It also shows why bounding the number of exceptional edges incident to each vertex differs from bounding their total number.
