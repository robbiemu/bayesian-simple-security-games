# A4g Bounded search for equilibrium supports

The useful maze analogy is to try a restricted set of routes and verify the endpoint exactly. A4d needs any clearing equilibrium, and A4c selects minimum prices by a supply perturbation. There is no shortest-route requirement to relax. Bounding the work and returning an unresolved result is compatible with exact answers when the search succeeds. Candidate prices propagated along tight paths give one such search. A second solves selected affine regions of the regularized LCP. Ordinary breadth-first search and following the standard Lemke line can both take superpolynomial time on A4d matrices. [A4d](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md) · [A4c](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md)

## The implicit basis graph

On an explicit maze, breadth-first search is polynomial in the number of vertices and edges and already gives a shortest unweighted route. Planar obstacle-following methods have different guarantees. For example, Bug2 bounds its route by quantities involving obstacle perimeters and intersections with the start–target line. These quantities have no given polynomial analog in an implicit LCP basis graph. [Lumelsky and Stepanov, Theorem 3](https://robotics.caltech.edu/~jwb/courses/ME132/handouts/LumelskyBug.pdf#page=19)

In an \(N\)-variable P-LCP, a complementary basis chooses which variable of each pair is basic. A principal linear solve gives the corresponding values, and negative basic values specify outgoing pivot directions in the usual cube orientation, with a tie convention in degenerate cases. The graph has \(2^N\) vertices. Its unique-sink property does imply a shortest possible directed route from any vertex to the sink. Fix all bits where the vertex agrees with the sink. In the resulting face, the sink is still unique, so any other vertex has an outgoing edge flipping a differing bit. Iteration reaches the sink in Hamming distance. The proof needs the unknown sink to identify those bits. [Fearnley et al., §4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03841#page=33)

There is an A4d example where ordinary breadth-first search visits at least \(2^n\) bases. Use one buyer of mass \(2n\), \(n\) goods of capacity 1, and unit intercepts and slopes. For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the regularized solution has \(u=0,\ p_j=(1-\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon^2)\), and \(x_j=(1+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon^2)\). Its basis has \(2n\) bits set. All \(2^n\) bases selecting just a subset \(S\) of the purchases are reachable at depth \(|S|\). Their basic purchases equal \(1/\varepsilon\) on \(S\) and every unselected purchase has residual \(-1\). Directed additions can therefore build every \(S\). Any exact original support repair also needs all prices and purchases, since total mass exceeds supply and clearance requires \(u=0,\ p=\mathbf1,\ x=\mathbf1\). A certificate confined to a basis cannot appear before depth \(2n\). For the one-good instance and \(\varepsilon<1/3\), the only outgoing pivot from the zero basis also worsens the number, maximum and sum of negative basic values. A rule that permits only nonworsening steps in those merits stalls immediately.

## A long all-ones Lemke path inside A4d

**Theorem.** The A4d market construction has inputs for which the all-ones Lemke covering ray crosses \(n^{\Omega(\log n)}\) distinct affine pieces. Attempting exact repair after each step using only purchases positive at the current ray point still needs superpolynomially many pieces. The result concerns this covering vector and this support restriction. The market slopes are in A4a’s polynomial-time near-factorized class. [All-ones covering vector in Fearnley et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03841#page=35) · [A4a](A4a-near-factorized-slopes.md)

Take B3f’s matrices \(B,C\) with \(|B_{ij}|+|C_{ij}|\le M\) and disjoint witness intervals in \(s\in[1/(4n),1/n]\). Each interval has a distinct unique best assignment of weights \(B_{ij}-C_{ij}s\), separated from all others by at least 1. Set \(\gamma=1/(64nM^2)\). Give each of \(n\) core buyers mass 1 and offers \(2+\gamma B_{ij}-(1+\gamma C_{ij})p_j\). Add a buyer of mass 1 with offers \(1/n-p_j\) on every good. Use capacities \(1+\eta\), where \(\eta=1/(4n)\), and zero reserves and outsides. A4d uses \(M_{\mathrm{LCP}}+\varepsilon I\). Its prescribed \(\varepsilon\) is at most \(\gamma/(128n)\) because some slope differs from 1, so its reduced denominator is at least \(1/(\gamma M)\). The integer row bound in A4d’s repair includes that denominator, and the denominator bound used in its perturbation is at least its square. Both the original and reduced binary sizes are polynomial in \(n\). [B3f separated assignments and input size](B3f-forced-flow-switches.md#a-parametric-assignment-with-separated-optima) · [A4d choice of diagonal](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md#one-exact-p-lcp-call)

On the all-ones covering ray the right side is shifted by \(\tau\mathbf1\). For \(0\le\tau\le1/n\), write \(s=1/n-\tau\). The edge residual is \(u_i+d_{ij}p_j-b_{ij}+\tau+\varepsilon x_{ij}\). The row and column bounds are \(1+\tau+\varepsilon u_i\) and \(1+\eta+\tau+\varepsilon p_j\), with equality at positive utility or price. A positive utility or price requires a positive purchase in that row or column, and an edge equality then bounds its utility and price by \(u_i\le3,\ p_j\le4\). Every purchase is less than 2 by the row bounds.

The minimum price is at most \(s\). A zero price suffices. If every price is positive, total sales are at least \(n(1+\eta+\tau)\), whereas the core buyers can send only \(n(1+\tau+3\varepsilon)\). An extra-buyer purchase thus gives \(p_j\le s\) by its edge equality. This low price makes every core utility positive by its edge inequality and the core intercepts near 2. The extra buyer has zero utility. Otherwise every buyer would buy at least \(1+\tau\), exceeding total capacity \(n(1+\eta+\tau+4\varepsilon)\). Its edge inequalities now give \(p_j\ge s-2\varepsilon\) on every good.

A set of \(r\) core buyers purchases at least \(r(1+\tau)\). If they touched at most \(r-1\) goods by positive purchases, those goods could accept at most \((r-1)(1+\tau)+n(\eta+4\varepsilon)<r(1+\tau)\). Hall’s theorem gives a perfect matching on the positive core purchases. In particular a core buyer purchases each good. Compare that purchase to the cheapest good in the same row. Its unregularized utility is within \(2\varepsilon\) of the best in the row, and the deviations from common slope and intercept are bounded using \(p_j\le4\). This gives \(p_j-\min p\le8\gamma M+2\varepsilon\), and hence \(|p_j-s|\le8\gamma M+2\varepsilon\).

For any matching \(\pi\) in the positive core support, sum the same row comparisons against a competing assignment \(\rho\). The common price sums cancel. Writing \(W_\rho(s)=\sum_i(B_{i,\rho(i)}-C_{i,\rho(i)}s)\), one obtains

\[
 W_\rho(s)-W_\pi(s)\le
 2nM(8\gamma M+2\varepsilon)+2n\varepsilon/\gamma<1 .
\]

Thus the positive support admits exactly the prescribed matching in each witness interval. The P-matrix has a unique solution at every shifted right side, and the Lemke path follows this continuous piecewise-affine solution from the all-slack point down to \(\tau=0\). On the relative interior of one affine segment a nonnegative coordinate is either identically zero or strictly positive. Hence one segment cannot account for two witness intervals having different unique matchings. The bound counts geometric pieces regardless of zero-length pivots. [B3f gap](B3f-forced-flow-switches.md#a-parametric-assignment-with-separated-optima) · [Lemke trajectory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03841#page=35)

In the original market at \(\varepsilon=\tau=0\), the same comparisons require the last witness matching \(\pi_{\mathrm{last}}\). Every price is at least \(1/n\), so all goods sell out and the extra buyer purchases positive mass. At \(\tau\ge1/n\) the regularized extra buyer has no positive edge, excluding an original solution supported there. For the remaining ray let \(s_0\) be an interior sample from the penultimate witness interval. The function \(\max_\rho W_\rho(s)-W_{\pi_{\mathrm{last}}}(s)\) is convex, is zero at \(1/n\), and is at least 1 at \(s_0\). It is at least 1 for every \(0\le s\le s_0\) by interpolation between \(s\) and \(1/n\). The comparison bound thus excludes \(\pi_{\mathrm{last}}\) from the positive support until after the ray has crossed all earlier witnesses and reached the penultimate one. Exact repair using only that support cannot finish earlier. A guessed support that adds other edges is outside this bound.

## Candidate prices from tight paths

Consider the original market normalized to zero reserves and outside utilities. A price is *buyer-feasible* if all masses can be sent to best options with only upper capacities, including outside. Define \(f_p(S)\) as the mass whose entire demand is in the goods set \(S\). A buyer-feasible price has \(f_p(S)\le c(S)\) for all \(S\). The componentwise least clearing price is also below every buyer-feasible price by A4’s strict minimum-price test. [Fixed-price Hall tests](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#one-call-at-enlarged-capacities) · [Price domination](A4b-direct-hall-search.md#removing-one-good-at-a-trial-price)

### Searching a product of lists without enumerating it

For each good, supply a finite sorted list of rational prices at or above reserve, including the reserve itself. Start there. If a buyer flow fails, find an inclusion-minimal set \(S\) with \(f_p(S)>c(S)\). This takes at most \(n+1\) flow tests. Starting with all goods, try deleting each good from a current containing set \(U\). Keep the deletion if a deficient subset remains, which is tested by restricting to the buyers whose entire demand is in the new \(U\), with the same edges and capacities. Once a deletion fails, subsequent restriction cannot make that same deletion succeed. At the end \(U\) itself is an inclusion-minimal deficient set.

Every coordinate in \(S\) must rise in every buyer-feasible \(q\ge p\). Otherwise let \(T=\{j\in S:q_j>p_j\}\subsetneq S\). Buyers trapped in \(S\) at \(p\) but not trapped entirely in \(T\) have a best good in \(S\setminus T\). That good keeps its price. All offers in \(T\) strictly worsen and options outside \(S\) were already worse and cannot improve. At \(q\) these buyers are trapped in \(S\setminus T\). Their mass is \(f_p(S)-f_p(T)>c(S)-c(T)\) by minimality, a contradiction.

Raise every good in \(S\) to its next listed price. If a successor is missing, there is no buyer-feasible point in the grid. Otherwise induction keeps the iterate below every buyer-feasible grid point. At the first feasible iterate it is therefore the least buyer-feasible point on the grid, after at most the total list length many rank advances. Test it for original clearance and minimum price by the usual flow and reachability certificate. If the minimum price was in the lists, this test succeeds. Otherwise the procedure can report that the grid misses the minimum. The proof does not require knowing which anchor paths are tight. Garg gives a related minimal-overdemand step for the unit common-slope assignment market. [Garg, SPAA 2020](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~garg/dist/garg-spaa20.pdf) · [A4 certificate](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#certifying-a-candidate-minimum-price)

### Choosing the lists

For complete generators using masses, see the block and load constructions in [A4h](A4h-complete-candidate-generation.md).

Drop the edges with \(b_{ij}\le0\), since such trades can be sent outside whenever they occur at reserve. In the remaining bipartite graph, each good at the minimum has a tight path to a good at reserve or a buyer at outside. This follows by forgetting the directions of A4’s reachability certificate. Start from zero at a guessed anchor and propagate \(u_i+d_{ij}p_j=b_{ij}\) along a proposed simple path. The terminal price is rational with polynomial bit length. If the path is tight at the minimum and the anchor is correct, the resulting candidate is exact. Paths from other guesses only add list entries.

- Enumerating every path of a fixed maximum length gives a polynomial algorithm whenever each price has a tight anchor path of that length.
- Given a guessed forest of offers, enumerate paths using at most a fixed \(a\) edges outside the forest. Choose the ordered, oriented exceptional edges and join them by the unique forest paths, discarding nonsimple walks. This costs \((1+2|E|)^{O(a)}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) and allows long portions in the forest. Several forests can contribute to the same lists. Success needs one qualifying tight anchor path per good.
- In an incidence component of cycle rank \(r=|E|-|V|+1\), two simple paths between the same endpoints differ by an element of the binary cycle space. For each endpoint pair, take the symmetric differences of the tree path with the \(2^r\) sums of fundamental cycles, retaining simple paths. This lists every simple path. The resulting exact solver takes \(2^{O(r_{\max})}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bit time, where \(r_{\max}\) is the largest component cycle rank. In particular logarithmic rank suffices, and the number of components can grow.

One practical forest guess sorts edges by their offer slack at a candidate price, then runs Kruskal’s algorithm. If the true tight graph is a forest and all true tight slacks are ranked before all other slacks, this forest contains the true anchor paths. For example, that ranking holds if the price error in sup norm is less than \(\gamma/(4d_{\max})\), where \(\gamma\) is the smallest strictly positive slack at the minimum. The maximum utility of a buyer varies by at most \(d_{\max}\) times the price error, as does any offer. A4f’s generic intercept perturbation supplies the forest property, and the final exact support repair can undo that perturbation. A rough guess can instead allow a few off-forest edges. [Generic tight forests and recovery](A4f-handicap-formulation.md#locating-a-well-conditioned-support)

Full path lists can be exponentially large. In a chain of \(h\) diamonds, use two buyers between each consecutive pair of goods. Unit-slope offers on the left have intercept \(C=2^{h+1}\). On the right one buyer has \(C\) and the other has \(C+2^{i-1}\) at link \(i\). From the first good at reserve, path propagation gives all \(2^h\) sums of the increments at the last good. The goods-interaction graph is a path, so A4b can solve those markets by a different method. The example bounds the list strategy, rather than exact market search. [A4b forest algorithm](A4b-direct-hall-search.md#polynomial-time-on-arbitrary-forests)

## Testing affine regions of the regularized market

In A4f’s signed node coordinates \(h=(u,-p)\), the regularized edge flow is \(x_e=[b_e-B_e^{\mathsf T}h]_+/\varepsilon\). For a proposed set \(E'\) of edges using the positive part, eliminate their flows by the affine formula and fix the other flows to zero. The remaining node problem has affine matrix \(\varepsilon I+\varepsilon^{-1}A_{E'}B_{E'}^{\mathsf T}\). Its directed Laplacian part can be balanced by a positive stationary vector on each connected component. Column scaling by that vector makes the symmetric part positive definite, since the regularizing diagonal is positive. Undoing the good sign convention puts the node bounds in ordinary complementarity form. The resulting monotone LCP can be solved in polynomial bit time, for example by minimizing the resulting convex quadratic complementarity gap over nonnegative variables and residuals. This keeps the natural price and utility bounds inside the solve and needs no guesses about which node coordinates vanish. Verify all proposed edge signs exactly, then apply the original support repair. [A4f node matrices](A4f-handicap-formulation.md#eliminating-flow-variables) · [Exact convex quadratic programming](https://doi.org/10.1016/0041-5553(80)90098-1)

Along a rational segment in node space, each affine offer sign changes at most once. Testing all sign patterns on the segment and at breakpoints therefore costs polynomially many region solves. This makes a bounded set of ray or segment guesses a partial exact algorithm. It does not need the true solution to lie on a segment. It is sufficient that a visited affine region contains the solution in its closure. Either formula agrees at a zero offer. Every returned solution is checked independently of the way the segment was chosen.

For a certified search, maintain lower and upper bounds on the unique fixed point of A4f’s order-preserving map \(F\). Starting from its box endpoints, applications of \(F\) keep the bounds nested. Offer intervals on this box certify all signs except those spanning zero. If \(s\) offers remain ambiguous, try \(2^s\) regions and solve their node problems. After certified-inactive offers are removed, independent components only cost a sum of such bounds, with \(s\) counted separately in each component. This is polynomial on brackets with logarithmically many ambiguous signs per component. Arbitrary brackets can be cut off and still give a sound partial search.

Some ambiguous offers can stay in a single solve. Let \(P\) be the fixed-active offers and \(U\) the unresolved ones. Eliminate flows in \(P\), leaving complementarity on \(U\). Seek a positive vector \(\pi\) satisfying \(B_U^{\mathsf T}\pi=0\) and \((\varepsilon^2 I+A_PB_P^{\mathsf T})\pi\ge0\). These are rational linear feasibility conditions with a normalization \(\pi\ge\mathbf1\). Scale nodes by \(\operatorname{diag}(\pi)\) and unresolved edge residuals by \(1/\pi_i\) at their buyer \(i\). The cross blocks become skew, since \(\pi_i=d_{ij}\pi_j\) on \(U\). The scaled node block has nonpositive off-diagonals and nonnegative row and positive column sums, so its symmetric part is positive definite. This yields one monotone LCP. With certified fixed signs the true solution lies in it, and with guessed signs an exact final check still decides whether the solve succeeded.

One can take long straight jumps while preserving these bounds. For a lower endpoint \(\ell\le F(\ell)\) on a fixed dyadic grid, let \(d\ge0\) be \(F(\ell)\) rounded down to the grid minus \(\ell\). Along \(\ell+t d\) take the largest nonnegative integer \(t\) inside the box with \(\ell+t d\le F(\ell+t d)\). The affine offer breakpoints divide the ray into polynomially many scalar intervals over which these inequalities are linear. Solve each interval and inspect its integer endpoints. If \(d\ne0\), isotonicity guarantees \(t=1\) is feasible. Any accepted endpoint remains below the fixed point. The grid bounds bit growth, and the upper jump is symmetric. When rounding gives zero direction, sufficiently fine polynomial-bit mesh permits exact rational reconstruction by A4f’s residual bound. A polynomial bound on the number of other jumps is unavailable. Even the exact residual-direction version can be slow in a single cell. With one buyer and one good, \(b=2,d=1,m=c=1\), its signed node matrix is proportional to \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+\varepsilon^2&-1\\-1&1+\varepsilon^2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Once one node residual is zero, maximal postfixed residual rays alternate coordinate updates and reduce both coordinate errors by only \((1+\varepsilon^2)^{-2}\) per pair. The region solve bypasses these steps.

There is also a direct shortcut for a small set of exceptional complementarity variables. Fix one coordinate to \(t\ge0\) and solve the remaining principal P-LCP. The removed residual is a continuous piecewise-affine function of \(t\). On a piece whose other active set is \(S\), its slope is \(\det M_{S\cup\{e\}}/\det M_S>0\) by the Schur complement. Testing zero, then bisecting and reconstructing the unique positive root when needed, eliminates that coordinate. If deleting a fixed number of offer or node variables leaves a slope-balanced matrix, the deepest principal problem is monotone after diagonal scaling. For the direct game formulation include the budget multiplier among the deleted variables so it does not couple price scales. This gives a polynomial number of polynomial-bit solves at fixed deletion count. For a uniform bit bound, clear the matrix rows once and use a principal determinant bound \(\Delta\) independent of the right side. With an integer right side, integer bisection followed by dyadic refinement of one unit interval to width \(1/(4\Delta^2)\) gives trial denominators of polynomial bit length. Clear each trial denominator in the next right side and scale back afterward. Each level adds only polynomially many bits. This trades basis exploration for scalar order on the omitted variables. [A4d principal minors](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md#why-every-principal-minor-is-nonnegative)

## Why tuning a mass cutoff alone fails

For one buyer of mass 1, use unit slopes, intercepts \((1+\zeta,1,1-\zeta)\) and capacities \((\zeta,4,4)\), with \(0<\zeta\le1/64\). The original clearing prices are \((\zeta,0,0)\) and purchases are \((\zeta,1-\zeta,0)\). For \(8\zeta\le\varepsilon\le1/4\), the regularized purchases satisfy \(x_C>x_A>0\). To see this, the total is bounded by \(1+\varepsilon(1+\zeta)<2\), so \(p_B=p_C=0\) and \(x_A\le\zeta+\varepsilon(1+\zeta)\). The utility is positive since \(u=0\) would make \(x_B=1/\varepsilon\ge4\). If \(x_C=0\), then \(u\ge1-\zeta\) and \(x_B\le\zeta/\varepsilon\), making total purchases less than 1. Thus \(x_B,x_C>0\), with \(x_B-x_C=\zeta/\varepsilon\). The row balance and \(3[\zeta+\varepsilon(1+\zeta)]+\zeta/\varepsilon<1\) give \(x_C>x_A\). If \(x_A=0\), its price is zero and its slack requires \(u\ge1+\zeta\), contradicting positive purchases elsewhere. So dropping the smallest purchases cannot retain the indispensable first edge while removing the spurious third edge. This concerns a single within-row threshold or top-\(k\) repair rule. It leaves capacity-aware support guesses available.
