# L2 · Exact bit complexity of the Catcher–Evader continuation

An exact rational implementation of the single-attack continuation runs in $2^{O(\sqrt{nk}\log(nk+1))}\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit time, where $n$ is the number of targets, $k$ is the number of positive-prior types, and $L$ is the input length. The implementation uses a fixed order for selecting the coverage-increase seed and returns vertices of two rational flow polytopes. An independent proof of signature non-repetition handles zero-cost cycles and changes of seed. The perturbation and repair in [A1](A1-threshold-transport.md#4-transfer-an-exact-positive-coefficient-solver-to-weak-inequalities) extend the bound to zero gains and slopes. These deductions concern the continuation path. The direct search in A1 has an independent bit-complexity analysis. [Conitzer, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08796) · [Li, Conitzer and Korzhyk, §6.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896)

## State and exact steps

L4 identifies the eligible seeds by the greatest coverage at a fixed threshold and gives an exact market-oracle jump across the corresponding budget interval. Its Newton comparison treats a separate algorithm for the endpoint. [Threshold endpoints and Newton bounds](L4-newton-and-continuation.md)

Let $x_t\in[0,1]$ be defender coverage and $q_{it}\ge0$ be attack mass with row sum $p_i>0$, where $\sum_i p_i=1$. Write $z_t=\sum_i q_{it}$, $s_t=\beta_t z_t$, and $v_i=\max_t(a_{it}-d_{it}x_t)$, with $d_{it},\beta_t>0$. Let $A=\{(i,t):a_{it}-d_{it}x_t=v_i\}$, and let $D_i$ be the neighborhood of row $i$ in $A$. While coverage remains to be assigned, use

\[
\lambda=\max_{t:x_t<1}s_t,\qquad B=\{t:x_t<1,\ s_t=\lambda\}.
\]

Defender best response requires $s_t\ge\lambda$ wherever $x_t>0$. All attack mass lies on $A$ since each type can place its entire prior on any one target. Initialize at $x=0$ and assign each type to its first maximizing target. Test budget completion before evaluating the open boundary, including with budget zero or when the last target becomes full. [Li et al., §§2, 3.1 and Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896)

**Algorithm 2.** Minimize $\sum_{(i,t)\in A}q_{it}\log d_{it}$ with the prescribed row and column totals. The residual graph used below consists of type and target vertices. Each active pair contributes a forward arc of cost $\log d_{it}$ and each positive entry a reverse arc of cost $-\log d_{it}$. Row conservation makes the individual cap $q_{it}\le p_i$ redundant. Omitting it can add a forward arc on the single edge already carrying its entire row. A target-rooted simple path that enters this row must enter from the same target, so the extra arc only permits a zero-cost return to that target. The capped and uncapped formulations give the same target-rooted distances and reachability. Fixing row and column totals implements the prescribed full flow in the paper's source–sink formulation. [Li et al., Algorithm 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=7)

**Algorithm 3.** Use the first seed $b\in B$ in a fixed order whose reachable targets all belong to $B$. For every vertex $u$ set $\gamma_u=\exp(-\operatorname{dist}_b(u))$ and use zero for unreachable vertices. Forward-arc inequalities give $\gamma_i\le d_{it}\gamma_t$ on active pairs. A reverse arc adds the opposite inequality, giving equality on every positive entry. Thus increasing $x_t$ at rate $\gamma_t$, with $q$ fixed, keeps those entries best responses as $v_i$ falls at rate $\gamma_i$. Stop at the first budget event, unit cap, or new tie. An inactive pair can catch up only if $\gamma_i-d_{it}\gamma_t>0$, and its event time is

\[
\frac{v_i-a_{it}+d_{it}x_t}{\gamma_i-d_{it}\gamma_t}.
\]

This agrees with line 23 after negating its numerator and denominator. The forward-rate test handles a zero denominator. Every tested time is positive, and the seed rate is one. Defender best response is preserved because all increasing targets were at the maximum open score. If the last members of $B$ fill and budget remains, recompute $\lambda$ from the remaining unsaturated targets. It may fall at that step, with a change of $B$. [Li et al., Algorithm 3 and Lemmas 3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=7)

**Algorithm 4.** If every seed fails, maximize $\Delta$ over a prospective flow $q'$ supported on $A$, with nonnegative entries, row sums $p_i$ and column totals $z'_t=\sum_iq'_{it}$. Impose

\[
\begin{aligned}
 0&\le\Delta\le\lambda,\\
 \beta_t z'_t+\Delta&=\lambda &&(t\in B),\\
 z'_t&\ge z_t &&(t\notin B),\\
 \beta_t z'_t+\Delta&\le\lambda &&(t\notin B,\ x_t<1).
\end{aligned}
\]

The signed difference $q'-q$ traverses forward and reverse residual arcs. Its net departure from each $t\in B$ is $\Delta/\beta_t$, and its net arrival at each other target is $z'_t-z_t$. Conversely a feasible residual rerouting gives a prospective flow satisfying these constraints. A full target can absorb any incoming mass permitted by the rows. At each unsaturated target outside $B$, the last inequality and $z'_t\ge z_t$ imply the paper's upper bound $\Delta\le\lambda-s_t$. If this family is empty, $\Delta\le\lambda$ supplies the endpoint. The low-site case in the proof of Lemma 5 uses $s_t<\lambda$, a comparison between scores. The LP is compact and exactly implements the maximum feasible decrease that the pseudocode describes via binary search. [Li et al., Algorithm 4 and Lemma 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=8)

The LP has a positive optimum after failure. Pick a residual path from every $b\in B$ to a target outside $B$. Simultaneously send $\delta/\beta_b$ along these paths for sufficiently small positive $\delta$. Used reverse arcs have positive mass, every unsaturated endpoint outside $B$ has positive score slack, and failure implies $\lambda>0$. The new threshold is $\lambda-\Delta$, and the inequalities preserve both best responses. If the optimal move adds no target to $B$, its unsaturated outside inequalities are all strict. Reoptimize the flow with Algorithm 2 at the new column totals. If every seed still failed, the same small-path argument at that flow would give a further decrease, a feasible point of the original LP because outside column totals remain nondecreasing. This contradicts optimality. At a zero threshold every unsaturated target has zero mass and its residual search is immediately eligible. This proves the failure accounting for every maximizing flow, including optima with ties. [Li et al., Lemmas 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=9)

## Non-repetition with exact flow ties

The published proof of Lemma 8 invokes hypothetical Algorithm 4 outputs at repeated signatures and leaves the effect of changing the seed implicit. The argument below counts the signatures immediately before successful increases. [Li et al., Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=9)

**Threshold at a successful input.** For any nonempty $S\subseteq B$, rows with $D_i\subseteq S$ can only send their mass into $S$. Hence $\sum_{i:D_i\subseteq S}p_i\le z(S)=\lambda\sum_{t\in S}1/\beta_t$. For an eligible seed, take $S$ to be its reachable targets. Every row with positive flow into $S$ is reachable via a reverse arc, and all its active targets are reachable via forward arcs. The inequality is then an equality. Thus at every successful input,

\[
\lambda=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq B}
\frac{\sum_{i:D_i\subseteq S}p_i}{\sum_{t\in S}1/\beta_t}.
\]

The right side serves as a proof certificate. The implementation uses residual reachability. In a fixed-$B$ phase, two successful inputs with the same $A$ consequently have the same threshold. Each intervening Algorithm 4 would strictly drop it, so all intervening moves preserve the column totals.

**Optimal-flow ties.** Fix $A,p,z$, and two true min-cost flows $q,h$. The signed difference is a circulation in the residual graph of $q$, which decomposes into directed cycles with positive coefficients. Each cycle has nonnegative cost and the total cost difference is zero, so every used cycle has cost zero. If a new reverse arc $t\to i$ is present at $h$ but absent at $q$, a difference cycle contains its forward arc $i\to t$. Removing that arc from the cycle gives a directed path from $t$ to $i$ of exactly the reverse cost. Replacing new reverse arcs by those paths and using symmetry shows that reachability and every shortest distance are independent of the optimal flow. Zero-cost cycles therefore permit arbitrary optimal-flow tie choices in Algorithm 2. A fixed coordinate-order rule can make the implementation deterministic.

**Progress with fixed totals.** In a successful nonterminal increase within the same $B$, write $A'$ for the next signature. Any lost active arc belongs to an unused edge, since positive flow remains a best response. Its forward arc $i\to t$ satisfies $d_{it}\gamma_t>\gamma_i$, so $t$ is reachable from the seed. If $i$ is reachable too, the arc has strictly positive reduced cost $\log d_{it}+\operatorname{dist}_b(i)-\operatorname{dist}_b(t)$. Every shortest path from the seed survives these deletions. Every old path from an ineligible seed to outside $B$ avoids the current seed's reachable set and also survives. Adding newly active arcs only adds possible paths. The old flow is feasible for $A'$, and residual reachability for fixed margins is invariant under a change to any other feasible flow by the same cycle replacement, regardless of cycle cost. Thus the set of eligible seeds can only shrink while $B,z$ stay fixed, and the first eligible seed can only advance in its order.

The optimum flow cost for $A'$ is at most the old cost because the old flow is feasible. If the cost is equal, the old flow is optimal for $A'$ and can be used to compare distances. The old shortest paths from the current seed survive. The step hits a new tie since a cap or budget event would change $B$ or terminate. Its new forward arc has $\operatorname{dist}_b(t)>\operatorname{dist}_b(i)+\log d_{it}$ by the event's positive relative rate, with infinity allowed on the left. Therefore at least one distance strictly decreases, and every distance weakly decreases. The progress proof compares real-valued objective values analytically.

Now suppose a successful input signature repeats in the same $B$ phase. The threshold formula yields fixed column totals on the intervening segment, and the first eligible seed is the same at both ends by residual invariance. Seed monotonicity makes it constant between them. A strict decrease in optimal flow cost or, at constant cost, in a component of the seed's distance vector rules out equal endpoint signatures. This proves non-repetition with a fixed seed order and all exact optimizer ties.

Membership in $B$ changes at most twice per target. An open target leaves only at saturation, since Algorithm 4 lowers all open scores together. At positive threshold, the $n$-dimensional arrangement of pairwise row payoffs determines $A$. The $k$-dimensional arrangement compares $g_{it}=(a_{it}-v_i)/d_{it}$ to one another and to zero at each target. Every positive-coverage target has positive mass, so $x_t=\max(0,\max_i g_{it})$ and the active types are exactly the equalities to $x_t$. The identity also holds at $x_t=0$ by all attack payoff inequalities. Both arrangements have polynomially many hyperplanes. Their sign-cell bounds, including ties, give at most $C=(nk+1)^{O(\min\{n,k\})}$ signatures at positive threshold.

There are at most $(2n+1)C$ positive-threshold successes, by counting the input signatures in each phase. At zero threshold, increasing one seed of zero load keeps all attack maxima fixed and ends at a cap or the budget. There are at most $n$ such calls. Each failed call either brings in a new target or is followed by a success, giving at most $2n$ failures beyond the success count. The total bound $2(2n+1)C+4n$ yields the [B1](B1-classify-run-lengths.md) scaling $T=2^{O(\sqrt{nk}\log(nk+1))}$ for the exact implementation. [Li et al., Lemmas 6–8 and Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=9)

## Bit-length bound and exact LP outputs

Let $b$ bound the binary lengths of slope numerators and denominators, and put $m=n+k$. Any simple residual cycle of true log cost different from zero has product $U/V\ne1$, with integers $U,V\le 2^{mb}$. Its absolute log cost is at least $|U-V|/\max(U,V)\ge 2^{-mb}$. Precompute rational edge costs within $2^{-mb}/(4m)$ of the true logarithms, using opposite costs on reverse arcs. The precision takes polynomial time. Reduce each slope to $2^e u$ with $1\le u<2$ and use the series for $\log u=2\sum_{j\ge0}((u-1)/(u+1))^{2j+1}/(2j+1)$, also at $u=2$ for $\log 2$. Its geometric tail gives the required accuracy with polynomially many rational operations on polynomial-size numbers. Each true negative simple cycle stays negative. Thus a flow minimizing the rational costs is a true log-minimizer by the residual negative-cycle criterion. A true zero-cost cycle may acquire a sign in the surrogate objective, which only selects among true optima. For Algorithm 3, run Bellman–Ford directly with rational products $d_{it}$ and $1/d_{it}$ on arcs, comparing minima by integer cross-products. Each shortest walk can be made simple by removing nonnegative cycles, and at most $m-1$ synchronous passes suffice. Reciprocals of the winning products are the exact rates.

Store the retained $x,q$ with a common denominator $D$. Its initial value can be the product of the prior denominators. Use polynomial-time exact rational LP optimization and request a vertex by fixing the optimal objective and successively minimizing each coordinate on the resulting face in a fixed order. Each successive face is a face of the original polytope, so the optimal values used to fix coordinates have the original vertex bit-length bound. This provides a basic optimum even in degenerate cases. [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=zvmmf&option_lang=eng&paperid=5239&wshow=paper)

1. In Algorithm 2, scaling flow coordinates by $D$ gives integral row and column totals. The transportation constraint matrix is totally unimodular, so every basic optimum after scaling is integral and $D$ still suffices.
2. In Algorithm 3, every candidate winning event time is an affine function of the old coverage. For a catch-up event choose any current maximizer to write $v_i$ as one affine payoff and divide by the nonzero relative rate. Rates, their sums, and the affine map $x'_t=x_t+\gamma_t\Delta$ have coefficients of polynomial bit length independent of $D$. The product of the coefficient denominators in this one map is an integer $H_3$ of polynomial bit length. $DH_3$ suffices for the new state.
3. In Algorithm 4, all coefficients multiplying prospective variables come from the input or are small integers. The old state enters only in the right-hand sides. Multiply each row by a common product $Q$ of input denominators. The basis matrix is integral, and the scaled right-hand sides have denominator dividing $D$, including $Q\lambda$ because $\lambda=\beta_b z_b$ for some $b\in B$. With $M$ an invertible matrix of tight rows at the chosen vertex, Cramer's rule gives the common denominator $D|\det M|$ for the entire solution. Hadamard's bound gives $\log|\det M|=\operatorname{poly}(L)$. This controls the whole optimizer with one factor even when several residual capacities become tight together.

The retained coverages and flow entries lie in $[0,1]$. After $j$ outer calls, their numerator and denominator lengths are $O((j+1)\operatorname{poly}(L))$. Each call costs polynomial time in that length and $L$, hence all $T$ calls cost $T^{O(1)}\operatorname{poly}(L)$. The estimate permits growth with the number of calls. For one fixed lexicographic tie rule on Algorithm 4, L2b bounds the retained numerators and denominators polynomially in the input length alone. It uses tight paths to anchor coverage and a common transportation baseline for each phase. With fraction reduction between calls, $T$ outer calls cost $T\,\mathrm{poly}(L)$ bit time after preprocessing. [Input-only arithmetic bound](L2b-input-only-arithmetic.md)

## Weak slopes and exact replay

A1 replaces each zero gain or slope by a positive rational $\varepsilon=2^{-\operatorname{poly}(L)}$ and preserves $n,k$. Extract the perturbed optimizer's support and coverage endpoint classes by exact comparisons. On the original input, minimize a common violation over that closed pattern. Its coefficient matrix has original input bit size, the perturbed solution witnesses violation at most $\varepsilon$, and the determinant bound in A1 forces the optimal violation to be zero. Repair consequently composes with the continuation's bit bound and returns an original-game equilibrium of polynomial output size. The hypersimplex decomposition in A1 converts marginals to a short explicit defender mixture. [A1, §4](A1-threshold-transport.md#4-transfer-an-exact-positive-coefficient-solver-to-weak-inequalities)

Independently replayed the six states and five events of the [L1 edge-revisit instance](L1-published-algorithm.md#exact-edge-revisit-instance) with Python fractions. For each state, scaling the transportation problem by four makes its vertices unit row assignments. Exhaustive enumeration at column counts $(2,1,1)$ found the unique minimizing assignment by exact slope-product comparisons. Exact shortest-path products agreed with and without the redundant row caps. The computed coverages and active sets matched all six rows of the L1 trace, as did the five recorded rates and step lengths. Edge $(4,C)$ leaves and returns despite constant $B,z$.

The [B1 two-target example](B1-classify-run-lengths.md#how-much-competition-causes-extra-events) supplies two checks of the Algorithm 4 endpoint. At its first failure the movable type has mass $1/4$, and the waiting target's score bound allows $\Delta\le1/2$. The solution $\Delta=1/4$ leaves the waiting target strictly below the new threshold. At the second failure that target starts with score $1/4$ and can gain at most $3/4$ from the newly tied type. The constraint $1/4+\Delta\le3/4-\Delta$ binds first, so $\Delta=1/4$ makes the waiting target join $B$.
