# A4h6 Alternating trees and exact price jumps

A failed Hall test gives a cut on which prices can safely increase after a suitable change of partial flow. The first new tie is computable exactly. The steps give a finite ascent to the minimum, with polynomial bit time for certain slope and margin classes. General continuous ascent can take superpolynomially many steps. A separate Hall shortcut selects the next restricted market. [Directional-auction lower bound](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#lower-bound-for-the-directional-ascending-auctions)

## A direction that preserves a selected partial flow

Use prices and bids above reserve and utilities above outside. At trial \(p\), put \(u_i=\max(0,\max_j(b_{ij}-d_{ij}p_j))\). Maintain a partial flow on demanded edges filling every positive-price good. The invariant holds at zero; residual augmentations to a maximum flow keep full columns full. Outside is unlimited for buyers who demand it.

If any mass is unserved, let \(I,S\) be the buyers and goods reachable from the unserved rows in the residual graph. The buyers in \(I\) are precisely those whose demands are contained in \(S\). All reached goods are full and receive flow only from \(I\). The total defect is \(D=m(I)-c(S)>0\). The residual cut is the inclusion-minimal maximizer of \(f_p(T)-c(T)\), where \(f_p(T)\) counts buyers trapped in \(T\). In particular, for every nonempty \(R\subseteq S\),

\[
 m\{i\in I:D_i(p)\cap R\ne\varnothing\}=m(I)-f_p(S\setminus R)>c(R).
\]

Choose a common quantity quantum \(q\) and let \(\delta=q/(2n)\), where \(n\) is the number of goods. The strict inequalities have margin at least \(q\), so a transportation flow with row caps \(m_i\) fills all columns in \(S\) up to \(c_j+\delta\) on current demand. Minimize \(\sum_{ij}x_{ij}\log d_{ij}\) in that transportation problem. Its dual has \(a_i\le0\) and \(a_i+b'_j\le\log d_{ij}\). Set \(v_j=\exp(-b'_j)\) on \(S\) and \(z_i=\min_{j\in D_i(p)}d_{ij}v_j\) on \(I\), with rates zero elsewhere. Every used edge attains that minimum.

Each positive-flow component containing goods has a slack row. Otherwise its full buyer mass would equal \(c(R)+|R|\delta\) for a nonempty set \(R\) of goods, impossible on the \(q\)-grid. Slack rows anchor \(a_i=0\), so positive edges fix the whole dual. Compute the speeds as rational products of slopes along the support from these anchors. The optimal transportation flow can be found by adding a dummy column for unused row mass and approximating \(\log d_{ij}\) finely enough to preserve all nonzero simple-cycle cost signs. Bounded-bit rational slope products give the required gap. [A4c rational log objectives](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#a-polynomial-local-search-on-fractional-transportation-supports)

Truncate the augmented columns to \(c_j\) and keep the old allocation outside \(I,S\). The resulting partial flow still has maximum volume. Increase each price at rate \(v_j\) and lower each utility at rate \(z_i\). Used edges stay tight. Other old tight edges have nonnegative slack derivative. Buyers outside \(I\) retain a static best option. The first new tie is obtained by taking the smallest positive ratio

\[
 \frac{u_i+d_{ij}p_j-b_{ij}}{z_i-d_{ij}v_j}
\]

over currently slack edges with positive denominator, including outside with slope zero and slack \(u_i\). The jump is finite because every trapped buyer strictly prefers real goods and her utility decreases at a positive rate.

If \(p\le p^*\), the jump stays below \(p^*\). At a hypothetical first contact strictly before the next tie, let \(R\) be the moved coordinates that have reached \(p^*\). Buyers in \(I\) touching \(R\) have mass at least \(c(R)+|R|\delta\), by the surviving augmented flow. Their offers outside \(S\) are still strictly worse. At \(p^*\) the offers in \(R\) stay fixed and the offers on \(S\setminus R\) strictly worsen. All those buyers would have to buy in \(R\), violating capacity. The endpoint follows by continuity.

### A forest can be immovable and a new tie can be internal

At zero prices let two buyers of mass \(3/2\) each bid 10 on both unit-capacity goods, with slopes \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). The crossed flow sends one unit from each buyer to her slope-2 good. Its support is a forest. Keeping it tight requires \(2v_2\le v_1\) and \(2v_1\le v_2\), so the only nonnegative speed is zero. Changing to the diagonal flow allows a rise.

For an event without expansion, take two unit goods \(j,k\) and three unit buyers. Their bid and slope pairs are R \((10,10),(2,1)\), A \((10,9),(2,1/2)\), and B \((0,12),(1,1)\). At zero prices assign A to \(j\) and B to \(k\), leaving R unserved. The tree already reaches everyone. Along \(p=(t,2t)\), A's edge to \(k\) first ties at \(t=1\). Neither the reached vertices nor the maximum flow grows. [Oki–Sato, Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10563v3#App6)

## Finite progress and a polynomial subclass

In the interior of a jump, \(I\) stays trapped and the supported partial flow keeps defect \(D\). The surviving augmented flow gives the strict lower Hall property on every nonempty subset of \(S\). Hence \(S\) remains the minimal maximizing cut throughout the open segment. The interior edges persist at the endpoint. If the maximum volume stays unchanged, the reachable sets \(S,I\) can therefore only grow. There are finitely many defect values on the \(q\)-grid.

While \(D,S,I\) stay fixed, the old augmented flow is feasible at the new price. The minimum log cost is nonincreasing and has finitely many possible values. During a sequence of equal costs, the old flow remains optimal, so its slack anchors pin the same unique dual. Every old minimum-rate edge is still available at the endpoint. The event adds an edge of strictly smaller rate for some buyer and no buyer's minimum rate increases. There are only finitely many such decreases for fixed speeds. This proves termination and, by no overshoot, exact minimum prices.

Normalize slopes on a viable spanning forest to 1 by positive row and column scalings. Let \(h\) be the multiplicative rank of the resulting slopes, the dimension of the span of their prime-exponent vectors. Let \(P,G\) count distinct buyer masses and good capacities and let \(N\) count vertices. The ascent takes polynomial bit time for fixed \(h,P,G\). This is a promise on the data and requires no factorization of the labels by the algorithm.

A vertex augmented flow has a forest support, and every component has exactly one slack row. Two slack rows would allow a perturbation on the path between them. Cutting a tree edge away from its slack root expresses that load as a signed sum of original margins and \(\delta\), with coefficients of magnitude at most \(N\). Grouping the log objective by these \(P+G+1\) possible quantities gives one log product per quantity. Each product's prime exponents have magnitude at most \(N^2B\) if normalized labels have at most \(B\) bits. Projection onto some \(h\) prime coordinates is injective on their span, giving at most \((2N^2B+1)^{h(P+G+1)}\) possible costs. There are at most \((N+1)^{P+G}\) defect values and at most \(N+1\) successive reached-set enlargements per defect. Equal-cost steps are bounded by the edge count. Alternatively, polynomial total mass in units of \(q\) and fixed rank suffice by counting products using the common augmented grid \(q/(2n)\).

Each jump uses ordinary flow, log-cost transportation and exact rational comparisons. Given a common denominator of the trial prices, the next denominator divides it times a factor of polynomial bit length. Speeds come from original data and the first-tie computation divides one current slack by a bounded-bit rate difference. Therefore bit length grows at most additively by a polynomial per jump.

## General lower bounds and a nonlocal shortcut

The B3f market bounds any continuous piecewise-affine price path from reserve to clearing with a supported partial flow filling all positive-price goods. It forces superpolynomially many assignment regimes even with unit masses and capacities two. The preceding ascent has this invariant. [Forced assignment regimes](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#why-the-auction-must-pass-through-the-forced-assignments)

Even with unit slopes, ordinary tight augmentations can take \(F=2^k\) stages at binary capacities. Use the A4h counting network with marginal costs \(1,3,\ldots,2F-1\), goods for vertices, and one buyer of mass \(U_e\) per arc \(e=(u,v)\), with bids \(L,L-\kappa_e\) at tail and head. Nonsink capacities are outgoing capacity sums, and the sink capacity is \(F\). Preload each arc buyer at the tail and insert an extra source-only buyer of mass \(F\). In a tight augmentation preserve the regular full rows and full nonsink columns. The amount bought at each head defines a network flow of throughput \(y\), the extra amount served. The tight choices give residual reduced-cost inequalities with potentials \(p\). Every supported throughput is minimum-cost and two supported throughputs at the same prices satisfy \(C(y')-C(y)=(p_s-p_t)(y'-y)\). While the sink is slack its price stays zero. A fixed-price blocking augmentation therefore gains at most the length of one unit interval. Take \(L\) and the source-only bid larger than \(2F+(|V|+2)\max_e|\kappa_e|+1\). Before clearing, reaching the slack sink requires a residual shortest-path cost at most \(2F-1\), and each reached price differs from \(p_s\) by a simple residual path cost, so outside does not interrupt these augmentations. Scaling margins by \(1/F\) makes each augmentation at most \(1/F\) of a unit extra row.

For the compressed ascent above on this unit-slope instance, the unique log dual is zero and rates are one on the reached set. The reached cut is independent of the choice of maximum flow. A canonical maximum flow keeping all regular rows full can therefore be carried along as a witness, using tight residual augmentations to advance \(y\). It yields the same reached sets and price jumps and forces the same distinct marginal prices. An assignment LP solves the supplied instance directly. [Counting network](A4h-complete-candidate-generation.md#a-single-boundary-table-and-a-two-dimensional-load-hull)

### Exponentially many events at defect one

The counting network also separates internal events from augmentations. Add a return arc \(t\to s\) of capacity \(F\). Double each arc buyer's mass and each outgoing-capacity sum used for a good. For the return buyer use offers \(L-p_t,L-p_s/2\), with the other arc buyers as before. The only extra buyer has unit mass and the single offer \(B-p_s\). Take \(B>2|V|\max_e\kappa_e+4F+1\) and \(L>B+(|V|+2)\max_e\kappa_e+1\). At zero prices, all arc buyers can buy from their tails, filling every good. There is a clearing witness with \(p_s=B\): take a minimum-cost flow of value \(F\), support its regular arc purchases by residual potentials shifted to \(B\) at \(s\), use the return buyer entirely at \(s\), and send the extra buyer outside. The residual potentials give \(p_t\ge B/2\) and keep every arc buyer strictly above outside at this witness.

Along a flow-preserving ascent bounded by the minimum, the witness bounds every price above, so every arc buyer remains strictly above outside. While \(p_s<B\), the extra buyer also strictly demands real goods. Total real demand is then total capacity plus one. All columns remain full by the invariant, so the maximum volume is constant and its defect is one. The minimum must reach \(p_s=B\). At any earlier trial, for any goods set, the arc buyers touching it have an even total mass and the extra buyer can add at most one. Since the capacity is even and the set can fill, the arc buyers alone have enough mass to fill it. Thus ordinary lower Hall gives a full core allocation at every trial, using all arc buyers.

Divide that allocation by two and interpret head purchases as an ordinary circulation, with throughput \(y\) on the return. The residual tightness inequalities say that it minimizes \(C(y)-(p_s/2)y\), since the return's effective cost is \(-p_s/2\). For \(2(2j-1)<p_s<2(2j+1)\), \(j=1,\ldots,F-1\), the unique minimizing throughput is \(j\). One fixed demand graph cannot support different unique throughputs, so a first-tie ascent visits \(\Omega(F)\) distinct graphs at the **same defect**, using only slopes \(1,1/2\). The input has \(O(k^2)\) bits. This rules out bounding internal events by augmentation count or by slope rank alone. Replacing the exceptional slope by \(1-\eta\) makes the effective cost \(-\eta p_s\). Choosing \(\eta B>|V|\max_e\kappa_e+2F\) and increasing \(L\) accordingly gives the same result even with \(\eta=1/(4|V|)\), where A4a solves the market directly.

### Hall cuts select a restricted market

For a different use of a Hall failure, maintain a set \(A\) certified above reserve at the original minimum. Pin all other goods at reserve, fold their offers into outside, and solve only the conditional market on \(A\). Its reinserted minimum \(q^A\) lies below \(p^*\) because the strengthened outside options make the original \(p^*_A\) buyer-feasible. If a full Hall test passes, domination gives equality. Otherwise take a minimal maximizing deficient cut \(S\). Each coordinate of \(S\) is strictly below \(p^*\). If \(T\subsetneq S\) were the set of its rising coordinates, the buyers trapped in \(S\) whose demand meets \(S\setminus T\) would have mass \(f(S)-f(T)>c(S)-c(T)\) and would all require the unchanged goods at \(p^*\). At least one good of \(S\) is outside \(A\), since the restricted solve passes every Hall cut contained in \(A\). Add \(S\setminus A\) and repeat.

This takes at most \(1+|\{j:p_j^*>0\}|\) restricted solves of polynomial encoding length. It is polynomial when the unknown above-reserve set has bounded size, or when its slopes satisfy a tractable condition inherited by deletion and higher outside options, such as factorization or A4a's column bounds. Slopes on goods that stay at reserve can be arbitrary. In the general case the restricted solve can grow to the full market. [A4a slope conditions](A4a-near-factorized-slopes.md)
