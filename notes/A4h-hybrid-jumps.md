# A4h9 Combining price jumps with certified relations

Price ascent supplies lower bounds and comparison events. Hall tests and temporary grouping can turn those events into new information about the minimum. Certified bounds can then change the remaining market by raising floors, pinning coordinates, or grouping goods. This gives exact shortcuts and new structural cases. A general polynomial-time bound on finding a useful certificate remains open. ## A tie during ascent can disappear

Use two unit goods and three unit buyers. The offers of A are $4-p_1,3-p_2$, B's are $4-p_1,5-4p_2$, and C has only $2-p_1$, with zero reserves and outside utilities. The exact A4h6 jump sequence is

$$
 (0,0),\quad (1,0),\quad (5/3,2/3),\quad (2,3/4).
$$

Initially the first good is overdemanded by A and C. Raising only its price first ties A. At $(1,0)$, the augmented log-cost flow with columns $5/4$ each uses $1/4$ of A at good 1, $3/4$ of A at good 2, one unit of C at good 1, and $1/2$ of B at good 2. The rates at the two goods are equal until B ties. At $(5/3,2/3)$, log minimization gives A's whole unit to good 2 and leaves $1/4$ of B there. The direction now raises the first price four times as quickly as the second. A loses her earlier tie, and C next ties outside at $(2,3/4)$. At that endpoint assign A to good 2, B to good 1 and C outside. The two positive singleton goods have touching masses two and two, and their union has mass three. Strict Hall certifies the exact minimum. This also shows that an edge used in the augmented flow immediately after a jump may cease to be tight. ## Relaxations that screen a proposed relation

Keep a rational covering polyhedron containing $(p^*,u^*)$. It can include price bounds, previously certified equalities and reserve or utility pins. To test one strict side of a proposed affine equality, retain each buyer option that can be tight somewhere on that side. Testing this takes one LP per option: impose its tight equality and maximize the signed gap of the proposed relation. Include an outside edge when $u_i=0$ passes the same test. For each good, a test with its price at reserve determines whether it must sell out on the given side. Empty strict sides can be rejected directly.

Run an unweighted flow on the resulting possible graph with the original margins. One can also test the supply-enlarged market on the same price hypothesis. A4c ensures the same $p^*$ clears both, and requires $c_j+\delta$ sales at positive-priced goods in the enlarged market. If either flow is impossible, the strict side is impossible. Rejecting both sides certifies the relation. This certifies an affine relation needed for grouping even if the compared offers are never used. To certify that both are best, test their two cover slacks. The union used in these tests contains every demand graph compatible with the hypothesis. [A4c supply increase](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#one-call-at-enlarged-capacities)

There are polynomially many pairwise offer comparisons. One may prioritize events or scan the whole list. Repeat at a fixed input box after each new independent equality. At most the original number of variables many rank increases occur, and the LPs in original coordinates use the original comparison coefficients and the supplied bounds. This gives polynomial work for closure under the stated tests. A side can still pass because its individually possible edges need not be simultaneously demanded.

The test resolves A4h7's two-buyer example. There the certified box is $[0,1]\times[1,2]$, the two unit buyers have offers $(4-p_1,5-p_2)$ and $(4-p_1,7-2p_2)$, and both goods have unit capacity. If the first buyer strictly prefers good 1, only one unit can ever fill positive good 2, below its enlarged capacity $5/4$. If the first buyer strictly prefers good 2, then $p_2<p_1+1$ makes the second strictly prefer it as well, and two units cannot fit. The equality $p_2=p_1+1$ follows. The enlarged market also certifies $p_1=0$, since both positive-price goods would otherwise need sales totaling $5/2$.

One other flow test pins coordinates directly. At a lower point $\ell\le p^*$, if $S=\{j:\ell_j<p_j^*\}$ is nonempty, every buyer touching $S$ at $p^*$ is trapped there at $\ell$. Strict Hall at $p^*$ gives $f_\ell(S)>c(S)$. Hence a good belonging to no deficient set at $\ell$ is already at its exact price. Check each good by maximizing $f_\ell(T)-c(T)$ over sets containing it, using a closure cut. Known rising goods can be required and known pins excluded. If a buyer is currently tied between a pinned good and a rising one, the pinned offer keeps her current utility and the rising offer becomes strictly worse. Ties between two pinned demanded goods persist. [Strict Hall certificate](A4-minimum-price-markets.md#certifying-a-candidate-minimum-price)

## One temporary grouping decides the sign exactly

For a proposed line $p_j=A+Bp_k$ with $B>0$, let $q$ be a common quantity unit of the original masses and capacities and let $n$ be the number of goods. Enlarge all capacities by $\delta=q/(2n)$ as in A4c. The resulting market has the unique clearing price $p^*$. Use this same enlarged market throughout the sign test.

Temporarily aggregate $j,k$, using the maximum of their offers on the line, their summed enlarged capacity, and the group reserve enforcing both physical reserves. Take **any** clearing outcome of this smaller market. Lift its prices to a physical vector $v$. If a physical flow clears the enlarged capacities at $v$, then $v=p^*$ and the line is certified. Otherwise distribute the grouped purchases arbitrarily among individually best aliases. This makes $v$ clear a modified enlarged market whose only capacity change is a transfer $\tau$ into $j$ and out of $k$. At the group reserve, assign any unsold aggregate capacity to a physical member at reserve. This preserves full sales at every positive-priced alias.

Suppose $\tau>0$. If $S=\{h:v_h<p_h^*\}$ is nonempty, every buyer touching $S$ at $p^*$ is trapped there at $v$. The strict lower Hall inequality in the original market has a gap of at least one quantity unit. The total supply increase is smaller than that gap, giving $h_{p^*}(S)>c^+(S)$, where $c^+$ is enlarged capacity. Therefore $S$ must contain $j$ and exclude $k$. If $T=\{h:v_h>p_h^*\}$ is nonempty, every good in $T$ sells out at $v$. Its buyers are trapped in $T$ at $p^*$, where $f_{p^*}(T)<c^+(T)$. Hence $T$ must contain $k$ and exclude $j$. For the upper inequality, original feasibility gives $f_{p^*}(T)\le c(T)$, and the supply increase is positive on $T$. As $v\ne p^*$, at least one of the resulting coordinate bounds is strict:

$$
 p_j^*\ge v_j,\qquad p_k^*\le v_k,\qquad p_j^*> A+Bp_k^* .
$$

A negative transfer reverses the bounds and the sign. These deductions hold for any split, so a split of the wrong sign cannot occur. A zero transfer would itself give physical clearance. This provides a certified answer and new coordinate bounds even when there is no tie to contract. It also works after raising certified floors and shifting the original market accordingly. [Supply perturbation and Hall gaps](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#one-call-at-enlarged-capacities) · [Grouped offers](A4h-forced-price-relations.md#exact-contraction-after-certification)

In the transient example, test A's line $p_1=1+p_2$. The one-group market with total capacity $5/2$ clears at $(p_1,p_2)=(2,1)$. B strictly uses good 1, and C must supply half a unit there. Thus every split puts at least $3/2$ on good 1 against enlarged capacity $5/4$. The test certifies $p_1^*-p_2^*>1$ and gives the safe bounds $p_1^*\ge2,\ p_2^*\le1$. Testing B's line $p_1=4p_2-1$ returns $(2,3/4)$ with physical flows A2 $=1$, B1 $=3/4$, B2 $=1/4$, C1 $=1/2$, and C outside $=1/2$.

The exact test costs one grouped clearing solve on one fewer good plus flow checks. With an ordinary affine-market solver, a merged pair has at most one envelope switch per buyer. Try each of the resulting at most $m+1$ intervals in the group price, extending each active affine offer to the whole price line. The grouped enlarged capacities are grid capacities plus positive increments with sum below $q$, so the A4c proof makes the clearing price unique for each affine extension. The extension containing a grouped clearing price therefore recovers it. Check interval membership and include end ties. This gives polynomial overhead in calls to markets on one fewer good. There is no general polynomial total bound for recursively solving those smaller markets.

## Safe floors, outside utilities, and physical repair

If $r\le\ell\le p^*$ is a certified lower bound, raise reserves to $\ell$ and restart there. One may simultaneously raise buyer $i$'s outside utility to any $w_i$ between her original outside and $u_i^*$. For example a certified upper price vector $U$ gives $w_i=\max(o_i,\max_j v_{ij}(U_j))$. The old allocation clears at $p^*$ under these changes. Someone who used the old outside has $u_i^*=o_i=w_i$. All old best real goods remain best. Every good above its new reserve was above its old reserve, so the original strict Hall inequalities still certify $p^*$ as minimum. The new viable graph retains only edges with $v_{ij}(\ell_j)>w_i$. Cases where this pruning exposes an already tractable class are solvable by restarting in that class and taking one original clearing flow at the end.

For example, if buyer $i$'s exact utility is certified, lift every good's reserve to at least her indifference price. She can trade only at newly reserved goods and touches no above-reserve cut at $p^*$. Drop her from the reduced price search. If a good's exact price is certified, fold that good into the remaining buyers' outside options and drop its column. In both cases final recovery uses the original margins. This permits repeated deletion of pinned rows and columns. A buyer too large to place her entire mass on viable goods must take outside. A good whose capacity covers all its possible buyer mass is at reserve at the minimum. These tests can start the deletions without an equilibrium oracle.

To keep the **old** physical invariant at a proposed floor $\ell$, require a partial demand flow that fills every good priced above its original reserve. This is one ordinary flow test and can fail for a true relation. Use one buyer of mass 3, capacities $(3/2,3/2,1/2)$, and offers $10-2p_1,11-p_2,12-p_3$. The minimum is $(0,1,2)$, with all three offers equal and loads $(1,3/2,1/2)$. Strict Hall holds on every positive subset. The buyer must use goods 1 and 2 in every clearing since neither can be omitted within the other capacities, giving $p_2=1+2p_1$. At the propagated point $(0,1,0)$, only good 3 is demanded, so good 2 cannot sell out. Raising the reserve of good 2 to 1 makes a restart valid. A single jump of good 3 to 2 then gives the minimum. Physical support can also fail in the entire open segment between two supported endpoints, as it does on $(0,t,2t)$, $0<t<1$.

A failed saturation test can feed a new lower price bound. To test $p_k^*\le t$, a possible purchaser of positive good $j$ must satisfy $v_{ij}(\ell_j)\ge\max(w_i,v_{ik}(t))$. Try filling certified positive goods to the enlarged capacities on this relaxation. As $t$ rises its graph only gains edges, at explicitly computable pairwise thresholds. A flow search therefore bounds $p_k^*$ in polynomial bit time. In the one-buyer example the positive second good requires $p_3^*\ge2$. The test can also use a whole competitor group with a shared price parameter. Independent scalar tests can stall. For example, use capacities $(3,1,3)$, prices bounded between $(1/2,1,1/2)$ and $(4,3,4)$, and three buyers of masses $2,2,4$ with offers $(4-p_a,3-p_b)$, $(4-p_c,3-p_b)$, and $(4-p_a,4-p_c)$, respectively. The upper corner is the minimum by strict Hall, while the lower corner has no support on $b$. Fixing any single coordinate to its lower value leaves enough individually possible demand to pass even the full enlarged flow. For example, fix $a$ and enlarge by $\delta=1/6$. Assign A's two units to $a$, C's $1+\delta,3-\delta$ to $a,c$, and B's $1+\delta,2\delta$ to $b,c$, with B's remainder outside. Fixing $c$ is symmetric. At fixed $b$, each of A and B can take $(1+\delta)/2$ at $b$, then spend the rest at her other good, with C filling the remainder.

### The greatest physical restart in a price box

There is an exact dual test if retaining the original physical invariant matters. Normalize original reserves and outside utilities to zero and let $L$ be the upper box corner for the proposed restart. Put $w_i=\max(0,\max_j(b_{ij}-d_{ij}L_j))$. In the reciprocal transpose set buyer-$i$-as-good reserves to $w_i$ and compute its least prices $u^\circ$. Then the greatest supported physical vector under $L$ has

$$
 q_j=\max\{0,\max_i(b_{ij}-u_i^\circ)/d_{ij}\}\quad(c_j>0),\qquad
 q_j=L_j\quad(c_j=0).
$$

For any supported physical $p\le L$, its ordinary buyer utilities are at least $w$ and are buyer-feasible prices in the transpose. Transpose its partial flow and fill missing mass from original reserve-priced goods into the transpose outside. For positive-capacity goods the transposed indirect utility is exactly $p_j$, by physical full sales at positive $p_j$ and cover at zero. Thus $u^\circ\le u(p)$ implies $q\ge p$. Conversely a transpose clearing flow at $u^\circ$ supports the stated $q$ and sells out each positive physical column. Raising a zero-capacity column to its cutoff can only weaken those offers. The definition of $w$ gives $q\le L$. [Reciprocal transpose](A4e-covering-lp.md#factorized-slopes-and-repeated-covering-solves)

Computing this greatest repair in general is already an exact market search problem. Enlarge any input market's supplies so that its unique clearing price is its requested original minimum, and take a common ceiling $H$ for outside indifference. All physical partial-flow prices are at most $H$, and the greatest one obtained by the transpose is clearing, because the transpose reserves $w_i$ at $L=H\mathbf1$ are zero. To make this a certified lower box in a larger market, add a good $h$ of capacity one and, for every old good $j$, a guard of mass $c_j^++2$ offering $P-p_j$ and $K-p_h$, where $P=H+1$ and $K=P+1$. The new minimum is explicitly $(P,\ldots,P,K)$. The guards fill every good and leave excess outside there, and strict Hall holds on every nonempty goods set. Under $L=(H,\ldots,H,0)$ the guards strictly demand only $h$, at reserve. Thus supported prices under this certified cutoff are exactly the old supported vectors with $p_h=0$, and computing the greatest one returns the arbitrary original market answer.

### Capacity evidence can replace an entire sequence of price events

In the constant-defect lower-bound family of A4h6, a minimum-cost flow of maximum throughput gives an upper witness with $p_s=B$ and all arc buyers strictly above outside. They are therefore real buyers at $p^*$. Their total mass already equals total capacity, so the added unit buyer has to take outside. That buyer's only offer $B-p_s$ forces $p_s^*\ge B$. The witness provides the reverse bound. Pinning $p_s^*=B$ and folding those offers into outside removes the exceptional slope. A unit-slope conditional solve and one original flow finish in polynomial bit time, bypassing the family’s superpolynomial ascent. [Constant-defect family](A4h-alternating-tree.md#exponentially-many-events-at-defect-one)
