# B3g. Independent audit of the fixed-flow lower bound

**Verdict.** A rational one-resource instance family forces $n^{\Omega(\log n)}$ distinct attack matrices on disjoint intermediate budget intervals. Every successful coverage step of the published continuation holds the attack matrix fixed. The same count applies to B3a's greatest fixed-flow jumps. The theorem concerns the number of positive-budget steps taken on the way to an exact equilibrium at budget one. [Li, Conitzer and Korzhyk, §6.2, Algorithms 1–4 and Lemma 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=7) · [B3a](B3a-fixed-flow-jump.md) · [Audited construction](B3f-forced-flow-switches.md)

**Source and assignment.** With source parameter $h$, depth $m=\lfloor\log_2 h\rfloor$, $B=1,D=0$, Lemma 14(ii),(iii),(vi) supplies $K=h^m$ distinct winners, each beating *every other path to the last layer* by at least one across its entire interval. Attaching a common sink by zero edges preserves that gap. Subdivision distinguishes parallel arcs if needed. Use a row and column for each vertex. Give the sink row a single permitted entry returning to the source. Permit DAG arcs and zero-weight diagonals at internal vertices. Each permitted permutation consists of that return, a complete source–sink path, and diagonals, since every other nontrivial cycle would be a DAG cycle. Negate the path weights to maximize. If permitted scores have magnitude at most $H\ge1$ on the counted range, assigning score $-(2n+1)H$ to every missing entry makes each forbidden permutation score at most $-(n+2)H$, versus at least $-nH$ for every permitted one. [Gajjar and Radhakrishnan, §§3.1–3.2 and proof of Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05115#page=12)

**Uniformity and the gap.** Write the rescaled assignment score as $B_{it}-C_{it}s$, $s=b/n$. Take $M\ge\max(1,\max_{it}(|B_{it}|+|C_{it}|))$ for the dense table and $\varepsilon=1/(64nM^2)$. Each type's utility is $-x_t+\varepsilon(B_{it}-C_{it}x_t)$. At coverage spread exceeding $2\varepsilon M$, every type strictly prefers a least-covered target to every most-covered target. The latter receive zero mass. A positive-mass target has less coverage than the maximum and therefore room to receive coverage. Transferring a little coverage there from a maximally covered target strictly improves defender utility. Hence every equilibrium on $1/4\le b\le1$ has $|x_t-s|\le2\varepsilon M$, which puts all targets strictly inside the box for $n\ge2$. Pairwise feasible transfers force uniform target mass. B3f's choice $M=(2n+1)H$ bounds the sum of the two absolute coefficients on every pair, including missing pairs.

Now $nq$ is doubly stochastic. Every permutation in a Birkhoff decomposition uses best-response edges. Put $y_t=(x_t-s)/\varepsilon$. Summing utilities along a permutation and removing common terms gives its rescaled assignment score minus $\varepsilon\sum_i C_{i,\pi(i)}y_{\pi(i)}$. The $\sum_i y_{\pi(i)}$ term cancels across permutations. The error in a comparison is at most $4n\varepsilon M^2<1$. A rowwise best permutation must therefore equal the unique assignment winner on each witness interval. All Birkhoff terms coincide, proving uniqueness of the whole attack matrix even in the presence of row ties.

**Encoding and endpoint.** The original rational coefficients have $O((\log h)^3)$ bits each. The integer interval endpoints have $O((\log h)^2)$ bits. Rescaling their containing range affinely, forming B3f's ceiling bound $H$ and its integer multiple $M$, and using the stated rational $\varepsilon$ preserves $O((\log h)^3+\log n)$ bits per payoff. Polynomial size subdivision and optional padding give $h\le n\le h^{O(1)}$. The dense explicit length $L=O(n^2(\log n)^3)$ therefore gives $K=L^{\Omega(\log L)}$. B3f maps the endpoints of the budget range to the first and last witness midpoints, preserving positive length for each witness and forcing the last permutation even at budget one. Hence one assignment solve at $s=1/n$ identifies that permutation. A rational feasibility LP enforcing its row comparisons, budget, and box recovers an exact equilibrium coverage because existence and the forcing argument guarantee feasibility. At one resource these coverages give the defender's probabilities directly.

**Counting.** Positive-budget steps cover the entire budget range. Within a published step the attack matrix stays fixed and row comparisons hold throughout by linearity. B3a's LP segment has the same property. A finite run must cover a positive-length subset of each witness interval with some positive step, and distinct intervals require different steps. This accounts for every legal ordering, tie choice, and greatest fixed-flow jump. A method allowed to compute a distant budget's equilibrium with a different flow can skip intervals. The count gives no lower bound for that operation. Fractional budgets are intermediate states of the continuation on an integer one-resource input.

**Log costs and cycles.** For any two successive witnesses separated by $\Delta s$, the two unit gaps in opposite directions imply $C_{\rm old}-C_{\rm new}\ge2/\Delta s\ge 8n/3$ for the respective sums of assignment loss coefficients. Replacing each log by its linear term gives total comparison error at most $2n\varepsilon^2M^2=\varepsilon/32$. Thus each successive forced flow has strictly lower log cost. B3a's cycle lemma counts a different permanently lost negative tight cycle for each pair of consecutive greatest jumps at fixed margins and boundary. All but at most two of the counted steps lie fully within this single phase, so its $K-O(1)$ cycle corollary follows. [B3a's cycle lemma](B3a-fixed-flow-jump.md#the-cycle-consumed-at-fixed-margins)

**Exact check.** For vertices $0,1,2$, give arc $0\to1$ score $3-12s$, arcs $1\to2,0\to2,2\to0$ and diagonals at $0,1$ score zero, and all other entries score $-25$. With $\varepsilon=2^{-18}$, exact rational vertex enumeration of all six permutations' row-comparison polytopes found only $(1,2,0)$ feasible at $b=1/2$ and only $(2,1,0)$ at $b=1$. The tuples list the attacked columns in row order.
