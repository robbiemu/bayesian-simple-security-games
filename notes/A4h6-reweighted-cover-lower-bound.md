# A4h6. Exponential reweighted-cover descent at Hall defect one

**Theorem.** On the A4h6 counting-network family, the exact A4e reweighted-cover descent from zero can require exponentially many covering solves. The bound applies to every choice of frozen assignment dual and every choice of optimal cover vertex. For depth \(k\), put \(F=2^k\) and let the sole exceptional slope be \(1-1/(2m)\), with \(m\) a positive integer. The number \(T\) of reweighted solves through the first clearing prices obeys

\[
  \left\lceil\frac{F+1}{m+1}\right\rceil\le T\le
  \left\lfloor\frac Fm\right\rfloor+1 .
\]

Both bounds are attainable. The cover optimum with componentwise least prices realizes the upper bound. The case \(m=1\) is the original defect-one family, and choosing \(m=2n\) for \(n\) goods puts the same exponential lower bound inside the polynomial-time near-factorized class. ## The market and its frozen transportation problems

Use the acyclic counting network with source \(s\), sink \(t\), regular arc capacities \(U_e\), nonnegative costs \(\kappa_e\le K\), and least flow cost \(C(y)\) at throughput \(y\in[0,F]\). Its slopes on the successive unit intervals are \(1,3,\ldots,2F-1\). Add the return \(t\to s\) of capacity \(F\). Each arc gives a core buyer of twice its capacity, each good has twice its outgoing capacity sum, and \(M\) denotes the resulting total core mass and stock. For regular arc \(e=(v,w)\) the buyer's offers are \(L-p_v\) and \(L-\kappa_e-p_w\). The return buyer has offers \(L-p_t\) and \(L-(1-\eta)p_s\), where \(\eta=1/(2m)\). There is one extra unit buyer with sole real offer \(B-p_s\). Use zero reserves and outside utilities, \(\eta B>2F-1\) and \(L>B+(n+2)K+1\). The choice \(B=(2F+1)/\eta\) satisfies the required bound for every \(m\). [Counting-network marginal costs](A4h-complete-candidate-generation.md#a-single-boundary-table-and-a-two-dimensional-load-hull)

At trial source quote \(\lambda=p_s\), only the return buyer's bid at \(s\) changes in the frozen unit-slope assignment. It becomes \(L+\eta\lambda\). A full core assignment that fills all goods corresponds, after division by two, to a circulation with return flow \(y\). The amount delivered to an arc's head is its network flow. Maximizing total frozen bid over these assignments gives

\[
  LM+2V(\lambda),\qquad V(\lambda)=\max_{0\le y\le F}\{\eta\lambda y-C(y)\}.
\]

For \(0\le\lambda\le B\), an optimal circulation admits supporting quotes \(p_s=\lambda\) and \(p_v\in[(1-\eta)\lambda,\lambda]\). To see this, minimize \(\sum_e\kappa_e y_e-\eta\lambda y\) on the regular network with internal balances and arc bounds. Its residual price inequalities in difference variables \(a_v=\lambda-p_v\) have \(a_s=0\), \(a_t=\eta\lambda\), \(a_w-a_v\le\kappa_e\) if the tail is used, and \(a_w-a_v\ge\kappa_e\) if the head is used. They follow from LP duality with the source and sink coefficients fixed by the throughput term. Clip each \(a_v\) into \([0,\eta\lambda]\). This preserves the upper differences since the costs are nonnegative. Every arc with positive head flow lies on a positive-flow \(s\)-to-\(t\) path. Along that path the original differences are nonnegative, so the endpoints of that arc were already in the interval and its lower difference is preserved. The return buyer is indifferent between her two frozen offers at these quotes. Core buyers have positive utility because \(L>\lambda\).

Shift these quotes for the same frozen bids by \(B-\lambda+\epsilon\) for \(0<\epsilon<L-B\). The core preferences are preserved, every good has a positive price, and the extra buyer strictly prefers outside. Complementary slackness with this frozen dual shows that **every** frozen optimum sells out to the core and sends the extra buyer outside. With any frozen optimal dual \(q\), that buyer has zero utility and \(q_s\ge B\). Thus for \(\lambda<B\) the single nonzero coefficient in A4e's logarithmic tie-break is \(-\log(1-\eta)>0\) on the return buyer's head purchase. It chooses the largest maximizing throughput \(z\) of \(V(\lambda)\).

## Project the next reweighted cover onto the source price

The A4e weight on a good is \(c_j+\sum_i(d_{ij}-1)x_{ij}\). At frozen throughput \(z\), this changes only the source coefficient, by \(-2\eta z\). At a proposed new quote \(\lambda\), sum the true cover inequalities against a full core assignment realizing an optimal circulation for \(V(\lambda)\), and use the extra buyer's maximum utility bound. The reweighted cover objective is at least

\[
  LM+2V(\lambda)+(B-\lambda)_+-2\eta z\lambda .
\]

This lower bound is attained for every \(0\le\lambda\le B\) by the supporting quotes just constructed before their uniform shift. With \(p_s=\lambda\), the true and frozen offers agree at those quotes, so the inequalities summed against the core are all tight. For \(\lambda>B\) the displayed lower bound increases strictly when \(z<F\), since then \(V\) has slope \(\eta F\). Therefore for \(z<F\) the cover minimizers project exactly onto the minimizers of the displayed function on \([0,B]\). At \(z=F\) the lower bound is constant from \(B\) onward and minimized there. The frozen flow of throughput \(F\) with the extra buyer outside has zero A4e gap at a cover optimum with \(\lambda=B\), so the same flow has zero gap at every reweighted optimum when \(z=F\).

Every optimum attained with \(\lambda<B\) fails the original physical clearance test. Its equality in the projected lower bound means it supports the full core allocation used to attain that bound. On each positive-flow regular path from \(s\), tight head purchases imply that prices weakly decrease, so all vertices of such paths have price at most \(\lambda\). The sink has that bound also at zero throughput, from the return buyer's tight tail purchase. From any remaining vertex, follow an original path toward \(t\) until reaching one of those vertices. Each such arc has zero head flow and gives \(p_v-p_w\le\kappa_e\) by its tight tail purchase. All prices are consequently at most \(\lambda+nK<L-K\). Every core buyer has positive true utility, as does the extra buyer when \(\lambda<B\). The full supported core assignment fills all goods, so the physical Hall deficit is exactly one. At \(\lambda=B\) any optimum attaining the lower bound supports a throughput-\(F\) core assignment. It clears with the extra buyer outside.

## Solve the scalar recurrence

Set \(\ell_j=(2j-1)/\eta\) for \(1\le j\le F\). Below \(B\), the derivative of the projected objective on a segment of frozen throughput \(y\) is \(2\eta(y-z-m)\). From zero the first frozen throughput is \(z=0\). All subsequent ones below \(B\) are integers because the secondary objective chooses the largest optimizer. When \(z+m<F\), the next source quote is in \([\ell_{z+m},\ell_{z+m+1}]\), and the new frozen throughput is \(z+m\) except at the upper endpoint, where it is \(z+m+1\). If \(z+m=F\), the minimizing interval is \([\ell_F,B]\). Its upper endpoint clears, and any other choice reaches \(z=F\) and clears on the following step. If \(z+m>F\) with \(z<F\), the next quote is \(B\) and clears immediately. At \(z=F\), every reweighted optimum clears.

The largest-quote policy therefore requires \(\lceil(F+1)/(m+1)\rceil\) solves and the smallest-quote policy requires \(\lfloor F/m\rfloor+1\). Each finite projected endpoint is attained at a cover vertex by optimizing \(p_s\) over the optimal face and choosing a vertex. The cover optimum with componentwise least prices selects the smallest source quote. It exists because the objective with row-maximized utilities is submodular under coordinatewise minimum and maximum. For each row the maximum utility at the minimum of two prices is the larger of the two utilities, and at the maximum it is at most the smaller. The price term is linear. Thus the price minimizers are closed under componentwise minimum. Each coordinate minimum is attained on the projected optimal polyhedron, so the coordinatewise least point is attained as well. At an optimum the utilities are determined by the prices, since every buyer has positive mass. A convex combination of optimal covers can have the least price vector only if every term has that same vector. Thus the optimum with least prices is a vertex of the original cover polyhedron.

There are \(O(k)\) goods, arcs and buyers. Arc data and the choices of \(B,L\) use \(O(k+\log m)\) bits per number. For \(m=1\) the lower bound is \(2^{k-1}+1\) when \(k\ge1\), and for \(m=2n\) it is \(\Omega(2^k/n)\). Even a dense tabulation uses $O(k^3)$ bits for fixed $m$, so the lower bound is superpolynomial in the binary input length. Omitted offers can have negative bids and slope one. The LP and flow call data have polynomial bit length. The weights depend only on the integer \(z\) and the input \(\eta\), cover vertices have input-size determinant bounds, and the sole logarithmic coefficient serves only as a positive tie-break on one entry.

## Recover the minimum directly

The minimum source price is \(B\). A supporting witness at \(B\) exists by the same residual potentials. At any componentwise lower price every core buyer still has a positive offer, so a source quote below \(B\) would force mass \(M+1\) into stock \(M\). At quote \(B\) use the frozen unit-slope instance evaluated directly at \(\lambda=B\). Its least prices have \(q_s=B\) by the witness and the complementary lower bound \(q_s\ge B\). The true and frozen demand graphs coincide there, as do the reserves, so the strict minimum-price Hall certificate transfers. This takes one unit-slope cover, minimization of price sum on its optimal face, and one fixed-price flow. If descent was already run with arbitrary cover ties, the same solve recovers the minimum from its clearing answer. The direct solve bypasses the scalar recurrence even for the original slope \(1/2\). ## Exact small replays

Run `python3 -B experiments/a4h6_reweighted_cover_replay.py` from the repository root with Z3 on the path. The replay solves the original frozen assignment and reweighted cover over rationals, using the least or greatest source quote on each optimal face. A separate feasibility check uses the true tight edges and unweighted quantities. It verifies the two extremal policies for depths one through three and $m=1,2,3$. For example, at depth three and $m=1$, the first clearing takes five or nine reweighted solves, depending on the price policy. The universal bounds follow from the projection proof.
