# A2. Exact equilibria with common attacker intercepts

**Theorem.** Suppose the attacker utility of type \(i\) at target \(t\) is \(a_i-d_{it}x_t\), the losses \(d_{it}\) and defender gains \(\beta_t\) are strictly positive, and all inputs are rational. An exact Bayes–Nash equilibrium can be computed in time polynomial in the binary input length. The type and target counts, slope matrix rank, and prior denominators are unrestricted. The same bound holds for utilities \(A_i+d_{it}(c_t-x_t)\) when all \(c_t<0\), or when all \(c_t>1\). The defender can use any integer budget \(0\le r\le n\).

Conitzer asks the polynomial-time question for unrestricted intercepts and weakly monotone utilities. Li, Conitzer and Korzhyk use log slope costs on active arcs with fixed column totals in their continuation algorithm. The deduction here uses all type–target arcs in a transportation problem at a fixed defender threshold and recovers the exact resource sum through rational LP and threshold search. Part I of the project's earlier collection allows arbitrary intercepts and assumes a positive rank-one slope matrix. The hypotheses here also allow full-rank matrices. These are deductions within this investigation. Publication priority has not been established. [Conitzer, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08796) · [Li, Conitzer and Korzhyk, Algorithm 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01896#page=7) · [Prior project collection, Part I](../sources/prior-collection.md#part-i)

## Common intercepts and the fixed-threshold oracle

Discard zero-prior types during the computation. Write \(q_{it}\) for attack mass, so \(q\ge0\), \(\sum_t q_{it}=p_i>0\), \(\sum_i p_i=1\), and \(y_t=\sum_i q_{it}\). A certificate at threshold \(\lambda\) has each row attacking only maximizing targets. The defender conditions are \(\beta_t y_t\le\lambda\) at \(x_t=0\), equality at \(0<x_t<1\), and \(\beta_t y_t\ge\lambda\) at \(x_t=1\). These conditions certify maximization of the linear defender payoff for the budget \(\sum_t x_t\). Every defender best response admits such a threshold, which can be taken nonnegative.

For common intercepts, any certificate of positive budget has \(x_t>0\) everywhere. If some target has zero coverage, all rows strictly prefer every zero-coverage target to every positive-coverage one. A zero-coverage target with positive attack mass then has strictly higher defender reward than a positive-coverage target. A transfer between them improves the defender payoff. Thus for positive budgets \(y_t\ge h_t=\lambda/\beta_t\) everywhere, and \(y_t>h_t\) forces \(x_t=1\). In particular all relevant thresholds belong to \([0,\Lambda]\), where \(\Lambda=1/\sum_t\beta_t^{-1}\). Throughout assume at least one target. With \(r=0\) or \(r=n\) one can immediately fix the unique coverage vector and choose each row's best response.

For any \(\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]\) minimize \(\sum_{it}q_{it}\log d_{it}\) over nonnegative flows of row sums \(p_i\) and column sums \(y_t\ge h_t\). This LP is feasible since the complete bipartite graph can carry any nonnegative column totals summing to one. Its dual maximizes
\[
  \sum_i p_i u_i+\sum_t h_t v_t,\qquad
  u_i+v_t\le\log d_{it},\quad v_t\ge0 .
\]
At any positive-budget certificate, let \(m_i=\min_t d_{it}x_t\), \(u_i=\log m_i\), \(v_t=-\log x_t\). Its supporting flow and these dual variables are feasible and attain equality in every complementary-slackness condition. Conversely a dual optimum gives \(x_t=e^{-v_t}\in(0,1]\), and pairing it with any primal optimum gives a certificate. Finite dual optima exist at both endpoints, including when additive gauge shifts make the dual optimal set unbounded.

Choose a single true primal optimum \(q^*\) and denote its column sums by \(y_t^*\). Construct the rational polytope
\[
  0\le x_t\le1,\qquad m_i\le d_{it}x_t\quad(\text{all }i,t),\qquad
  m_i=d_{it}x_t\quad(q^*_{it}>0),\qquad
  x_t=1\quad(y_t^*>h_t).
\]
Every feasible point and \(q^*\) form a certificate, including any endpoints at zero coverage, since \(y_t^*\ge h_t\) and an unfilled target has equality. Every positive-budget certificate's coverage lies in this polytope by complementarity of **any** optimal primal and **any** optimal dual. Positive row sums guarantee each \(m_i\) is attained. Thus the attainable budgets at this threshold form a closed interval, available by minimizing and maximizing \(\sum_t x_t\). A feasible budget is decoded by adding its equality to the same LP. This argument avoids exponentiating computed dual variables.

At \(\lambda=0\) every positive-flow target is forced full, and full coverage everywhere is feasible because an optimum sends each row to targets minimizing its slope. At \(\Lambda\) all column sums equal their quotas and the recovery LP is homogeneous apart from the box. It includes the all-zero vector, for which the logarithmic dual coordinates are undefined but the certificate inequalities hold directly. For \(\lambda<\Lambda\) the quotas leave strictly positive excess, so a target is forced full. The positive-budget argument then excludes any zero coverage. Within the search interval, an all-zero certificate requires \(y_t\le h_t\) for every \(t\), which forces \(\lambda=\Lambda\). This accounts for the entire budget slice at each endpoint.

## Two-sided price bounds for the offset extension

Suppose there are rational \(A_i,c_t\) with \(a_{it}=A_i+d_{it}c_t\). In either of the two stated cases define target prices \(z_t(x_t)\) and edge weights \(w_{it}\) by the table.

| Offset condition | Increasing price \(z_t(x_t)\) | Weight \(w_{it}\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(c_t<0\) for every \(t\) | \(\log(x_t-c_t)\) | \(-\log d_{it}\) |
| \(c_t>1\) for every \(t\) | \(-\log(c_t-x_t)\) | \(\log d_{it}\) |

Each row's original maximizers are exactly the maximizers of \(w_{it}-z_t(x_t)\). In the first case the type minimizes the positive product \(d_{it}(x_t-c_t)\). In the second it maximizes the positive product \(d_{it}(c_t-x_t)\). The price ranges independently over the finite interval \([\ell_t,H_t]=[z_t(0),z_t(1)]\), with \(\ell_t<H_t\).

Use the threshold range \([0,B]\) with \(B=\max_t\beta_t\), since coverage-zero targets can now receive less than quota. For fixed \(\lambda\) and \(h_t=\lambda/\beta_t\), solve
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{q,e,f\ge0}\quad&
  \sum_{it}w_{it}q_{it}-\sum_t H_t e_t+\sum_t\ell_t f_t,\\
 &\sum_tq_{it}=p_i,\qquad y_t-e_t+f_t=h_t .
 \end{aligned}
\]
Each optimal solution has \(e_t=(y_t-h_t)^+\) and \(f_t=(h_t-y_t)^+\), since reducing positive \(e_t,f_t\) by the same amount improves the objective. Feasibility holds for every \(\lambda\), and the optimum is finite and attained. The dual is
\[
 \min_{\alpha,z}\ \sum_i p_i\alpha_i+\sum_t h_t z_t,\qquad
 \alpha_i+z_t\ge w_{it},\quad \ell_t\le z_t\le H_t.
\]
At a certificate use its actual prices and \(\alpha_i=\max_t(w_{it}-z_t)\). The threshold rules give \(e_t>0\Rightarrow z_t=H_t\) and \(f_t>0\Rightarrow z_t=\ell_t\). The attacker's best-response rules give equality on positive \(q_{it}\). These are precisely the conditions for primal and dual optimality. Conversely invert any optimal prices and pair with any primal optimum to obtain a certificate. An optimal dual exists at every threshold.

Having found one optimal \(q^*\), recover the whole fixed-threshold coverage projection with rational linear constraints. Use \(0\le x\le1\) and \(V_i\ge a_{it}-d_{it}x_t\) everywhere, with equality on positive \(q^*_{it}\). Set \(x_t=1\) whenever \(y_t^*>h_t\), and \(x_t=0\) whenever \(y_t^*<h_t\). Any original certificate supplies an optimal dual, so its coverage satisfies these constraints with \(q^*\). Every point in this LP directly satisfies the original certificate conditions with that same flow. The extrema and exact-budget reconstruction follow as above. At \(\lambda=0\) full coverage and its row best responses give a certificate. At \(B\) the all-zero vector and its row best responses do. If the factorization is not supplied, recognize the two cases by rational LP in \(A,c,\delta\). Impose the displayed factorization, \(0\le\delta\le1\), and, respectively, \(c_t\le-\delta\) or \(c_t\ge1+\delta\) for every \(t\). A positive maximum recognizes the promised side and gives a polynomial-bit rational witness.

## Exact log optimization in polynomial bit time

Both flow LPs are transshipments. Use row-to-target arcs for \(q\) and a common auxiliary vertex. In the first LP, arcs from each target to the auxiliary vertex carry its excess at zero cost. In the offset LP use target-to-auxiliary arcs for \(e\), and auxiliary-to-target arcs for \(f\). In each case the target absorbs net flow \(h_t\) and the auxiliary vertex absorbs \(1-\sum_t h_t\), which can be negative in the offset case. Negate a maximization objective to use min-cost notation. Every cost is a signed logarithm of a positive input-derived rational or zero.

Write \(M=k+n+1\) for the vertex count and bound by \(2^b\) the numerator and denominator of every rational inside a logarithm, using \(1\) for zero arc costs. A simple residual cycle has at most \(M\) arcs, including signs for reverse arcs. Its true cost is \(\log(P/Q)\) with \(P,Q\le2^{Mb}\) integers. If nonzero, its absolute cost is at least \(|P-Q|/\max(P,Q)\ge2^{-Mb}\). Approximate each forward cost within \(2^{-Mb}/(4M)\) by a rational, and negate that same approximation on the reverse arc. Every true strictly negative simple cycle remains negative in the surrogate. In the offset model the two-arc cycle that simultaneously increases \(e_t,f_t\) retains its positive min-cost sign, so the surrogate remains bounded as well. Its canonical slacks minimize the cost for each \(q\).

Solve the resulting rational flow LP exactly. If its optimizer were suboptimal for the true objective, the feasible difference to a better flow would decompose into simple residual cycles, one of which has negative true cost. That cycle would improve the approximate objective, a contradiction. This proof permits splitting true zero-cost ties, and avoids comparing total objectives weighted by binary priors. The optimum returned has polynomial bit length by rational LP bounds. To compute the logarithms to the stated precision, write each argument as \(2^j u\) with \(1\le u<2\). The series \(\log u=2\sum_{s\ge0} ((u-1)/(u+1))^{2s+1}/(2s+1)\), also used at \(u=2\), has ratio in absolute value bounded geometrically using \(1/3\). Polynomially many terms suffice after allowing for the integer multiplier \(j\) of \(\log2\). Exact finite partial sums have polynomial bit length, for example by using a common power of the rational denominator and the product of the odd term denominators. ## Searching the threshold and recovering its last bits

For certificates at \(\lambda_1<\lambda_2\) their coverages satisfy \(x^1_t\ge x^2_t\) for all \(t\). To verify this, suppose \(S=\{t:x^1_t<x^2_t\}\) is nonempty. Any row sending mass into \(S\) at \(x^2\) has a maximizer there whose utility strictly improves at \(x^1\), while all utilities outside \(S\) weakly decrease. That row sends its entire mass into \(S\) at \(x^1\), hence \(y^1(S)\ge y^2(S)\). But \(x^1_t<1\) and \(x^2_t>0\) on \(S\), giving \(y^1(S)\le\lambda_1\sum_{t\in S}1/\beta_t<\lambda_2\sum_{t\in S}1/\beta_t\le y^2(S)\). This uses strict positivity of the slopes and gains. [Project threshold ordering proof](B2-component-composition.md)

Test the two rational endpoints of the appropriate search range. At each query compute its exact budget interval \([b_-,b_+]\). If it contains \(r\), recover a certificate immediately. If \(r<b_-\), retain larger thresholds. If \(r>b_+\), retain smaller ones. At least one threshold exists for \(r\), by mixed equilibrium existence in the finite game and the defender's linear optimality conditions. Thus bisection cannot discard any solution threshold. At either range's lower end some certificate spends \(n\), and at its upper end some certificate spends zero.

For an explicit stopping bound, use variables \(q,x,V,\lambda\), with \(V_i\) the actual original maximum utility. Consider a pattern consisting of allowed attack support and coverage classes zero, interior, full. Define a closed polytope by setting other flows to zero, enforcing equality of utility on the allowed support, all row best-response inequalities, row sums, the box and budget, and the threshold inequalities for the given classes. The zero and full classes fix the coordinate. The interior class allows closed box endpoints and imposes score equality, which remains valid at either endpoint. Include the threshold search range. Any equilibrium in range belongs to one such polytope, and every feasible point in one is a certificate. Row sums and tight support bound \(V_i\). Thus a nonempty pattern polytope has a vertex, giving a solution threshold of bounded denominator. No patterns are enumerated.

Concretely let \(N=kn+n+k+1\). For all possible constraint rows use a common multiple \(D\) of the denominators of their coefficients and right-hand sides, for example the product over the input rational parameters and the two computed search endpoints. After clearing denominators bound every resulting absolute entry by an integer \(C\ge1\). These rows use only the coefficients and signs in the previous paragraph, so \(\log D,\log C\) are polynomial in input length and can be bounded before choosing a pattern. Hadamard and Cramer's rule bound every vertex denominator by \(K=(NC)^N\). Thus at least one solution threshold of denominator at most \(K\) survives all unsuccessful queries. This reasoning needs only one small-denominator witness, even if the full solution set is an interval.

Bisect until the remaining bracket has length strictly below \(1/K^2\). Distinct reduced fractions of denominators at most \(K\) are separated by at least \(1/K^2\), so exactly one such fraction in the bracket is a solution and there is no other fraction of that denominator bound there. Recover it from the ordinary continued-fraction convergents of the bracket midpoint. Its distance to that midpoint is strictly below \(1/(2K^2)\), hence below \(1/(2s^2)\) for its denominator \(s\le K\), unless it is the midpoint itself, which is already a convergent. Select the convergent in the bracket with denominator at most \(K\) and call the oracle there. The endpoint tests and strict comparisons retain all actual solutions, and the small-denominator witness proves the call succeeds. The number of bisections is \(O(1+\log^+ B'+\log K)\) for upper endpoint \(B'\). Each query has polynomial bit length, and uses polynomial-size rational LPs. This establishes the binary polynomial bound for both theorems.

Divide positive-prior flows by their priors to recover conditional strategies. Give each zero-prior row any maximizing target at the returned coverage. For an explicit mixed defender strategy, lay consecutive half-open intervals of lengths \(x_t\) on \([0,r)\). For \(u\) uniform on \([0,1)\), select intervals containing \(u+j\) for \(j=0,\ldots,r-1\). Lengths at most one ensure the \(r\) selected intervals are distinct, and an interval is selected with probability equal to its length. The fractional parts of the interval endpoints partition \([0,1)\) into at most \(n+1\) pure strategies with exact rational weights. For \(r=0\) use the empty set. ## Offsets crossing the coverage box

The fixed-threshold method also extends to arbitrary rational offsets. At a positive threshold all types agree on the sign of their best payoff relative to their row baselines. When the sign is positive use prices (-log(c_t-x_t)) on (c_t>0), deleting the excess arc wherever the price has no finite ceiling. When the sign is negative use (log(x_t-c_t)), deleting the deficit arc wherever the price has no finite floor. At sign zero, positive coverage implies positive flow and hence (x_t=c_t), forcing (x_t=max(0,c_t)). The independent A3 derivation gives the full case analysis, oracle and exact-budget reconstruction. [A3 mixed-offset theorem](A3-mixed-offsets.md)

## Weak slopes and gains

Both the common-intercept class and the factorized-intercept class are preserved by exact perturbation and support repair. For a weak instance with a witness \(A,c\), let \(\varepsilon_0\) be the violation tolerance from the A1 determinant bound for the original data. Choose \(\delta=\varepsilon_0/(1+\max_t |c_t|)\), using \(c_t=0\) for common intercepts. Replace zero \(d_{it}\) by \(\delta\), adjusting their intercepts to \(A_i+\delta c_t\), and replace zero \(\beta_t\) by \(\delta\). All changes have polynomial encoding length. Each changed row–target utility varies by at most \(\delta(1+|c_t|)\le\varepsilon_0\) throughout the box. Every defender score changes by at most \(\delta\). At the perturbed equilibrium keep its row maxima, threshold, support, and coverage classes. This gives an \(\varepsilon_0\)-feasible point for the A1 original-data support repair LP. Its determinant lower bound on positive optimal violation is \(2\varepsilon_0\), so the repair LP returns an exact original-game equilibrium. The same recognition equations \(a_{it}=A_i+d_{it}c_t\) work with zero slopes, where they require \(a_{it}=A_i\). ## Arithmetic checks

With \(p=(3/5,2/5)\), unit gains, common intercept zero and slope rows \((1,3,4),(2,1,1)\), take \(r=2\). At \(\lambda=1/5\) let the first row attack target 1 and split the second row equally between 2 and 3. Then \(y=(3/5,1/5,1/5)\). The recovery LP gives \(x_1=1\), \(x_2=x_3\in[1/3,1]\), so it recovers \(x=(1,1/2,1/2)\). The threshold is below \(\Lambda=1/3\) and a dyadic bisection started at \(0,1/3\) never queries \(1/5\) exactly. Below this threshold, ordering with the interval maximum forces budget at least 3. Above it, ordering with the interval minimum forces budget at most 5/3. Thus this budget requires precisely the non-dyadic search point.

For each offset sign, take one row, unit gains, \(r=1\), \(x=(1,0,0)\), \(q=(3/5,2/5,0)\), and \(\lambda=1/2\). With \(c=(-1,-1,-1)\) and slope row \((1,2,9)\), the three products to minimize are \((2,2,9)\). With \(c=(2,2,2)\) and row \((2,1,1/10)\), the products to maximize are \((2,2,1/5)\). Both examples have one excess and two deficits. Their defender inequalities and all attacker ties hold exactly, and their threshold exceeds the common-intercept upper bound.
