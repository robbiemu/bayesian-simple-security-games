# A4h11 Combine offset cores and heterogeneous conditional solvers

An exact minimum-price solver on a private market can be used as a branch oracle when conditioning its single shared vertex preserves that solver's promise. Different branches can use different solvers. A4h9 previously required a factorized or nearly factorized core. The common-offset transformation of A4e also works on the core alone, with rational exact quotes for its heterogeneous branches. ## A common-offset core with single-vertex branches

**Theorem.** Normalize the reserve and outside of every vertex to zero. Suppose the explicit core edges admit $b_{ij}=A_i+d_{ij}C_j$, with arbitrary positive rational $d_{ij}$. Suppose each internally disjoint branch meets the core at one vertex and has a polynomial-bit-time conditional minimum-price solver in the appropriate orientation. The conditional solvers must handle the small supply increase in A4c. The market minimum and a clearing allocation can be recovered in polynomial bit time.

The offsets can be found by sparse rational linear algebra. Raising physical reserves or outsides preserves the equations after shifting the corresponding offsets. In the reciprocal transpose, $b_{ij}/d_{ij}=C_j+(1/d_{ij})A_i$, so shared buyer and good orientations both work. Private branches themselves may use any applicable conditional algorithm, including factorization, A4a, A4b, or A4e on the private market.

### Transform only the core

Enlarge the physical capacities by A4c. Choose an integer $H$ at least three above the magnitudes of the bids and offsets and above every nonnegative outside-indifference price $b_{ij}/d_{ij}$. It will bound the physical prices and utilities used in the final repair. Let $Q_0$ be a rational LP vertex denominator bound for the original price-and-utility support template in this box with one utility-violation variable. The rows enforce cover, ties on used edges, exact reserves on undersold goods, and exact outsides on buyers who send mass outside. The only variable row selection is which ties and bounds to impose. Cramer's rule with all possible cleared rows gives a bound of polynomial bit length before seeing the support.

Let $g\ge1$ count core goods, $\delta=1/(16g)$, and choose $L\ge1$ with $2^{-L}\le d_{ij}\le2^L$ on core edges. Put $K=32g(L+1)$, $t_0=1/(8Q_0\max(1,d_{\max}))$, and $a=t_0 2^{-K}$. Define an odd increasing rational piecewise-affine map $\Phi$. Use $t/a$ on $[-a,a]$, and map the positive knots $a(1+\delta)^\ell$ to $1+\ell\delta$, interpolating between them. Keep knots up to and including the first beyond $2+(1+d_{\max})(H+\max_j|C_j|)$, then extend with an increasing affine tail. There are $O(\delta^{-1}\log((2+(1+d_{\max})(H+\max_j|C_j|))/a))$ knots. Exact rational powers and the affine inverse formulas have polynomial bit length.

Use core quotes $Z_j=\Phi(p_j-C_j)$, $W_i=\Phi(u_i-A_i)$. Their transformed floors are rational. Outside the window $|p_j-C_j|\le t_0$, the transformed offer on a core edge is $-\Phi(d_{ij}\Phi^{-1}(Z_j))$. Within the window replace it by its secant

$$
 V_{ij}(Z_j)=-\frac{\Phi(d_{ij}t_0)}{\Phi(t_0)}Z_j .
$$

Between positive knots, $1/s\le\Phi'(s)\le(1+\delta)/s$. Both $t_0$ and $d_{ij}t_0$ are above $a$. On the part outside the window and within the physical price box, the transformed slope magnitude lies between $1/(1+\delta)$ and $1+\delta$. For the secant, the numerator's difference from $\Phi(t_0)$ is at most $(1+\delta)|\log d_{ij}|$, while $\Phi(t_0)\ge1+K\log 2$. Its slope differs from one by less than $\delta$. For physical prices above $H$, extend the transformed core offer with slope $-1$ from the value at $H$. All incident physical offers strictly lose to outside at $H$, including for the surrogate. The positive enlarged capacities therefore keep all clearing prices in the box. Surrogate physical utilities are also bounded by $H$.

The private quotes stay in their original coordinates. On an edge incident to the port, apply the appropriate $\Phi$ or $\Phi^{-1}$ to preserve its comparisons. After fixing the port, every private conditional market is the original one at the exact inverse-transformed boundary quote. Monotone piecewise-affine compositions add breakpoints, so the explicit surrogate, including these edges, has polynomially many rational pieces. The only error in physical offers comes from the central secant. Its physical utility and the original utility both lie within $d_{ij}t_0$ of $A_i$ there. The difference is at most $2d_{ij}t_0\le1/(4Q_0)$.

### Exact surrogate and exact repair

Shift the transformed floors to zero. Let $V_{ij}$ now denote the shifted core offer. Freeze it at trial $t$ by using the unit-slope offer $V_{ij}(t_j)+t_j-z_j$. Each frozen market has a factorized core and the same private conditional oracles. Its minimum can be computed by A4h9. The conditional branch load functions, and hence the conceptual unary offers of A4h9, are fixed across those solves. At the enlarged physical margins every frozen clearing has unique prices by A4c's Hall proof, which uses strict monotonicity and works also for the rational piecewise-affine offers.

The map to frozen core prices is a contraction in the sup norm. Each $V_{ij}(z_j)+z_j$ is $\delta$-Lipschitz. The A4a sensitivity bound counts only the $g$ changing core columns, giving constant at most $(2g-1)\delta<1/2$. A surrogate clearing at the enlarged margins exists by the continuous price-and-allocation fixed point. Its core prices are a fixed point because the frozen offers agree there and the frozen prices are unique. Clip the frozen map to any rational integer box of radius $R$ containing that point. Clipping preserves the contraction.

To bound the exact fixed-point denominator $Q_1$, choose an explicit affine piece of each transformed offer at the surrogate equilibrium. Keep those piece bounds, the cover constraints, and the support ties and slack floors of one clearing flow. Every point of this rational price-and-utility polyhedron clears at the enlarged margins. Uniqueness of prices, and maximum utility in each positive-mass row, make it a singleton. All possible piece rows have polynomial bit length. A uniform Cramer bound $Q_1$ is computable without knowing the pieces selected by the solution. Dyadic rounding on a mesh at most $1/(16Q_1^2)$ and $O(\log(R+1)+\log Q_1)$ frozen solves put the core prices within reconstruction tolerance. One further frozen solve at the reconstructed prices returns the surrogate equilibrium and an exact flow.

Decode the surrogate prices and utilities in physical coordinates. In the original support LP, keep that enlarged-market flow, impose its ties and slack floors, and minimize the common violation $\eta$. The decoded prices witness $\eta\le1/(4Q_0)$. A positive optimum would be at least $1/Q_0$. Thus $\eta=0$ gives original clearance at the enlarged capacities, which has exactly the original minimum prices. A final original-capacity flow recovers the allocation. All conditional quote lengths, rounding lengths, and numbers of frozen, LP, and flow calls are polynomial. ### A separating family

Use a dense $m$-buyer, $m$-good core with all uncovered offers equal to one and slope four on the diagonal and one off it. Its offsets are $A_i=1,C_j=0$. Every pair of matching indices has an alternating slope-product ratio of 16. At each core good attach one extra good and two local buyers, all slopes one, with intercept rows $(1,1)$ and $(1,3/2)$ on the shared and private good. No other branch edges are present. These branch slopes factor, but the alternating intercept sum of each four-edge branch is $1/2$, excluding common offsets on it. For growing $m$, no fixed number of deletions can make the dense core factorized. Two disjoint index pairs also rule out the A4a weighted column bound. In each pair a column slope distortion is at least four, costing at least $3/4$ in the column-spread sum. The offset-core solver handles all branches and the dense core together.

## Recognize a decomposition with different private solvers

Suppose the candidate core class and the private classes are recognizable and hereditary under vertex and edge deletion. For private admissibility use any finite union of such classes, testing in the orientation required by the port. Exact factorization and the offset equations are tested on the retained offer graph. Both conditions survive raised outside offers and reserves, with the offsets shifted as above.

In each connected component, form the incidence tree of its biconnected blocks and their member vertices. Try each vertex as a core root. When a child block is reached through a retained vertex $a$, test its entire descendant side after deleting $a$. If the private graph passes, detach the side as a branch through $a$. Otherwise retain that block and continue. Test one common set of core equations on the final retained graph.

No block can straddle a one-vertex attachment. A witness decomposition whose core contains the root can split a branch at a retained vertex into child sides, since private admissibility is hereditary. If the greedy test detaches a side, removing any extra witness core vertices on that side preserves core admissibility. If a side fails, its first block must be retained by every remaining witness. Induction shows that whenever a witness exists for the root, the retained core passes. The number and sizes of the tests are polynomial. This avoids requiring offset solutions on individual blocks to agree at shared vertices.

## Transfer to weak losses and gains

L4b changes capacities and reserves across its threshold and budget searches. All strict games with a decomposition valid under those changes inherit the mixed-core solver. Positive losses are needed for reciprocal quotes. To handle zero losses use a perturbation chosen to preserve the structure, then apply A1's exact repair once on the original game.

For a sparse factorized part, first test the multiplicative cycle equations on the positive edges. Factor them within each positive-edge connected component, including isolated vertices as singleton components. The choice of positive factors in one component has a gauge $s$ that multiplies its buyer factors by $s$ and divides its good factors by $s$. For each allowed zero edge draw a directed edge from its buyer component to its good component.

**Completion criterion.** The zero slopes can be made arbitrarily small and positive while preserving the positive slopes and exact factorization precisely when this directed graph is acyclic. A directed cycle, including a loop, cancels all gauges in the product of its new slopes and fixes a positive product. If the graph is acyclic, take gauges $s_C=B^{t(C)}$ in topological order. Choose the rational base $B>1$ large enough that every new zero-edge slope is below the allowed perturbation. The exponents are at most the number of components. The initial factors, the base, and all completed slopes have polynomial bit length. In a near-factorized part with supplied tolerances $e_j<1$, find suitable factors on the positive edges by the A4a feasibility LP. The same gauges preserve all their relative slope errors, and placing the new slopes exactly at the normalized value one meets the zero-edge tolerances.

For an offset part, keep the rational solution $a_{ij}=A_i+d_{ij}C_j$ on its full allowed graph. On each allowed zero edge set $d'_{ij}=s$ and $a'_{ij}=A_i+s C_j$. Then it has the same offset equation. Unconstrained port edges and omitted offers with zero slopes can receive an ordinary small positive slope. Perturb zero defender gains by $s$ as well. The change in any utility on $[0,1]$ is at most $K_0s$ for $K_0=1+\max_j|C_j|$ over the chosen pieces, or $K_0=1$ if there are no offsets. The score error is at most $s$. If $\gamma$ is the least strictly positive original viability gap for the omitted offers, choose $s\le \min(1/(8K_0\Delta),\gamma/(4K_0))$, with the gap condition omitted when there are no omitted offers. Here $\Delta$ is A1's original support LP determinant bound. The allowed graph remains valid because all originally omitted offers remain strictly dominated. Formerly viable edges that become dominated may be kept in the input graph.

Solve the strict game through L4b and return the original support LP optimum on its support and coverage classes. The perturbation errors witness a common violation below $1/\Delta$, so a positive optimum is impossible. This applies when the chosen core and all private structures admit these perturbations on their allowed edges. Conditional slopes after reciprocal inversion and capacities at temporarily tiny gains still have polynomial binary encoding. A flat edge within one connected positive-edge factor component can violate the completion criterion. For instance the slope array $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ on a viable four-cycle cannot complete the flat edge by an arbitrarily small factorized slope. An offset or other conditional solver can still apply on such a part.
