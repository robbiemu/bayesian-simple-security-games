# A4f Formulations with bounded handicap

The target is an exact reduction of the arbitrary binary rational buyer–good market to a polynomial-size sufficient LCP with polynomial handicap, followed by exact recovery. This would also give a polynomial-time solver for the game through the market reduction. The current A4d matrix cannot meet that target by shrinking its regularization or rescaling its coordinates. Two different reformulations survive these obstructions. One uses a smaller monotone contraction, and the other gives exact slope normalization by adding buyers and goods. Neither currently supplies a polynomial-handicap LCP for the full problem. [Newton preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07815v1#S1) · [A4d](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md) · [Exact threshold and budget decoder](L4-newton-and-continuation.md#exact-threshold-and-budget-recovery-with-a-clearing-oracle)

## Why rescaling the regularized matrix fails

Use the unsigned incidence arrays in \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&B^{\mathsf T}\\-A&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). For an edge \(ij\), \(A\) has a 1 at each endpoint and \(B\) has 1 at buyer \(i\) and \(d_{ij}\) at good \(j\). Use two buyers and three goods with

\[
 d=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

On the cycle of goods 1 and 2 in edge order \(11,12,21,22\), put \(f=(1,-1,-1,1)\) and \(g=(-12,-22,0,14)\). Set \(f=0\) off the cycle, \(g_{13}=44,\ g_{23}=0\), and take the node vector \(y=(5,7,-6,-4,0)\). Then \(Af=0\), \(B_C^{\mathsf T}y=-f_C\) on the cycle, and \(Ag=2y\) at nodes where \(y\ne0\). At the test vector \((f+\varepsilon^2g,2\varepsilon y)\), all node products for \(M+\varepsilon I\) vanish. Each cycle edge product is \(-\varepsilon+\varepsilon^5g_e^2\le-\varepsilon/2\) for \(0<\varepsilon\le1/8\). The only positive product is the one at edge \(13\), equal to \((440+1936\varepsilon^2)\varepsilon^3\le500\varepsilon^3\).

For any positive diagonal \(L,R\), the product at coordinate \(j\) for \(L(M+\varepsilon I)R\) tested at \(R^{-1}v\) is the old product times \(s_j=L_{jj}/R_{jj}\). Write \(H=1+4\widehat\kappa\) for this scaled matrix. The witness gives \(H\ge s_{11}/(1000\varepsilon^2s_{13})\). Swapping goods 1 and 3 gives the reciprocal weight ratio, so

\[
 1+4\widehat\kappa\bigl(L(M+\varepsilon I)R\bigr)\ \ge\ \frac{1}{1000\varepsilon^2}.
\]

The same principal block embeds in the game with unit defender gains. This survives even scaling factors that depend on \(\varepsilon\). Ordinary positive diagonal congruence and principal pivot transformations of a homogeneous LCP preserve the handicap exactly. In a principal pivot, the two factors in each selected complementary product swap, and the induced change of test vector is a bijection. Restricting to a principal submatrix can only decrease the handicap. Hence a lift that recovers the old matrix by principal pivots and restriction inherits the obstruction. [Handicap and its optimization over scalings](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10701v2)

The full block can occur on the positive regularized support. Take \(y^*=(3,3,2,2,2)\), \(x^*=B^{\mathsf T}y^*=(5,5,5;5,7,5)\), intercepts \(b=x^*\), masses \((15,17)\), and capacities \((10,12,10)\). Put \(l=(1,1,-1,-1,-1)\). The matrix \(AB^{\mathsf T}\) maps \(l^\perp\) to itself and has full kernel spanned by \((8,10,-9,-6,-9)\), outside \(l^\perp\). Its restriction is invertible. Solve \((AB^{\mathsf T}+\varepsilon^2 I)\eta=AB^{\mathsf T}y^*+\varepsilon y^*\) in \(l^\perp\). Then \(x^\varepsilon=B^{\mathsf T}\eta,\ y^\varepsilon=y^*-\varepsilon\eta\) satisfy both residual blocks at zero and converge to the strictly positive \(x^*,y^*\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), pruning only zero variables of the regularized solution leaves the full bad block.

There is a reason to need a binary-small uniform \(\varepsilon\) beyond the particular determinant bound in A4d. Add a disjoint buyer of mass 1 facing two goods of capacity 4, with unit slopes and intercepts \(2,2-\zeta\), where \(\zeta=2^{-t}\). Give cross-component edges negative intercepts to keep their flows zero. For \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), the buyer utility in the regularized system is at most 2 and her total purchase is at most \(1+2\varepsilon\le3\), so both good prices are zero. The utility is positive, since otherwise the first edge alone would carry \(2/\varepsilon>1\). If \(\varepsilon>\zeta\) and the second purchase were zero, the first would be \(1+\varepsilon u\), hence \(2-u=\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon u)>\zeta\). The residual of the second edge would be negative. Thus both purchases are positive for \(\varepsilon>\zeta\). A4d’s decoder would make both offers tight in the original market, which is impossible at the only clearing prices, zero. This example forces \(\varepsilon\le\zeta\) for that uniform regularization and decoder. Together with the first block, it forces a handicap exponential in \(t\) even after arbitrary positive diagonal scaling. A decoder that discovers a different support is outside this argument. [A4d support decoder](A4d-p-matrix-complementarity.md#one-exact-p-lcp-call)

## Cycle gains and exact changes of variables

An alternating cycle is balanced when the two products of alternating slopes agree. A balanced slope graph admits factors \(d_{ij}=\alpha_i\beta_j\), obtained by propagation along a spanning forest. In that case diagonal scaling makes the unregularized matrix skew-symmetric, so its handicap is zero. The exact unweighted flow decoder is the one in A4e. [Covering reduction for factorized slopes](A4e-covering-lp.md#factorized-slopes-and-repeated-covering-solves)

On an unbalanced simple cycle, let \(f\) be its alternating signed unit flow, so \(Af=0\). The square array \(B_C\) on the cycle nodes and edges is nonsingular. Its determinant has the two alternating products as its two monomials, with opposite signs. Choose \(y\) on those nodes with \(B_C^{\mathsf T}y=-f_C\). The homogeneous complementarity products are all negative on the cycle edges and zero elsewhere. This proves that the unregularized matrix is insufficient under any positive row and column scaling. The same witness also obstructs entrywise-small changes beyond the uniform diagonal. For \(\|E\|_{\max}\le\eta\) on a fixed cycle, its edge products stay bounded away from zero on the negative side and its node products are \(O(\eta)\). If \(S_E,S_V\) are the sums of diagonal product weights on the cycle edges and nodes, the handicap inequality gives \(H\ge c S_E/(\eta S_V)\) for a positive constant \(c\) depending on the cycle. Pair each cycle node with a distinct adjacent cycle edge. At a test vector with just those two coordinates equal to one, the edge product stays positive of bounded size and the node product stays negative of bounded size. Summing their inequalities gives \(H\ge c' S_V/S_E\). Thus any sufficiently small entrywise perturbation has optimized \(H=\Omega(\eta^{-1/2})\) on this fixed slope block.

The two-cycle block strengthens this to \(H=\Omega(\eta^{-1})\) for arbitrary dense perturbations. On one cycle use a spanning tree plus the other branch’s first edge as columns for a correction to \(f\). Restricted to the cycle nodes, those columns in the ordinary incidence array are nonsingular. They stay nonsingular for small perturbations. Adjust by \(O(\eta)\) to zero all tested node residuals, keeping the four cycle products negative of order one. Only the escape edge can have a positive product, of order at most \(\eta\). Exchanging the cycles gives the reciprocal weight ratio and the bound. If the lower-left flow balance block changes by only \(O(\eta^2)\), scale the original node witness by \(C\eta\) for a sufficiently large constant \(C\). The correction is now \(O(\eta^2)\), the negative products are of order \(\eta\), and the escape product is at most of order \(\eta^3\). This gives \(H=\Omega(\eta^{-2})\) even for non-diagonal small perturbations of the other blocks.

Another limitation concerns affine projections of solution sets. For any two solutions of a sufficient LCP, the products of their coordinate differences satisfy

\[
 (z_i-z'_i)(w_i-w'_i)=-z_iw'_i-z'_iw_i\le0 .
\]

Finite handicap forces every such product to vanish, so every convex combination is also a solution. The full market clearing set can be nonconvex. An affine projection of a sufficient LCP therefore cannot describe all clearing utility and price pairs of that example. A reduction selecting one equilibrium or using a nonlinear decoder need not have that property. [Nonconvex clearing set](A4e-covering-lp.md#failed-hall-tests-and-linear-cuts)

Global increasing coordinate changes that keep every offer affine also have a rigid case. Fix a connected offer graph and regard \(b_{ij}-d_{ij}t\) as a map from good \(j\)'s price to buyer \(i\)'s utility. These maps and their inverses generate positive-slope affine maps around closed walks at a base good. A coordinate change at the base good conjugates this group into the affine group. If all loop slopes are one, the slopes factor. If some slope differs from one but the group has no nonidentity translations, every commutator is trivial because its slope is one. The group then fixes the point of any nonunit-slope map. Propagating this point gives common offsets \(b_{ij}=A_i+d_{ij}C_j\), the signed-root case in A4e. Otherwise the group contains a nonzero translation and a nonunit-slope map. For an increasing bijection \(G\) of the real line conjugating both to affine maps, translation gives \(G(t)=s t+O(1)\) with a continuous periodic remainder. The nonunit map's conjugate has the same slope by these asymptotics. Conjugating the translation by powers of that map gives arbitrarily small periods of the remainder, which must be constant. All node changes are then affine and preserve cycle gains. This argument is for whole affine offer lines. [Signed-root reconditioning](A4e-covering-lp.md#common-offsets-with-arbitrary-slopes)

## An exact slope subdivision

An edge with intercept \(b\) and slope \(d=d_1d_3/d_2\) can be replaced by the path of offers \(i\to G,\ I\to G,\ I\to j\), with slopes \(d_1,d_2,d_3\). Give the dummy buyer \(I\) and good \(G\) equal mass and capacity \(R>\max(m_i,c_j)\). Let \(\alpha=d_1/d_2,\ b_3=\max(1,1+b/\alpha),\ b_2=b_3+1,\ b_1=b+\alpha\). The new intercepts satisfy \(b_2>0,\ b_2>b_3,\ \alpha b_2>b_1\).

At any clearing equilibrium at least one of \(u_I,p_G\) is positive. The corresponding dummy balance makes the middle flow positive, since each end flow is less than \(R\). Thus \(u_I+d_2p_G=b_2\). If both are positive, the balances make the end flows equal. If \(u_I=0\), the first edge is slack and its flow is zero, so the middle flow is \(R\) and the last flow is zero. If \(p_G=0\), the last edge is slack and the same argument forces both ends to zero. Writing \(\ell_1,\ell_3\) for the end slacks, the original slack equals \(\ell_1+\alpha\ell_3\). Matching end flows project to the original edge and satisfy its complementarity.

Conversely, fix an original equilibrium with purchase \(x\) on the edge. Use flows \(x,R-x,x\). Choose \(p_G\) in

\[
 [0,b_2/d_2]\ \cap\
 \left[(b_1-u_i)/d_1,\ (b_2-b_3+d_3p_j)/d_2\right],
\]

and set \(u_I=b_2-d_2p_G\). The intersection is nonempty by the original inequality and the intercept choices. If \(x>0\), the latter two bounds coincide and both end slacks vanish. This proves exact equivalence by projection for markets with any rational slopes. Splitting off rational factors in \([1,1+\eta]\), using numerator or denominator factors as needed, takes \(O(1+|\log d|/\eta)\) steps for \(0<\eta\le1\). For inverse-polynomial \(\eta\), dimensions and bit lengths are polynomial in the original input.

The construction preserves the unbalanced loop. A signed test flow \(f\) on the original edge lifts to \((f,-f,f)\). Set the dummy test coordinates to \(y_I=-d_3y_j,\ y_G=(d_3/d_2)y_j\). The two new node imbalances and the last two edge residuals vanish. The original edge product appears on the first edge and all the old node products are preserved. This embeds the original handicap witness in the unregularized larger market under every positive scaling. On any fixed subdivision of the two-cycle block, the stronger regularized lower bound also persists with constants depending on the subdivision. For either unbalanced cycle, take the first edge of the other branch at their shared buyer. Its ordinary incidence column restricted to the cycle gives one extra independent column, so the correction \(Ag=2y\) on the cycle nodes can use it as the sole off-cycle edge. Testing \((f+\varepsilon^2 g,2\varepsilon y)\) and exchanging the roles of the branches gives the reciprocal weight ratios as before. For a cycle of length \(\ell\) with slopes in \([1,2]\) and gain 2 or \(1/2\), propagation bounds the test \(y\) by \(2^{O(\ell)}\). Closing the cycle divides by its gain's constant distance from one, and solving \(Ag=2y\) on a tree plus the extra edge increases the bound by only \(O(\ell)\). Hence even for growing subdivisions the scaled handicap factor \(H\) is at least \(2^{-O(\ell)}\varepsilon^{-2}\) for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\le2^{-O(\ell)}\). This still leaves a factorial obstruction for A4d’s prescribed perturbation in the expanded dimension. The near-factorized tolerance in A4a also depends on that dimension. Near-unit edge labels alone do not satisfy it after arbitrary subdivision.

## Eliminating flow variables

There is an exact lower-dimensional formulation of the regularized market. Sign the good coordinates and use \(h_i=u_i,\ h_j=-p_j\), \(g=(m,-c)\). Use signed columns \(A_e=e_i-e_j,\ B_e=e_i-d_{ij}e_j\). The edge complementarity constraints give the purchases explicitly,

\[
 x(h)=\varepsilon^{-1}[b-B^{\mathsf T}h]_+ .
\]

The node residual with the same signing is \(G(h)=g-Ax(h)+\varepsilon h\). Take a rational box with natural bounds \(h_i\ge0,\ h_j\le0\) and artificial opposite bounds larger in magnitude than the A4d coordinate bounds. For sufficiently small rational \(a>0\) consider the map \(F(h)=\operatorname{clip}(h-aG(h))\) to this box.

For any two points the secant matrix of \(G\) is

\[
 J_\theta=\varepsilon I+\varepsilon^{-1}A\operatorname{diag}(\theta)B^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad 0\le\theta_e\le1 .
\]

The incidence part has nonpositive off-diagonals and column sums zero. For \(a\) at most the reciprocal of a uniform upper bound on the diagonal, \(I-a J_\theta\) is nonnegative with column sums \(1-a\varepsilon\). Thus \(F\) is order-preserving and an \(\ell_1\) contraction of factor \(1-a\varepsilon\), on only \(k+n\) variables. The A4d solution is a fixed point, so uniqueness rules out a spurious artificial-bound solution. A residual of size \(s\) bounds the distance to the exact point by \(s/(a\varepsilon)\). A uniform determinant bound on the rational regularized solution permits exact coordinate reconstruction from polynomial-bit precision, followed by A4d’s original support LP. Polynomial \(\ell_1\) query bounds do not by themselves give polynomial computation of the query locations. [Query-efficient fixed points](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16089)

Every affine cell does have a monotone signed node matrix after its own scaling. For the active edge set, \(A\operatorname{diag}(\theta)B^{\mathsf T}\) is a column Laplacian with reciprocal arcs. Choose a positive stationary vector \(\pi\) on each component. Multiplying columns by \(\pi\) makes both row and column sums zero, so its symmetric part is a positive semidefinite Laplacian. The added diagonal \(\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(\pi)\) is positive. This is handicap zero for a known cell. A shared scaling that balances all single-edge Laplacians would require \(\pi_i=d_{ij}\pi_j\) on every edge, hence balanced cycle gains. The open part is choosing the relevant cells or replacing their dependence on the unknown solution by a global formulation.

## Locating a well-conditioned support

Expand supplies as in A4c. Then a sufficiently small generic rational perturbation of the intercepts can make every tight component a tree with exactly one outside or reserve anchor at the unique clearing price. To see this, perturb edge \(e\) by \(t^{r_e}\) for distinct positive integers \(r_e\). A minimally dependent set of tight equations has a dependence with some nonzero edge coefficient, since the anchor rows alone are independent. Its right side evaluated at the perturbation is a nonzero polynomial, because the edge powers are distinct. Determinant bounds on all such dependencies and on the data allow one sufficiently small dyadic \(t\), of polynomial bit length, to keep the first nonzero coefficient dominant for every dependence. Thus the tight equations are independent.

A4’s strict minimum-price Hall test gives a path from every good to an anchor in its undirected tight component. An isolated buyer is itself at outside. A cycle plus an anchor would give more equations than coordinates, as would a tree with two anchors. Therefore all tight components have the asserted structure. On a supplied correct tight forest, factor propagation gives a zero-handicap restricted problem. The original expanded capacities admit the same price after removing non-tight edges, and A4c’s unique-price argument also applies to that restricted graph. To undo the intercept perturbation, retain the perturbed support and anchors and solve the original support LP with a single violation variable. The perturbation is below any positive determinant gap for this template, so the optimum is zero. Finally the expanded-capacity price gives the original minimum. This separates exact recovery on a correct support from finding the support in polynomial time. ## A zero-handicap transform on supplied intervals

Suppose a rational price box \([\ell,U]\) contains the original least price and \(\ell\) lies above or at the original reserves. Supply rational knots for continuous, strictly increasing piecewise-linear changes \(\Phi_i\) of utilities and \(\Psi_j\) of prices. The condition to obtain unit slopes is

\[
 \Phi_i(b_{ij}-d_{ij}p)+\Psi_j(p)=C_{ij}
 \qquad (\ell_j\le p\le U_j).
\]

For each offer, split the price interval at the price knots and at the inverse images of the utility knots. On each open segment the equation is \(\Phi_i' d_{ij}=\Psi_j'\). These positive ratio equations on the graph of intervals can be solved by rational factor propagation, then integrated to give the maps and the constants \(C_{ij}\). The transformed market has unit slopes, outside \(\Phi_i(0)\), and reserves \(\Psi_j(\ell_j)\). The true least price transforms to one clearing price, so the transformed least price lies in the box. Its inverse is buyer-feasible for the original, hence at least the original least price by A4’s strict Hall test. The order inequality in the other direction gives equality and an original flow check recovers clearance. The transformed market has a skew complementarity matrix with handicap zero, or can use two ordinary covering LPs. This is a polynomial test and exact solver for supplied polynomial-size knot sets. A tight cycle with unbalanced slopes wholly in the box can obstruct this condition. [A4b domination by the least price](A4b-direct-hall-search.md#removing-one-good-at-a-trial-price)

## Binary precision with a small handicap bound

A polynomial upper bound on the handicap would give polynomial bit time as well as polynomial solve counts. A conservative short-step argument suffices for this implication. Use the preprint’s bounded initialization embedding and its support-recovery target for a supplied rational bound on \(\widehat\kappa\). Let \(H=1+4\kappa\) for that bound and use a small neighborhood \(\|x\odot s/\mu-\mathbf1\|_2\le\beta\), with \(\beta=1/(100(1+H))\). For embedded dimension \(d\), reduce \(\mu\) to \(\mu'=\mu/(1+\beta/(8d))\) and solve the Newton equations. In the standard relative coordinates \(a_i=\sqrt{s_i/x_i}\,\Delta x_i/\sqrt{\mu'}\), \(b_i=\sqrt{x_i/s_i}\,\Delta s_i/\sqrt{\mu'}\), the equations give \(\|a+b\|_2\le2\beta\). Their positive products have sum at most \(\|a+b\|_2^2/4\), and their negative magnitudes sum to at most \(H\) times that. This keeps the Newton point positive and bounds its new centrality error \(\|a\odot b\|_2\) by \((1+H)\beta^2=\beta/100\).

The initialization embedding has coordinates \(x,\tilde x,s,\tilde s\) with \(\tilde s=\tilde q-x\), so \(x\) and \(\tilde s\) are bounded by \(\|\tilde q\|_\infty\). For each coordinate, either \(x_i\) or \(\tilde s_i\) is at least half \(\tilde q_i\). The neighborhood then bounds \(s_i\) or \(\tilde x_i\) using the product bound, and the affine equation \(s=Mx+q+\tilde x\) bounds both. Thus a single upper bound \(U\) of polynomial bit length holds for all coordinates in the neighborhood. Until the first step below the recovery target, \(\mu\) stays above a computable \(\mu_{\mathrm{floor}}>0\) of polynomial bit length. Every coordinate is consequently at least \(\mu_{\mathrm{floor}}/(2U)\).

After each exact Newton update, round the embedded primal coordinates to a fixed rational grid of mesh at most \(\beta\mu_{\mathrm{floor}}/(100d U(1+\|\widehat M\|_\infty))\), and recompute the affine residuals. Positivity and the neighborhood persist by the slack \(\beta-\beta/100\). All Newton systems then have polynomial-bit inputs. With polynomial \(H\), the number of steps and the bit size of the rational \(\mu\)'s are polynomial. At the preprint’s target, its support LP returns an exact solution. This argument covers a less aggressive step size than Theorem 1.2 and provides the binary-time implication needed for the proposed formulation.
