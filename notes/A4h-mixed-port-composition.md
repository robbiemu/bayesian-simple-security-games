# A4h9 Exact composition at shared buyers and goods

**Theorem.** Suppose a market has a factorized assignment core and internally disjoint branches, each attached at one core buyer or good. If the conditional branch problems can be solved exactly in polynomial bit time at rational boundary quotes and with the small supply perturbation below, then the original minimum prices and a clearing allocation can be recovered in polynomial bit time. Branch tables may be exponential. The solver queries a branch only at a selected boundary price or buyer utility. The same construction applies after contracting certified groups that have a factorized core in suitably transformed coordinates. [Price grouping](A4h-forced-price-relations.md#exact-contraction-after-certification) · [Good-only query solver](A4h-supplied-capacity.md#query-branch-loads-at-selected-prices)

## The full interval at one boundary quote

Take a branch with one shared good $h$, and fix its price $t\ge r_h$. An internally clearing outcome assigns all branch buyers to best options, including outside, and respects all private capacities and above-reserve sell-outs. Give the shared good unlimited capacity until combining the branch with the core. Write $L(t)$ for the minimum boundary load over internally clearing outcomes.

Fold the boundary offer into each private buyer's outside option and compute the private minimum prices. At those prices, reinsert $h$ at price $t$ and minimize its load subject to the buyers' demands and the private capacity upper bounds. Call that value $\ell$. If we give $h$ capacity $\ell$ and trial reserve $t$, the upper Hall cuts pass by construction. The lower Hall cuts on private above-reserve goods pass because every demanded private edge of the folded equilibrium is still demanded. Both families together give an internally clearing flow with boundary load $\ell$. The conditional-price swap cited below transfers the boundary load of any internally clearing outcome at quote $t$ to the same private prices. Thus $L(t)=\ell$, and one conditional minimum-price solve and one minimum-load flow compute it. [Hall cuts for upper capacities and required sell-outs](A4c-any-clearing-equilibrium.md#one-call-at-enlarged-capacities)

For two internally clearing outcomes with $t<t'$, let $D$ be the goods whose prices are strictly higher in the second. This includes $h$. Every buyer who purchases in $D$ at the higher trial strictly prefers $D$ at the lower trial. Private members of $D$ sell out at the higher trial, so comparing the mass that must fit in $D$ shows $y(t')\le y(t)$ for any such pair of boundary loads. Taking the lower trial to have its minimum load gives $y(t')\le L(t'^-)$. That upper value is attained by taking a left limit of conditional minimum-price outcomes with minimum loads. The prices can be kept in the original finite offer ceilings, and limit flows keep every private sale that is still required. At the fixed quote, the conditional-price swap transfers this boundary allocation to the conditional minimum. Convex combinations of flows at those same prices then realize the entire interval

$$
 [L(t),L(t^-)]\qquad(t>r_h).
$$

Taking a right limit of minimum-load outcomes at quotes decreasing to the trial quote shows that the limit is feasible there. Monotonicity gives the reverse inequality, so the minimum-load function is right-continuous. It has finite range because each demand graph and set of above-reserve private goods determines a fixed rational flow problem. Far enough above reserve the port is unused. At reserve, the minimum load suffices for gluing since the shared good may have slack. The interval proof applies to continuous strictly decreasing offers. [One-good coordinate test](A4b-direct-hall-search.md#removing-one-good-at-a-trial-price) · [Conditional-price swap](A4h-boundary-dominance.md#one-price-representative-at-fixed-boundary-quotes)

For a shared buyer $i$, take the reciprocal transpose. Her utility becomes the shared good's price, and a branch load is the amount of her mass committed to that branch. For affine offers this is the transpose used in A4e. A strictly decreasing continuous piecewise-affine offer can also be transposed using its inverse on its range. Extend the affine tails if necessary outside the relevant ranges. The tightness conditions and required sales are exchanged. The same interval applies at positive utility, and at outside utility any remaining mass may go outside. [Reciprocal transpose](A4e-covering-lp.md#factorized-slopes-and-repeated-covering-solves) · [Earlier two-sided extremal-removal results, Alaei–Jain–Malekian](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~saeed/archive/competitive_equilibrium_with_general_utilities.pdf#page=10)

## Use the intervals without storing their breakpoints

Suppose the explicit core has factorized slopes, and disjoint branches each meet the core at one buyer or good. Require a polynomial-bit-time conditional solve for each private branch at a rational port quote, in the direct orientation for goods and the reciprocal one for buyers. Perturb the physical capacities first as in A4c, making the original minimum the unique price vector of the enlarged market. Conditional solvers must handle the perturbed margins. Use rational affine offers or explicitly specified continuous strictly decreasing rational piecewise-affine offers. The fixed-price Hall proof of the perturbation also applies to these offers, with existence from the same continuous fixed point. Let $q'$ be a common quantity unit for the perturbed margins.

Normalize the core to unit slopes with coordinates $U_i,Z_j$ for buyer utilities and good prices, and bids $b_{ij}$ in those coordinates. Represent a drop $\Delta$ at normalized port quote $\nu$ by a unit-slope unary buyer at a good port or a unit-slope unary good at a buyer port. At any strictly positive quote these artificial leaves support exactly the interval above. At reserve they implement the minimum load. Replacing a higher reserve load by the minimum simply releases capacity or increases the shared buyer's allowed outside flow. Hence any clearing outcome of the augmented core lifts to an enlarged physical clearing outcome. Conversely a physical clearing outcome projects after this reserve adjustment. No explicit listing of the virtual leaves is needed.

The assignment dual on the augmented core reduces to the convex function

$$
 F(U,Z)=m\cdot U+c\cdot Z+
 \sum_{\text{port drops }(\nu,\Delta,X)}\Delta(\nu-X)_+
 \quad\text{on } U,Z\ge0,\quad U_i+Z_j\ge b_{ij}.
$$

Here $c$ is enlarged core supply, $m$ is core buyer mass, and $X$ is the relevant core price or utility coordinate. A subgradient starts at $(m,c)$ and subtracts the queried right-hand load from each port coordinate. This uses one conditional solve and one flow per branch, with no evaluation of the large sum. Every minimizer lifts to physical clearance. The physical price is unique after the supply increase, and each positive-mass buyer utility is determined by the prices, so the normalized core minimizer $x^*=(U^*,Z^*)$ is unique.

### Polynomial-bit choice of queries

There is an input-bounded sharpness estimate. Any feasible nonzero direction at $x^*$ can be normalized to have sup norm one. Fix its orthant and fix a maximal coordinate to $1$ or $-1$. Intersect the active core cover and floor tangent constraints on this direction with the unit box in that orthant. Negating the buyer coordinates turns the active cover rows into signed incidence rows, with coordinate bounds. The vertices of this bounded polyhedron therefore lie in $\{-1,0,1\}$. The directional derivative of $F$ is linear on that orthant. At each such vertex it is positive, since $F$ is piecewise-affine and the minimizer is unique. All terms in that derivative are multiples of $q'$. Dividing a branch's fixed-price flow bounds by $q'$ makes them integral, so minimum loads and their differences are integer multiples of $q'$. Convexity thus gives

$$
 F(x)-F(x^*)\ge q'\,\|x-x^*\|_\infty .
$$

Take a rational box with ceilings at least one above all possible minimum core quotes, and bound the subgradient norm by a polynomial-bit quantity $M$, using total original mass and capacity. Each rational ellipsoid center outside the core cover box is cut by a violated domain inequality. At a center inside, the branch queries supply a subgradient for a central cut. If the center is farther than $\tau$ from the minimum in sup norm, the cut leaves every point within Euclidean radius $q'\tau/(2M)$ of the minimum that also satisfies the domain. That intersection has an entire positive orthant of the small ball for radii at most one. All core cover and floor constraints allow positive displacements, and the upper box has room.

For a computable denominator bound $Q$, take $\tau=1/(8Q^2)$. To obtain $Q$, restrict each explicit offer to an affine piece at the enlarged equilibrium. Fix the options that are tied there and the goods that are at reserve. The same equilibrium flow clears at every point of the resulting cover polyhedron, and the enlarged equilibrium prices are unique. Buyer quotes are determined by the prices. A rational vertex bound and the fixed core normalization therefore bound the denominators by $Q$ of polynomial bit length. If the core has dimension $k$ and its box lies in an initial ball of rational radius $R$, the rounded rational ellipsoid volume bound gives $O(k^2\log(2kR/\rho))$ cuts with $\rho=\min(1,q'\tau/(2M))$. This forces a center within tolerance and keeps every center's encoding length polynomial, as in A4h5. Rational reconstruction on each inside center supplies candidate core quotes. Extend each by the branch conditional prices in the appropriate orientation and test one physical flow at the enlarged capacities. The correct quote has a compatible extension by the interval and swap proofs. The unique physical solution of the enlarged market has the original minimum prices, and a final flow at original capacities recovers an allocation. There are polynomially many centers, with one conditional minimum and one load flow per branch per center and one physical check per recovered candidate. All query lengths and reconstruction bounds are polynomial in the explicit market input. [Rational ellipsoid and reconstruction in A4h5](A4h-supplied-capacity.md#exact-candidate-recovery-and-call-count)

In the [exact replay](../experiments/market_checks/a4h9_hybrid_examples.py), the core has one buyer and one good. After perturbation, the good branch has load interval $[5/12,1]$ at price $2$, and the buyer branch has interval $[7/12,13/12]$ at utility $1$. A physical clearing flow uses the interior loads $5/6$ and $3/4$, respectively. A final flow clears at the original capacities and prices.

## Contract first, then find or query the ports

Suppose certified price relations have been grouped as in A4h7. A branch may attach to several physical goods if they all lie in a single certified group. Treat that group as one port and use its decreasing envelope as the offer. The quotient minimum determines the physical minimum by strict Hall on groups. Apply the small supply increase in the **quotient** before building its branch messages, making the quotient minimum unique. After solving it, reconstruct every physical price and use an original unweighted flow.

The core need not have affine envelopes in the group coordinate. Suppose its offers have a common increasing rational piecewise-affine column shape $H_g$, with $V_{ig}(z)=b_{ig}-\alpha_iH_g(z)$. Divide buyer utility by $\alpha_i$ and use $Z_g=H_g(z)-H_g(R_g)$, with the corresponding intercept shift. The explicit core again has unit slopes. A branch is queried at the inverse-transformed quote, and its loads and quantity quantum are unchanged. Strictly increasing rational affine pieces give polynomial-bit inverse and reconstruction. [Quotient recovery](A4h-forced-price-relations.md#exact-contraction-after-certification)

A near-factorized affine core also works under the uniform A4a bound. Freeze only the explicit core intercepts. The branch data, the small capacity increments and all virtual unary offers are unchanged. The A4a price sensitivity proof counts only changed columns, so the frozen price map on the $k$ core goods is still a contraction when the normalized slope errors are at most $1/(4k)$. The exact minimum is its fixed point because the offered utilities agree there. Dyadically rounded iteration and rational reconstruction take polynomially many frozen mixed-core solves. Final recovery uses the physical conditional markets and flow. [A4a sensitivity and contraction](A4a-near-factorized-slopes.md#stability-of-least-tu-prices)

One way to obtain such groups uses two physical goods of capacities $2c,2c$, with a guard of mass $3c$ that bids $K-p_1,K-p_2$. At a common buyer-feasible upper price $H<K$, the guard strictly participates at the true minimum. Neither alias alone can take her mass, so she ties them and certifies $p_1=p_2=z$. Those upper prices can put all nonguard buyers outside by choosing $H$ above their ceilings and then $K>H$. Nonguard offers $b_{ig}-z$ and $b_{ig}+a_g-2z$ give the common shape $H_g(z)=\min(z,2z-a_g)$, with two distinct slopes when $a_g>0$. Many such groups can appear in one market.

For affine markets a recognizable subclass allows a factorized core and branches that factorize after deleting their sole buyer or good port. On each viable incidence component, try every vertex as core root in the block–cut tree. Absorb the factorized blocks and take the first unbalanced block on each route with its descendant subtree as one branch. Check factorization of that branch after deleting its entry vertex. To see completeness, a block cannot straddle a single-vertex attachment. If a branch becomes factorized on deleting its port, each unbalanced block in it contains that port. Thus moving balanced ancestor blocks into the core leaves an admissible decomposition for some chosen root. The test uses rational cycle products. The class is hereditary under deletion and higher outside options. Hence the A4h6 restricted-market Hall search is polynomial if the unknown above-reserve goods induce this class, even when goods that stay at reserve have arbitrary slopes. [Hall search over above-reserve goods](A4h-alternating-tree.md#hall-cuts-select-a-restricted-market)

This argument uses one independent quote per branch. A buyer with offers $20-p_1,20-2p_2$ chooses good 1 at $(9,5)$ and good 2 at $(5,2)$. The difference of negative-demand vectors dotted with the price difference is $-1$, which violates convex subgradient monotonicity. A core buyer with unit slopes can fix the relative core scaling while continuing to choose good 1 at both trials. Thus a branch touching two independently priced ports does not in general supply the same convex subgradient oracle.

Two crossing switch lines give a two-buyer obstruction under any continuous one-to-one change of price coordinates with the raw loads held fixed. A joint upper Hall test yields a different certificate that excludes a region of the boundary quotes even as the private market is solved again.
